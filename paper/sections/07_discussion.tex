\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Calibration Changes the Scientific Conclusion}

There are a number of requirements for the probabilities to have an operational meaning in the context of expected-F1 decoding; a probability input of 0.30 should represent roughly a 30\% chance of membership. Balanced logistic training increases attention to the rare positives, but the intercept reflects the reweighted training distribution. This intercept problem remains largely unchanged with temperature scaling, since it only affects the slope of the logits. The low deployment prevalence is restored and the selected set size is changed significantly by prior-shift, Platt and isotonic maps.

The direction of the evidence-F1 comparison is changed by calibration. It also explains why the learned size and redundancy penalties are unnecessary: if the probabilities reflect the rare membership rate, then the unpenalized utility already favors short prefixes. When making an adaptive selection assessment, report the absolute calibration against the prevalence reference and the difference between the model's uncalibrated and calibrated scores.

\subsection{Why Thresholding Is the Strong Baseline}

Expected-F1 uses the memberships of all candidates, but its independence approximation and complete-gold objective may not match the actual data. A tuned threshold directly uses the same calibrated probabilities with fewer assumptions. Here it has more recall, moderate set growth and is 0.00483 F1 better than expected-F1.

A calibrated-threshold baseline is required for future evaluations. Beating a fixed budget is not enough if the same probabilities support a stronger threshold rule. An adaptive decoder should exceed that rule, offer an alternate precision-cost operating point, or enhance a downstream outcome. Expected-F1 gives the second: it returns approximately two fewer items than thresholding while being substantially more precise, which might be important if human review time is limited.

This interpretation is supported by the reachability strata. The main advantage of expected-F1 is where the released-gold set is already complete, when there is the possibility of pruning a coherent short prefix from the packet. Once only a portion of the gold set is contained in the packet, it is not of benefit. The larger output and greater recovery of evidence is an advantage to thresholding in that larger stratum. This was a post hoc stratification, and therefore is not an appropriate way to make a prespecified claim for a subgroup. It does propose a concrete design target: selection should take into consideration the probability of incomplete retrieval.

\subsection{Retrieval Is the Larger Bottleneck}

This packet oracle of 0.3560 puts the observed selector scores into perspective. Over 25\% of eligible claims do not have any reachable released gold item and coverage for complete-gold claims is less than 5\%. There is a relatively small range of possible improvements that can be achieved in decoders. The image-only results are particularly poor and the image-only expected-F1 effect is slightly negative.

Based on the retrieval audit, there are some ideas to move forward. The performance of image fusion is better than either image signal. Deeper recall is improved by text cross-encoder reranking, but the first few ranks are affected. Another ranker trained to maximize early complete-gold coverage, a claim-adaptive retrieval budget, or a selector that explicitly models packet completeness may yield more value than another selection penalty. This diagnosis can be made using the fixed packet since retrieval and decoding are performed separately.

\subsection{Evidence Metrics and Verdict Metrics Differ}

Evidence F1 and verdict macro-F1 answer related but distinct questions. A positive evidence effect does not establish verifier non-inferiority. The gap is close to the margin and could be due to limited validation size, a weak shared verifier, or different information content in the shorter sets. A larger or stronger verifier study is needed before claiming an end-to-end benefit.

For deployed fact-checking, evidence precision, evidence recall, review-set size, retrieval reachability, calibration and verdict performance are useful metrics for the operating report. An aggregate score obscures the balance between conciseness of evidence and downstream adequacy.

\subsection{What the Study Supports}

There are three claims that can be substantiated with the evidence. For adaptive probability-based evidence selection under imbalance, prior-aware calibration is needed. Calibrated expected-F1 can be better than the fixed top-5 rule for complete-gold evidence F1 and return fewer items, primarily when the retrieved packet contains the complete released-gold set. The most successful tested policy on this development population is calibrated thresholding. There is no evidence of an end-to-end advantage, a benefit from size or redundancy penalties, or generalization beyond the MOCHEG setting evaluated.
