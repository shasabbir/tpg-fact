\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Probability Quality Changes the Operating Point}

Balanced training gives evidence positives more weight than their frequency at inference. With the same candidate universe, uncalibrated expected-F1 returns almost 25 items per claim on MOCHEG and loses over five evidence-F1 points relative to its calibrated counterpart. On AVerImaTeC, the uncalibrated version nearly fills the ten-item cap. The external Brier and reliability results show that the frozen map corrects substantial overconfidence. Its small Brier advantage over the prevalence reference and unchanged AP also show that this correction leaves substantial room for a better evidence scorer.

Thresholding provides a simple comparison with fewer distributional assumptions than the expected-F1 decoder. On MOCHEG, expected-F1 gives up some recall and returns a smaller, more precise evidence set. On AVerImaTeC, its higher F1 point estimate comes with a larger set. Changes in candidate composition, output caps, evidence units, and learned probability distributions could contribute to this reversal. The cross-benchmark comparison changes these conditions together.

\subsection{The Ranked Pool Sets the Scope of Selection}

The MOCHEG repair raises the packet-oracle F1, yet complete coverage remains 5.91\% on locked validation. In the AVerImaTeC audit, most missing exact targets had an identity in the store. The largest individual loss occurred when usable targets fell outside the ranked pool, followed by the loss of URLs without usable extracted text. These observations distinguish where to improve retrieval from where to improve selection. Exact image basenames provide a particularly narrow check because equivalent images may have different names.

\subsection{Evidence Overlap and Verdicts Measure Different Outcomes}

The completeness switch has a slightly higher evidence F1 point estimate than thresholding on both benchmarks. The evidence intervals leave its superiority unresolved. Its MOCHEG verifier effect clears the aggregate non-inferiority margin for the MLP, while three seed-level safeguards fail and the linear verifier moves in the opposite direction. The external aggregate safeguard also fails. This variation supports testing verdicts with fixed verifier parameters and reporting class-wise results whenever selector utility is claimed. It also motivates future evaluation of whether semantic evidence matches change the ranking of policies observed with exact identities.
