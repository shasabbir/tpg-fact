\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Why the Candidate Regime Changes the Conclusion}

Using the checked packet experiment and the full-corpus comparison gives opposite results. Adaptive calibrated selection is substantially superior to the best development-selected fixed budget on the released judgment-candidate packet, although a global calibrated threshold is stronger in every seed. However, under label-blind full-corpus retrieval, \method{} performs worse than the selected fixed budget in every seed. The candidate universe is therefore easier for the packet result, which does not establish end-to-end retrieval effectiveness.

One step in this change is accounted for by the reachability bound in Eq.~\ref{eq:ceiling-f1}. A selector cannot recover an item that retrieval did not put in its packet. Low text and image Recall@50 lower the potential evidence score before calibration or decoding. The remaining candidates are also distributed differently: assessed items in a judgment packet are related to the claim, while high-similarity distractors in a full-corpus packet are drawn from tens of thousands of documents and images. This means that the evidence-membership scorer faces both missing positives and a harder negative distribution.

A second qualification is added by the threshold result. Calibrated thresholding outperforms exact expected-F1 decoding in every seed on the judgment packet, even though both methods use the same probabilities. While this packet therefore supports adaptive set sizing in a general sense, it does not identify the expected-F1 decoder as the strongest adaptive rule. On the full corpus, the count head exceeds \method{} despite poor count mean absolute error. This indicates that count accuracy and set F1 are still not the same, and that directly decoding expected utilities is not necessarily robust to a poor ranking.

\subsection{What the Negative Result Establishes}

The results of the calibration and control show the reason for the conjunctive nature of the gate. Unrestricted temperature scaling slightly worsens the Brier score for one packet seed despite the positive evidence-F1 effect. The declared training-only guard repairs that failure without consulting validation labels. The full-corpus run retains identity scaling, yet expected-F1 decoding is still outperformed by the fixed-budget and count-classification controls. The shared verifier has a favorable mean point estimate, but this secondary verdict effect does not replace evidence quality.

The formal benchmark can be consumed only once for a confirmatory interpretation, thus making the development gate a demanding one. It also avoids the primary evaluation mistake of the previous count-prediction study: beating one convenient fixed budget while losing to another sensible budget. Here it is effective as intended: even with a positive feasibility packet and a favorable verifier diagnostic, the cleanly reproduced negative full-corpus effect stops the study before the formal test.

The result does not imply that expected-F1 decoding is inherently inferior. Full-corpus Recall@50 for text and images are only 0.3616 and 0.2138, respectively, and item-membership estimates might not satisfy the conditional-independence assumption of the decoder. It does establish that the declared method, scorer and retrieval configuration do not justify consuming MOCHEG's formal test. Future directions should be treated as new studies with a new development protocol, not an a posteriori rescue of this one.

This boundary is important when assessing a negative development decision. The experiment does not prove that all adaptive selectors, scorers or retrievers will fail; it falsifies the prespecified superiority claim for the evaluated configuration. On the other hand, the favorable packet and verifier diagnostics are not applicable outside the conditions in which they were measured. The checked conclusion is intentionally more limited: with the pinned BGE-M3/SigLIP2 retrieval artifacts, logistic evidence scorer, calibration guard and declared decoder, there is no primary evidence advantage, and the effect is consistently negative across the three development seeds.

\subsection{Implications for Evaluation Practice}

First, candidate construction needs to be reported as part of the evaluated system. A selector-only experiment on judgment candidates is helpful for mechanism development, but it should be identified as conditional on candidate availability. End-to-end claims require label-blind retrieval and complete-gold scoring to prevent missed evidence from being silently removed from the denominator. This distinction can be made apparent by reporting retrieval Recall@$k$ and the packet-conditioned ceiling in addition to selector F1.

Second, selector comparisons require a common packet and a strong fixed comparator. Permitting methods to receive different candidates confounds retrieval with set decoding. After observing results, it is possible to choose a weak fixed budget, which results in an artificial adaptive gain. In this study candidate identities and features are shared within each regime, the fixed budget is chosen using permitted calibration data, and all declared seeds are preserved. These controls help to interpret the negative effect, but do not make it better.

Third, evidence selection and verdict prediction should remain separate endpoints. A verifier may benefit from additional context that is not part of the released gold evidence, or it may use correlations that do not support faithful attribution. Thus the positive mean verifier diagnostic is relevant to system behavior but cannot establish evidence quality. A credible multimodal fact-checking evaluation should report both outcomes and specify in advance which one governs the primary claim.

Finally, staged evaluation is most informative when all the various stages keep their failures. This is a unified record of the unsuccessful temperature-scaling attempt, the more potent threshold control, the negative full-corpus seeds and the good verifier diagnostic. Saving all of them helps to keep a positive feasibility result from being separated from what led to the reversed result, and provides future researchers with tangible hypotheses to work into a different protocol in terms of retrieval coverage, calibration, and evidence dependence.
