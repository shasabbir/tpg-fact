\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Why the Candidate Regime Changes the Conclusion}

The checked packet experiment and full-corpus comparison lead to opposite conclusions. Adaptive calibrated selection substantially outperforms the strongest development-selected fixed budget on the released judgment-candidate packet, although a global calibrated threshold is stronger in every seed. Under label-blind full-corpus retrieval, however, \method{} loses to the selected fixed budget in every seed. The packet result therefore reflects an easier candidate universe and does not establish end-to-end retrieval effectiveness.

The reachability bound in Eq.~\ref{eq:ceiling-f1} explains one part of this change. A selector cannot recover a gold item that retrieval did not place in its packet. Low text and image Recall@50 reduce the attainable evidence score before calibration or decoding begins. The remaining candidates are also distributed differently: a judgment packet consists of assessed items associated with the claim, whereas a full-corpus packet contains high-similarity distractors selected from tens of thousands of documents and images. Consequently, the evidence-membership scorer faces both missing positives and a harder negative distribution.

The threshold result adds a second qualification. On the judgment packet, calibrated thresholding exceeds exact expected-F1 decoding in every seed even though both use the same probabilities. The packet therefore supports adaptive set sizing in a broad sense, but it does not isolate the expected-F1 decoder as the strongest adaptive rule. On the full corpus, the count head exceeds \method{} despite poor count mean absolute error. This shows that count accuracy and set F1 remain distinct, while also demonstrating that direct expected-utility decoding is not automatically robust to a weak ranking.

\subsection{What the Negative Result Establishes}

The calibration and control results illustrate why the gate is conjunctive. Unrestricted temperature scaling slightly worsens Brier score for one packet seed despite a positive evidence-F1 effect. The declared training-only guard repairs that failure without consulting validation labels. In the full-corpus run the guard retains identity scaling, yet expected-F1 decoding still loses to both fixed-budget and count-classification controls. The shared verifier has a favorable mean point estimate, but that secondary verdict effect cannot substitute for evidence quality.

The development gate is deliberately demanding because the formal benchmark can be consumed only once for a confirmatory interpretation. It also prevents a repetition of the preceding count-prediction study's main evaluation error: beating one convenient fixed budget while losing to another reasonable budget. Here it works as intended: the cleanly reproduced negative full-corpus effect stops the study before the formal test despite a positive feasibility packet and favorable verifier diagnostic.

The result does not establish that exact expected-F1 decoding is intrinsically inferior. Full-corpus Recall@50 is only 0.3616 for text and 0.2138 for images, and item-membership estimates may violate the decoder's conditional-independence assumption. It does establish that the declared method, scorer, and retrieval configuration do not justify consuming MOCHEG's formal test. Any future direction must be framed as a new study with a new development protocol, not a post hoc rescue of this one.

This boundary is important for interpreting a negative development decision. The experiment falsifies the prespecified superiority claim for the evaluated configuration; it does not prove that every adaptive selector, scorer, or retriever will fail. Conversely, the favorable packet and verifier diagnostics cannot be generalized beyond their measured conditions. The checked conclusion is deliberately narrower: with the pinned BGE-M3/SigLIP2 retrieval artifacts, logistic evidence scorer, calibration guard, and declared decoder, the primary evidence advantage is absent and consistently negative across the three development seeds.

\subsection{Implications for Evaluation Practice}

First, candidate construction should be reported as part of the evaluated system. A selector-only experiment on judgment candidates is useful for mechanism development, but it should be labeled as conditional on candidate availability. End-to-end claims require label-blind retrieval and complete-gold scoring so that missed evidence is not silently removed from the denominator. Reporting retrieval Recall@$k$ and the packet-conditioned ceiling alongside selector F1 makes this distinction visible.

Second, selector comparisons require a shared packet and a strong fixed comparator. Allowing methods to receive different candidates confounds retrieval with set decoding. Choosing a weak fixed budget after observing results can create an artificial adaptive gain. In this study, candidate identities and features are shared within each regime, the fixed budget is selected with permitted calibration data, and all declared seeds are retained. These controls make the negative effect interpretable even though they do not improve it.

Third, evidence selection and verdict prediction should remain separate endpoints. A verifier may benefit from additional context that is not part of the released gold evidence, or it may exploit correlations that do not support faithful attribution. The favorable mean verifier diagnostic is therefore relevant to system behavior but cannot establish evidence quality. A credible multimodal fact-checking evaluation should report both outcomes and specify in advance which one governs the primary claim.

Finally, staged evaluation is most informative when every stage retains its failures. The unsuccessful temperature-scaling trial, the stronger threshold control, the negative full-corpus seeds, and the favorable verifier diagnostic form one coherent record. Preserving all of them prevents a positive feasibility result from being detached from the more realistic condition that reversed it, and it gives future work concrete hypotheses about retrieval coverage, calibration, and evidence dependence to test under a new protocol.
