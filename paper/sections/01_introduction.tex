\section{Introduction}

Images are frequently reused with misleading captions, dates, locations, or event descriptions. Because the pixels may be authentic, detectors that emphasize manipulation artifacts or surface-level image--text compatibility can miss the misinformation mechanism. Automated fact-checking therefore increasingly uses external evidence, but retrieved documents create new failure modes: evidence may have been published after the required decision time, multiple results may copy the same origin, and a fluent model may issue a confident verdict when the retrieved material is insufficient.

Knowledge-aware and evidence-grounded multimodal systems address parts of this problem. KAMP learns from text, images, and background knowledge graphs \cite{zhang2025kamp}; RED-DOT explicitly reranks relevant multimodal evidence \cite{papadopoulos2025reddot}; COVE predicts an image's original context before caption veracity \cite{tonglet2025cove}; and multimodal RAG systems retrieve web information for claim reasoning \cite{khaliq2024ragar,kangur2025multireflect}. These advances do not by themselves guarantee that evidence was available at decision time or that apparently diverse results are independent.

We study the following question: \emph{Does reasoning over time-valid, independently sourced evidence improve future-event multimodal misinformation detection and reduce overconfident decisions compared with ordinary multimodal RAG?} The intended operating unit is an English image--text claim about a United States event. The system returns supported, refuted, or insufficient evidence and records the evidence and cutoff used for that decision.

This work makes four planned contributions. First, it defines \datasetname{}, a temporally partitioned benchmark constructed from professional fact-check metadata and open archives. Second, it introduces a temporal-provenance graph that represents publication order, duplication, hyperlinks, publishers, entities, events, and shared media. Third, it proposes a hierarchical detector that assesses evidence sufficiency before veracity. Fourth, it preregisters future-event, leakage, duplicate-source, calibration, and selective-risk evaluations. These contributions remain proposed until the corresponding real-data experiments pass the stated checks.
