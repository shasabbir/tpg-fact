\section{Limitations and Ethics}
\label{sec:limitations}

\subsection{Limitations}

The main MOCHEG validity constraint is evaluation reuse. Its 1,490 validation
claims are locked within each run, and every new choice is fitted on training
roles, but the broader project had inspected this resource before the present
protocol. AVerImaTeC supplies an independently frozen evaluation, but changes
the evidence unit, label space, candidate store, and output cap; it confirms
relationships rather than reproducing raw scores. Formal-test annotations for
both benchmarks remain unopened.

The external evidence endpoint is also incomplete. Exact normalized source
identity can penalize semantically valid alternative pages, while the official
Gemma-3-27B evidence judge cannot run on the single local GPU under the
organizer's prescribed condition. The development split is extremely
imbalanced (141 of 152 claims are Refuted), with one Conflicting claim, and the
static store was partly constructed with gold-question-derived queries. We
therefore report the external result as a locked exact-source diagnostic, not
an official shared-task score or unrestricted web-search estimate.

Released complete-gold sets can omit other valid web evidence. Exact-set
metrics measure agreement with those identifiers and may penalize a defensible
alternative source. The original complete-reachability subgroup contains only
79 validation claims and was identified post hoc. It motivates the frozen
completeness switch but cannot establish a population subgroup guarantee. The
repair raises validation complete-gold coverage to only 88 claims, so
completeness modeling remains data-limited.

The membership scorer and completeness estimator are deliberately low capacity
to preserve experimental control. Better content-aware multimodal scoring may
change ranking, calibration, and the apparent value of the switch. Conversely,
the stronger verifier is still a compact MLP over stored features rather than a
modern generative fact-checker. Aggregate non-inferiority depends on that
architecture and fails in three seed-specific intervals; the linear control
reverses the mean direction.

Expected-F1 assumes conditionally independent candidate memberships and
restricts decisions to prefixes of a fixed ranking. Sentences from one source,
duplicate images, and complementary cross-modal evidence violate that
assumption. A zero-valued redundancy penalty in this experiment does not show
that dependencies are absent; it shows only that the tested penalty does not
improve model-selection utility.

The robustness matrix is diagnostic rather than exhaustive. Temperature
perturbations expose sensitivity to probability scale but do not estimate how
often such drift occurs in practice. Runtime is recorded on one workstation
without standardized warm-up, energy instrumentation, or competing-system
measurements. We therefore make no throughput, energy, or deployment-efficiency
claim beyond observed selected-set size.

\subsection{Ethical Considerations}

Automated fact-checking can create false confidence when relevant evidence is
missing. A deployed interface should expose source identifiers, retrieval
limits, calibration status, and the distinction between ``no evidence
retrieved'' and ``evidence supports no conclusion.'' The system should assist
trained reviewers, not replace them.

Web evidence can contain personal, disturbing, copyrighted, geographically
skewed, or politically sensitive material. Retrieval and display should follow
source terms, minimize unnecessary reproduction, and provide safeguards for
reviewers. MOCHEG is not representative of all languages, communities, claim
types, or media ecosystems; results should not be generalized to low-resource
or high-stakes domains without direct evaluation.

The study preserves unfavorable and incomplete runs, keeps both formal tests
sealed, and reports uncertainty and failed gates. These controls reduce
selective reporting and make the difference between a useful development
finding and a deployment claim explicit.
