\section{Experimental Design}
\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{Frozen Protocol}

The configuration was frozen on 4 November 2026 before the outcome-bearing run. It records data and model revisions, five claim-grouped partition seeds, retrieval depths, scorer families, all calibration candidates, selector grids, evaluation metrics, bootstrap settings, and stopping rules. Prior-aware maps were included because class weighting changes the effective training prior, which a temperature-only map cannot generally repair. No locked-validation outcome was used to choose among the calibration maps.

The configuration permits 36 scorer-calibrator combinations: three scorer families, two loss weightings, and six calibration maps. Screening results are excluded from scientific evidence. Final model choice uses only the assigned training roles, after which every selected configuration is evaluated once on locked validation.

\subsection{Baselines and Outcomes}

The selector controls are fixed top-5, calibrated thresholding, and learned count prediction. Expected-F1, size-penalized expected-F1, and size-plus-redundancy expected-F1 are the adaptive set-utility variants. The primary outcome is claim-macro complete-gold evidence F1. We also report precision, recall, Jaccard similarity, exact-set match, selected count, calibration metrics, and verdict macro-F1.

For each method comparison, claim-level differences are first averaged over the five deterministic partitions for a given validation claim. A claim-aligned bootstrap then resamples the 1,490 claims. Reported selector intervals use 10,000 samples. Familywise comparisons against fixed top-5 use Holm correction. Robustness intervals use 5,000 samples and are descriptive.

After the primary comparison, we stratify validation claims by packet reachability: no released gold item reachable, some but not all reachable, or complete gold reachable. This diagnostic is explicitly post hoc and descriptive. It localizes the source of aggregate gains without changing the fitted models or the declared decision rule.

The declared superiority condition requires a mean evidence-F1 gain of at least 0.015 over the best fixed rule, a positive effect in every partition, a corrected interval lower bound above zero, calibrated Brier performance better than the prevalence predictor, a reasonable selected-to-gold size ratio, verdict non-inferiority, and exact clean reproduction. All conditions must hold before the selector can be considered superior at the system level.

\subsection{Robustness Matrix}

We rerun selection under narrower packets of 10 and 20 items, the full retained width, text-only and image-only packets, deterministic removal of 25\% and 50\% of reachable gold, promotion of five distractors, duplication of the top three items, and probability-temperature perturbations of 0.5 and 2.0. Perturbations leave the fitted selector unchanged. They measure dependence on reachability, modality, ranking contamination, duplication, and probability scale.

\subsection{Audit and Reproduction}

Every run saves candidate-level scores, calibrated probabilities, selected identifiers, claim-level metrics, selected hyperparameters, environment metadata, and formal-test access flags. An independent audit recomputes population sizes, calibration summaries, set metrics, paired effects, and guard conditions from saved records. A clean rerun writes to a new directory and compares core artifacts by SHA-256 rather than relying only on aggregate agreement.

The reporting and selector analyses use Python 3.13.14 and NumPy 2.5.4 on Windows 11. The lightweight scorers and verifier are implemented with deterministic numerical routines; the five partition seeds govern claim-role assignment rather than stochastic neural training. Pretrained retrieval revisions, packet depths, and all decoding grids are pinned in the recorded configuration.
