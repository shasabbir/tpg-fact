\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Research Questions}

The primary question is whether \method{} improves claim-level evidence-set F1 over the strongest fixed budget selected on development data. Secondary questions test whether expected-F1 decoding improves on count prediction, whether calibration contributes to selection quality, and whether the selected evidence preserves verdict macro-F1 under a shared verifier.

Table~\ref{tab:claim-test-map} makes the claim hierarchy explicit. Only the first comparison can establish the primary selector claim. Calibration, count prediction, and verification diagnose why a selector succeeds or fails, but none can compensate for a negative primary evidence effect. This hierarchy was fixed to avoid promoting whichever secondary endpoint happens to be favorable.

\subsection{Development and Formal Protocols}

The development pilot uses seeds 13, 37, and 73. A maximum of 20 declared configurations may be evaluated using train and development data. The official test is unavailable throughout this phase. A formal evaluation, if unlocked, uses seeds 13, 37, 73, 101, and 137 exactly once with the frozen code, data, features, configurations, and checkpoints.

The first GPU run uses the validated 400-claim packet to check the semantic feature path and selector mechanism. It is a feasibility experiment because its candidates come from the released judgment universe. The primary development comparison repeats the selector on the same eligible claim sample after label-blind retrieval from the full public text and train/development image corpus. Relevance judgments are joined only after rankings are frozen. The formal test is not unlocked by a qrel-packet result alone.

\subsection{Baselines and Fairness}

Controlled baselines are fixed top-$k$ selection, a calibrated 0.5 probability threshold, uncalibrated expected-F1 decoding, and a learned count head. The official MOCHEG baseline is reproduced where compatible. Published M-RAV and MEVER values are contextual because their candidate construction, model access, and tuning conditions differ \cite{luu2027mrav,zhang2026mever}; they are not entered into paired significance tests. Every rerun selector receives the same bounded candidates, features, supervision, and configuration budget.

\subsection{Metrics and Uncertainty}

Primary evidence metrics are claim-level macro precision, recall, and F1. Secondary evidence metrics are exact set match, Jaccard score, selected count, count mean absolute error, Recall@$k$, MAP, and nDCG. Calibration is measured with Brier score, negative log-likelihood, and expected calibration error. Verdict quality is measured by macro-F1 and class-wise F1.

Main selector effects use 10,000 paired bootstrap resamples. Source, event, or fact-check article is the preferred resampling unit when reliable grouping metadata are available; otherwise claims are resampled and that limitation is stated. Seed-aligned records are averaged before the main interval, while seed dispersion is reported separately. Holm correction is applied to the prespecified selector family. Verdict preservation uses a paired non-inferiority interval with margin $-0.01$.

\subsection{Development Gate}

The test remains sealed unless all of the following hold: the data and software gates pass; mean \method-minus-best-fixed evidence F1 is at least $+0.015$ over the three development seeds; the effect is positive for every seed; calibrated Brier score is no worse than the uncalibrated score; verdict macro-F1 is no more than 0.01 below the fixed control; matched comparisons share identical candidate and verifier artifacts; and a clean rerun regenerates the development table. The full-corpus selector fails both evidence-effect conditions: its mean effect is negative and no seed is positive. The gate is therefore fail-closed regardless of secondary diagnostics, and formal-test annotations remain unavailable.

The positive formal claim requires a mean official-test evidence-F1 effect of at least $+0.01$ with a positive 95\% paired interval, together with a verdict-effect lower bound above $-0.01$. A gain against an arbitrarily weak fixed budget does not satisfy this rule.

\subsection{Ablations and Robustness}

Prespecified ablations remove calibration, replace exact expected-F1 with a global threshold or ratio approximation, replace set decoding with count prediction, and remove modality or duplicate-cluster features. Robustness tests inject repeated evidence, vary packet width without retuning, remove each modality, and corrupt top-ranked candidates with irrelevant items. Subgroup analyses cover verdict, gold-set size, source, and whether image evidence is required. These analyses explain a primary result but cannot rescue a failed main comparison.

\subsection{Implementation and Resources}

The main execution host is an RTX 4090 with 24~GiB device memory, 64~GiB system memory, an Intel i9 processor, and approximately 1~TiB free storage. Feature extraction uses mixed precision, cached frozen encoders, text batch size 32, and image batch size 64. The Python package, configurations, tests, data preparation, feature builder, selector runner, and PowerShell orchestration are version controlled. Raw predictions, calibration values, selected identifiers, histories, hashes, and environment metadata are retained for independent recomputation.
