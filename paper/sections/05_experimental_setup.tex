\section{Experimental Design}
\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{Frozen Protocol}

The setup of the configuration was fixed on 4 November 2026 prior to the outcome-bearing run. It stores data and model revisions, the five partition seeds for the claim groups, depths of retrieval, scorer groups, all calibration candidates, selector grids, evaluation metrics, bootstrap settings, and stopping rules. Prior-aware maps were added because the change of the training prior due to class weighting cannot generally be fixed by a temperature-only map. No locked-validation outcome was used for selecting between the calibration maps.

There are 36 different combinations of scorer families, loss weightings and calibration maps possible with the configuration. The results of screening are not included in scientific evidence. Only the assigned training roles are used for final model selection, with every selected configuration then evaluated once on locked validation.

\subsection{Baselines and Outcomes}

Fixed top-5, calibrated thresholding and learned count prediction are the controls managed by the selector. The adaptive set-utility variants are expected-F1, size-penalized expected-F1 and size-plus-redundancy expected-F1. The main result is the claim-macro complete-gold evidence F1. Precision, recall, Jaccard similarity, exact-set match, selected count, calibration metrics and verdict macro-F1 are also reported.

The claim-level differences are averaged over the five deterministic partitions for a given validation claim, for each method comparison. A claim-aligned bootstrap resamples the 1,490 claims. Reported selector intervals are based on 10,000 samples. Familywise comparisons against fixed top-5 use Holm correction. Robustness intervals are descriptive, based on 5,000 samples.

Following the primary comparison, a distinction is made between validation claims with no released gold item reachable, some but not all released gold items reachable, or complete gold reachable. This diagnostic is clearly post hoc and descriptive. It identifies the origin of the aggregate gains without affecting the fitted models or the stated decision rule.

The superiority condition is a mean evidence-F1 gain of at least 0.015 over the best fixed rule, a positive gain in every partition, a corrected interval lower bound above zero, calibrated Brier performance better than the prevalence predictor, a reasonable selected-to-gold size ratio, verdict non-inferiority and exact clean reproduction. The selector will be superior if all the conditions are true at the system level.

\subsection{Robustness Matrix}

We rerun selection under five different packets (10 and 20 items, the full retained width and two packets with only text or only images), deterministic removal of 25\% and 50\% of reachable gold, promotion of five distractors, duplication of the top three items, and probability- and temperature perturbations of 0.5 and 2.0. The fitted selector does not change as a result of perturbations. They quantify the dependence on reachability, modality, contamination of the ranking, duplication and probability scale.

\subsection{Audit and Reproduction}

All candidate-level scores, calibrated probabilities, selected identifiers, claim-level metrics, selected hyperparameters, environment metadata and formal-test access flags are saved for every run. An independent audit recalculates population size, calibration summaries, set metrics, paired effects and guard conditions from saved records. A clean rerun writes to a new directory and compares the core artifacts using SHA-256 instead of aggregate agreement.

The reporting and selector analyses are conducted using Python 3.13.14 with NumPy 2.5.4 on Windows 11. The light-weight scorers and verifier use deterministic numerical routines; the five partition seeds control claim-role assignment instead of stochastic neural training. All the pre-trained retrieval revisions, packet depths and decoding grids are fixed in the recorded configuration.
