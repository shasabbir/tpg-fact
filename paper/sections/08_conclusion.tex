\section{Conclusion}

This paper defines a reproducible study of temporal and evidence-source provenance in multimodal misinformation detection. The central design combines time-constrained retrieval, dependency-aware graph aggregation, evidence-sufficiency prediction, and calibrated abstention. Its evaluation prioritizes future-event generalization, temporal leakage, duplicate-source robustness, and selective risk. The current artifact establishes the protocol and executable scaffold without presenting unmeasured findings. Completion requires freezing the real corpus, running controlled baselines and five-seed experiments, auditing all outputs, and narrowing the final claims to the observed evidence.
