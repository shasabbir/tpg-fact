Temporal and Evidence-Source Provenance-Aware
Multimodal Misinformation Detection with
Calibrated Abstention
Sabbir Hossain
Department of Computer Science
American International University-Bangladesh
Dhaka, Bangladesh
26-94030-1@student.aiub.edu

Abstract—Multimodal misinformation detectors can
retrieve convincing but temporally invalid, duplicated, or mutually dependent evidence. This paper
specifies TPG-Fact, a temporal and evidence-source
provenance-aware framework for image–text claim verification. The framework retrieves only evidence available before a declared decision cutoff, groups copied
and syndicated material into dependency clusters,
represents cross-document and cross-modal relationships in a heterogeneous graph, and separates evidence sufficiency from veracity prediction. The intended output is supported, refuted, or insufficient evidence, with validation-fitted calibration and selective
abstention. We also define US-TPMFC, a proposed
temporally partitioned benchmark of United States
events derived from professional fact-check metadata
and open web archives. The evaluation protocol uses
future-event splits, retrieval-quality measures, eventlevel uncertainty, temporal-leakage tests, and controlled duplicate-source interventions. This document
describes the preregistered design and executable research scaffold; real-corpus results are intentionally
withheld until the planned data audit and experiments
are completed.
Index Terms—multimodal misinformation, automated fact-checking, evidence provenance, temporal
retrieval, selective prediction, calibrated abstention

Research status: The method and evaluation protocol
are implemented as a reproducible scaffold. No realcorpus or comparative findings are claimed in this
version.
I. Introduction
Images are frequently reused with misleading captions,
dates, locations, or event descriptions. Because the pixels
may be authentic, detectors that emphasize manipulation
artifacts or surface-level image–text compatibility can miss
the misinformation mechanism. Automated fact-checking
therefore increasingly uses external evidence, but retrieved
documents create new failure modes: evidence may have
been published after the required decision time, multiple
results may copy the same origin, and a fluent model may
issue a confident verdict when the retrieved material is
insufficient.
Knowledge-aware and evidence-grounded multimodal
systems address parts of this problem. KAMP learns from

text, images, and background knowledge graphs [1]; REDDOT explicitly reranks relevant multimodal evidence [2];
COVE predicts an image’s original context before caption
veracity [3]; and multimodal RAG systems retrieve web
information for claim reasoning [4], [5]. These advances do
not by themselves guarantee that evidence was available
at decision time or that apparently diverse results are
independent.
We study the following question: Does reasoning over
time-valid, independently sourced evidence improve futureevent multimodal misinformation detection and reduce
overconfident decisions compared with ordinary multimodal
RAG? The intended operating unit is an English image–
text claim about a United States event. The system
returns supported, refuted, or insufficient evidence and
records the evidence and cutoff used for that decision.
This work makes four planned contributions. First,
it defines US-TPMFC, a temporally partitioned benchmark constructed from professional fact-check metadata
and open archives. Second, it introduces a temporalprovenance graph that represents publication order, duplication, hyperlinks, publishers, entities, events, and shared
media. Third, it proposes a hierarchical detector that
assesses evidence sufficiency before veracity. Fourth, it
preregisters future-event, leakage, duplicate-source, calibration, and selective-risk evaluations. These contributions remain proposed until the corresponding real-data
experiments pass the stated checks.
II. Related Work
A. Multimodal Misinformation Detection
NewsCLIPpings established a large benchmark of authentic but deliberately mismatched news images and
captions [6]. VERITE subsequently controlled unimodal
bias and topic imbalance in real-world multimodal misinformation evaluation [7]. KAMP introduced knowledgeaware multimodal pretraining using single-modality, crossmodal, and knowledge-content objectives [1]. Synthetic
multimodal examples have also been used to improve
transfer to real misinformation cases [8]. Event-Radar
models visual manipulation, textual emotion, and eventlevel inconsistency through an event graph [9]. These

content-focused approaches motivate strong no-retrieval
and event-aware baselines.
B. Evidence-Grounded Fact-Checking
MOCHEG formulates multimodal fact-checking as evidence retrieval, claim verification, and explanation generation [10]. MetaSumPerceiver summarizes evidence across
multiple multimodal documents [11]. RED-DOT challenges the assumption that all web evidence is relevant
and introduces relevance-aware reranking [2]. RAGAR and
MultiReflect combine multimodal large language models
with retrieval and iterative reasoning [4], [5]. SNIFFER
uses external retrieval for out-of-context detection [12].
Consequently, retrieval itself is not the novelty claimed
here; the proposed distinction is the joint enforcement of
temporal validity and source-dependency-aware aggregation.

D. Temporal Partitioning and Release
Training covers 2020–2023, validation uses 2024, and
final testing uses 2025–2026. Event identifiers, nearduplicate claims, and identical perceptual image hashes
are group constraints. The proposed release contains
URLs, cryptographic and perceptual hashes, timestamps,
normalized labels, graph annotations, queries, and split
manifests without redistributing copyrighted content.
Before treating US-TPMFC as a benchmark contribution, the filtered corpus must contain at least 2,000 cases,
300 per label, 50 test events, and two independent evidence
clusters for at least 70% of retained claims. Otherwise, it
will be reported only as a stress-test collection.

C. Image Context, Time, and Evidence Dependence
The 5Pils work recovers original image meta-context
from web evidence [13], while COVE predicts image context before assessing a caption [3]. Temporal evaluation is
essential because multimodal misinformation models lose
performance under chronological distribution shift [14].
ChronoFact demonstrates structured timeline reasoning
for complex temporal claims [15]. Research on conflicting
evidence further shows that source information affects
RAG-based fact verification [16]. The present study connects these concerns by representing time and content
dependence within the same evidence graph.
III. Task and Benchmark Construction
A. Task Definition
Each instance consists of claim text xt , an associated image xv , a decision cutoff τ , and an evidence collection E≤τ .
The output label is y ∈ {supported, refuted, insufficient}.
Evidence published after τ is unavailable by definition.
The independent unit for splitting and uncertainty analysis is the event, not the individual post or document.
B. Candidate Acquisition
The proposed corpus uses English ClaimReview records
concerning United States events from 2020–2026. GDELT
supplies reproducible discovery metadata and Common
Crawl supplies archived page observations. The collection
retains cases with an identifiable claim time, recoverable
image or archive, unambiguous professional rating, and
sufficient timestamp metadata. Fact-check domains and
documents containing the final verdict are excluded from
the retrieval pool.
C. Label Policy
Only explicit site ratings are normalized. True or correct
maps to supported; false, fake, or miscaptioned maps to
refuted; and unproven, unsubstantiated, or no evidence

IV. Temporal-Provenance Method
A. Time-Constrained Multimodal Retrieval
The claim representation combines named entities, temporal expressions, OCR text, and an image caption. Text
candidates are retrieved using BM25 and BGE-M3; visual candidates use SigLIP2 embeddings and perceptual
hashes. Reciprocal-rank fusion combines lexical, dense,
and visual rankings. The top 100 candidates are reranked,
after which the top 20 satisfying the publication-time
cutoff are retained.
B. Evidence-Source Provenance Graph
The graph contains claim, image, document, publisher,
entity, and event nodes. Typed edges represent retrieval
relevance, image containment, near-duplicate text or images, hyperlinks, publisher membership, entity or event
mentions, and temporal precedence. Document clusters
are estimated using exact hashes, MinHash-style lexical
similarity, semantic similarity, perceptual image distance,
hyperlinks, and publication order. Because true authorship may be unknowable, the earliest observed record is
described only as an origin candidate.
Let C(E) denote the partition of evidence documents
into dependency clusters. Instead of summing documentlevel support, the model first aggregates within each cluster and then combines clusters. This prevents repeated
copies of one source from receiving the same weight as
multiple independent sources.
C. Hierarchical Decision and Abstention
A heterogeneous graph transformer encodes the
temporal-provenance graph conditioned on the claim. A
sufficiency head estimates p(s = 1 | x, E≤τ ). If evidence
is sufficient, a veracity head estimates supported versus
refuted. Otherwise, the system returns insufficient evidence. Temperature scaling is fitted on validation predictions, and a validation-selected threshold targets 80%

answer coverage. The final prediction record includes class
probabilities, evidence identifiers, source clusters, and the
enforced cutoff.
V. Experimental Protocol
A. Baselines
Controlled baselines include majority prediction, textonly classification, image–text fusion without retrieval, an
open multimodal language model under zero- and few-shot
prompting, vanilla multimodal RAG, time-filtered RAG
without provenance, provenance-aware RAG without time
filtering, RED-DOT where protocol compatibility permits,
and the complete model. Every controlled method uses the
same splits, candidate corpus, evaluation code, and 20-trial
validation budget.
B. Metrics and Statistical Analysis
The primary metric is macro-F1 on the 2025–2026
event-disjoint test set. Secondary metrics are per-class F1,
balanced accuracy, macro one-versus-rest AUROC, Brier
score, negative log-likelihood, expected calibration error,
area under the risk–coverage curve, risk at 80% coverage, Recall@10, nDCG@10, latency, GPU memory, and
retrieved-document count. Final configurations are evaluated with five seeds. Differences use 10,000 paired eventlevel bootstrap replicates, with Holm correction across the
primary comparison family.
C. Ablation and Stress Tests
The preregistered ablations remove temporal filtering,
dependency clustering, temporal edge features, graph aggregation, text evidence, visual evidence, the sufficiency
gate, and evidence-dropout training. Robustness tests
inject syndicated copies, add post-cutoff verdict-bearing
documents, remove the earliest observed source, introduce
dependent and independent conflicts, swap captions, and
reuse historical images.
D. Selection and Falsification Rules
Hyperparameters and the abstention threshold are
selected on validation data only. The provenanceperformance claim is removed if the component improves
neither natural future-event performance nor duplicateinjection robustness. The abstention claim is removed if
selective risk fails to improve at matched coverage. Any
advantage arising from post-cutoff evidence is labeled
leakage rather than superiority.

VII. Discussion, Ethics, and Limitations
The principal methodological risk is mistaking accessible web redundancy for independent corroboration. The
proposed dependency clusters are estimates based on observable content and links; they do not prove journalistic
independence or original authorship. Similarly, archived
timestamps indicate observed availability and may differ
from the true first publication time.
The corpus inherits professional fact-check labels and
their editorial definitions. Restriction to explicit rating
mappings reduces ambiguity but can create selection bias
and class imbalance. United States events and English
sources limit geographic and linguistic generalization.
Common Crawl and GDELT coverage is incomplete, and
removed or blocked content may be systematically different from recoverable content.
No copyrighted full text or images will be redistributed
without permission. The release will emphasize URLs,
hashes, derived metadata, split manifests, and collection
code. The system is intended to support research and
human review, not to serve as an autonomous censorship
or content-removal mechanism. A prediction of insufficient
evidence should trigger further investigation rather than
imply that a claim is false.
VIII. Conclusion
This paper defines a reproducible study of temporal and
evidence-source provenance in multimodal misinformation
detection. The central design combines time-constrained
retrieval, dependency-aware graph aggregation, evidencesufficiency prediction, and calibrated abstention. Its evaluation prioritizes future-event generalization, temporal
leakage, duplicate-source robustness, and selective risk.
The current artifact establishes the protocol and executable scaffold without presenting unmeasured findings.
Completion requires freezing the real corpus, running
controlled baselines and five-seed experiments, auditing all
outputs, and narrowing the final claims to the observed
evidence.
