ABSTRACT

An evidence selector cannot recover items missed by retrieval, and probability-based set decisions become unreliable when membership scores are miscalibrated. We evaluate these coupled failures on MOCHEG and under a separately frozen AVerImaTeC organizer-store protocol. On MOCHEG, label-blind rank fusion raises packet-oracle evidence F1 from 0.3560 to 0.3637, while prior-aware calibration reduces mean Brier score from 0.0847 to about 0.033. Calibrated thresholding reaches 0.1864 evidence F1; expected-F1 reaches 0.1815 with 3.61 rather than 5.26 items. A completeness-aware switch reaches 0.1877, but its +0.00138 effect is uncertain. On locked AVerImaTeC development, the frozen packet reaches only 25.48% of exact annotated sources and has no reachable target for 59.87% of claims. Calibration transfers: calibrated expected-F1 exceeds its uncalibrated ablation by 0.0532 F1 with a multiplicity-adjusted interval of [0.0221,0.0885]. The other relationships do not: thresholding and fixed selection are tied, expected-F1 returns 3.20 items versus 1.17 for thresholding, the switch interval crosses zero after correction, and switch-selected evidence fails verifier non-inferiority. The official semantic judge is unavailable locally and both benchmarks' formal tests remain untouched. The cross-benchmark evidence supports reachability-first, calibration-aware evaluation, but not adaptive-selector superiority or a universally compact expected-F1 policy.

multimodal fact-checking, evidence retrieval, evidence selection, probability calibration, expected F1, reproducibility

1. INTRODUCTION

A fact-check is auditable only when a reader can inspect the material behind its verdict. For an image-text claim, that material can include sentences, documents, and images drawn from different sources. A retrieval system may return hundreds of plausible candidates, but a reviewer still needs a short set that contains enough support or refutation to make a decision. MOCHEG formalizes this setting with multimodal claims, separate text and image evidence, released evidence identifiers, and three-way verdicts.

Evidence selection is often reduced to a fixed top-k rule. It is simple and reproducible, but it assumes that every claim needs the same number of items. Adaptive policies instead map candidate-level membership scores to a claim-specific set. A threshold returns every item above a cutoff, a count model predicts the set size, and expected-F1 decoding chooses the probability- ranked prefix with the greatest expected set F1. These rules share a dependency that rank-only evaluation can hide: the scores must behave like probabilities. A monotone transformation preserves candidate order while changing threshold and expected-utility decisions.

Class imbalance makes this dependency consequential. Gold evidence is rare in a retrieved packet, while balanced logistic training deliberately changes the effective class prior. A temperature map can adjust confidence without fixing an intercept error produced by that prior mismatch. Modern calibration work therefore distinguishes ranking, probability quality, and the decision made from those probabilities. In an evidence pipeline, an apparently stronger adaptive decoder may otherwise be exploiting or amplifying a calibration error.

Retrieval creates a separate, harder boundary. A selector cannot return an item that never enters its packet. If packet reachability is not reported, a retrieval miss and a decoding error receive the same final evidence score. Figure 1 shows the information boundaries used here. All selection policies receive the same 40 text and 20 image candidates. Candidate rankings are written before the gold join; scoring, calibration, policy selection, and evaluation use disjoint roles; and the released validation set is evaluated only after train-role choices are fixed.

The study asks four questions:

1. How much released gold evidence is reachable at the actual packet depths, and can a bounded label-blind rank fusion improve that ceiling? 2. Which probability maps correct the prior error induced by imbalanced evidence membership? 3. How do fixed top-k, calibrated thresholding, learned count, expected-F1, and an uncalibrated ablation compare on identical packets? 4. Does a prespecified packet-completeness switch beat calibrated thresholding and preserve verdict quality under a stronger shared verifier? 5. Which of these relationships reproduce under a separately frozen AVerImaTeC organizer-store protocol with different evidence units and four verdict classes?

The answers are deliberately mixed. Weighted text fusion improves the all-eligible packet oracle from 0.3560 to 0.3637 F1 and complete-gold coverage from 4.39% to 4.74%, while image Recall@20 does not change. Prior-aware maps repair the dominant probability error. On the repaired packet, calibrated thresholding reaches 0.1864 evidence F1, expected-F1 reaches 0.1815 with a smaller 3.61-item set, and uncalibrated expected-F1 collapses to 0.1288 while returning 24.90 items. The completeness-aware switch has the highest point estimate, 0.1877 F1, but improves over thresholding by only 0.00138 with an interval that crosses zero. Its verifier result passes aggregate non-inferiority but not the per-seed safeguard. The independent evaluation is more restrictive: its retained validation packet reaches only 25.48% of exact annotated sources and has no reachable target for 59.87% of claims. Calibration remains decisive, improving expected-F1 by 0.0532 over its uncalibrated ablation. Threshold superiority, expected-F1 compactness, switch superiority, and downstream verifier preservation do not reproduce.

This paper makes four contributions:

- a retrieval-conditioned evaluation that reports operating-depth recall, packet-oracle F1, complete-gold coverage, and zero-reachable claims before interpreting selector quality; - a matched calibration and set-decoding study covering six probability maps, fixed and adaptive controls, exact expected-F1, and an uncalibrated failure case; - a prespecified retrieval repair and packet-completeness-aware switch, evaluated against the strongest calibrated-threshold comparator with claim-aligned intervals and partition-level safeguards; and - a strictly split-isolated and auditable external evaluation on the organizer's static AVerImaTeC store, preserving label-blind rankings, per-claim records, failed exports, and independent audits while confirming calibration but not selector superiority, compactness, or verifier preservation.

The contribution is empirical rather than a claim of state-of-the-art performance. Adaptive evidence sizing is useful only after retrieval and calibration are exposed as experimental variables. In this study, expected-F1 supplies a precision-oriented MOCHEG operating point and the completeness-aware switch supplies a testable design direction, but calibrated thresholding is not externally superior to fixed selection and expected-F1 is not externally compact. The paper retains these negative boundaries because they show that a selector conclusion can reverse when reachability, evidence units, and class balance change.

2. RELATED WORK

2.1 Evidence-Based Fact-Checking and Evaluation

Evidence-based verification can typically be broken down into four components: claim analysis, evidence retrieval, evidence assessment and verdict prediction. The findings vary with the domain, label construction, modality coverage, and controls for event or source overlap, as documented in dataset surveys. Difficulties with cross-domain transfer, deficient explanations, and benchmark protocols remain common. Our assessment therefore follows the retrieval-and-selection chain rather than content-only classification.

Evidence requirements are revealed through text benchmarks. SciFact contains sentence evidence from scientific publications, HoVer is multi-document reasoning, COVID-Fact is following evolving claims in the public-health arena, and X-Fact introduces multilingual variation. External-document systems minimize reliance on stylistic shortcuts, while also incurring retrieval, freshness, and attribution errors. So, we separate evidence-set quality and verdict accuracy.

2.2 Multimodal Misinformation Detection

Content-based multimodal detectors include bilinear pooling, hierarchical attention, topic memory and cross-attention, ambiguity learning and multi-level fusion detectors. Contrastive, in-modality, stylometric or semantic-alignment signals are added in later systems. These designs help to classify at the post level; however, the fused representation does not reveal the source of a verdict, such as an external document or image.

Recent works focus on local-global mismatch, cross-modal knowledge distillation, contextual inconsistency and conflicting unimodal signals. Out-of-context benchmarks like NewsCLIPpings, COSMOS, and VERITE evaluate pairs of images and captions where they do not match in context but control unimodal bias. DGM4, SNIFFER, MMDFND, and IMFND extend localization, explanation, domain coverage or vision-language model usage. They are not primarily aimed at a set of external evidence identifiers that can be audited, but at manipulation or veracity.

2.3 Multimodal Evidence Retrieval and Verification

MOCHEG offers a common evaluation criterion for multimodal retrieval, three-way verdict prediction and explanation generation. MEVER builds a text-image evidence graph and performs token- and evidence-level fusion, while RED-DOT identifies and reranks external multimodal evidence before prediction. Other recent studies distinguish utility from semantic similarity and learn when visual retrieval is needed. These systems address fixed retrieval, relevance filtering, modality routing, utility ranking, and adaptive set decoding as different decision problems.

AVerImaTeC assesses real-world image-text claims using open-web or organizer-provided knowledge, and its shared task conditions verdict scoring on evidence quality. In that context, REVEAL combines iterative sparse and dense retrieval. MOCHEG's stable item corpus differs from question-answer evidence and open-web access, so transfer requires a separately frozen unit definition and candidate-construction protocol.

2.4 Scientific and Citation-Grounded Evidence

Stable evidence locations in passages, tables and figures are provided by scientific verification. SciVer consists of claim-evidence instances extracted from scientific papers and annotated by experts; MuSciClaims focuses on figure-grounded claims and controlled reasoning perturbations; and M^2-TabFact covers tables presented across text and images. These tools can be used to make more precise localization analysis, but they do not explicitly reflect political or breaking-news verification.

An explanation might be expressed effectively, but with the use of the incorrect source. Evidence-rationale benchmarks check whether a prediction is connected to a selected span, and faithful-by-construction rationalizers limit predictors to explicit evidence subsets. Citation-grounded generation assesses whether retrieved sources support generated statements; RAGTruth catalogs hallucinations that persist despite retrieval; and citation masking tests whether a fact-checking explanation recovers the correct evidence. The present study adopts released evidence identifiers rather than generated rationales and allows exact-set precision, recall, F1, Jaccard similarity and exact match.

2.5 Calibration and Adaptive Set Decisions

A score may rank a candidate correctly while remaining a poor probability. Post-hoc calibration includes temperature and Platt mappings, beta calibration, and nonparametric isotonic regression; each makes different assumptions about the distortion being corrected. After class weighting, prior correction is important because the optimized training distribution differs from the operating distribution. The calibrated membership distribution can then support expected-utility decoding, while structurally different policies such as top-k, thresholding, and learned count use the same scores.

In this study, representation models and evidence identities remain fixed while a bounded, label-blind rank fusion changes only the retained ordering. The selector experiment then evaluates packet reachability, prior-aware probability correction, exact expected-F1 decoding, and one prespecified completeness-aware switch under matched candidates, tuning roles, and downstream verification. A separately frozen organizer-store run tests the same relationships on AVerImaTeC without transferring fitted MOCHEG parameters. This design isolates which conclusions are benchmark-specific and which survive a change in evidence units, candidate construction, and class balance (in the summary table).

3. PROBLEM DEFINITION AND DATA

3.1 Task

Let claim q have a released complete-gold evidence set G_q and let retrieval return an ordered packet

which can hold a maximum of 40 text candidates and 20 image candidates. A selector returns S_q ⊆ C_q. The main claim-level score is

We macro-average set F1 over claims. Other evidence metrics are precision, recall, Jaccard similarity, exact-set match and selected count.

The primary population includes only claims for which the released annotation defines a nonempty complete-gold set. Claims without such a mapping remain in retrieval construction and do not get a perfect oracle score. If they are given an oracle F1 of one, this will give the false impression of a high apparent packet ceiling.

3.2 Dataset and Population Flow

MOCHEG is a dataset that includes image-text claims, separate image and text evidence as well as verdict labels. By content hash we pin released versions of the train, validation, corpus and sealed-test. The test annotations are not opened in the official test.

The 13,159 released train and validation claims are encoded for retrieval. Of these, 10,970 have a nonempty released complete-gold mapping and are the eligible evidence-set population. The primary repaired-packet selector evaluation uses the 1,490 released validation claims. Claim-grouped hashing places the remaining released claims in fitting, calibration, model-selection, and internal-holdout roles. The roles are regenerated separately for deterministic partition seeds 13, 37, 73, 101, and 137.

3.3 Independent AVerImaTeC Population

AVerImaTeC changes the evidence unit and label space rather than supplying an unchanged MOCHEG replication. Each candidate is a claim-local organizer-store text chunk or image associated with a source. The official output permits at most ten evidence items and one of four labels: Supported, Refuted, Not Enough Evidence, or Conflicting Evidence/Cherrypicking. Exact normalized source identity is available for an auditable diagnostic; the task's primary semantic evidence score requires its separate multimodal judge.

The external protocol uses all 793 released training claims for fitting and selection roles and reserves all 152 development claims for one locked run. Training contains 756 Refuted, 18 Not Enough Evidence, 13 Supported, and 6 Conflicting claims; development contains 141, 6, 4, and 1, respectively. The organizer archives contain 1,586 training JSON files, 81,817 training images, 304 development JSON files, and 49,617 development images. No MOCHEG model, threshold, calibrator, completeness estimator, or verifier weight is transferred.

For an external claim, G_q denotes normalized annotated source identities and C_q the frozen organizer-store packet. Set F1 is reported as an exact-source diagnostic because an alternative valid webpage can be semantically correct without sharing the annotated URL. We therefore do not relabel exact-source F1 as the unavailable official semantic score.

3.4 Reachability

The retrieval boundary is specified by two oracle summaries. For every eligible claim, the packet oracle returns C_q∩ G_q. Its F1 is averaged only for claims with nonempty gold. Complete-gold coverage is the fraction of eligible claims for which G_q ⊆ C_q holds. We count eligible claims for which C_q∩ G_q=empty set.

These quantities provide answers to different questions. Oracle F1 is the best evidence set that can be achieved from the packet. Complete-gold coverage asks whether exact recovery can be made. The zero-reachable count identifies claims on which every selector must receive zero recall. None of these measures gives credit to a selector for evidence not exposed in the process of retrieval.

3.5 Leakage Controls

Candidate ranks are written and hashed before gold identifiers are joined. Only after candidate construction are evidence labels used to fit the membership scorer, calibration map, and selector hyperparameters in their respective training roles. The locked validation set is not part of any fitted component. For AVerImaTeC, the freeze rejects any artifact containing a non-training row; validation preparation starts only after the train-only code and manifest are committed, and the locked runner accepts only validation rows while rechecking every frozen input hash. Formal-test annotations for both benchmarks are absent from the execution path; seal records and access flags are checked before and after outcome-bearing runs.

4. METHOD

4.1 Label-Blind Retrieval and Weighted Fusion

The text branch combines BGE-M3 dense sentence retrieval with BM25, then records a BGE reranker ordering. The image branch records SigLIP2 image-text similarity and dense similarity between the claim and normalized image file names. Model repositories and immutable revisions are fixed in the configuration; the operational model cards are BGE-M3, BGE-reranker-v2-m3, and SigLIP2.

For two label-blind ranks r_a(c) and r_b(c), the bounded repair assigns candidate c the score

The text score combines the existing dense-lexical fused rank with the cross-encoder rank; the image score combines SigLIP2 and filename ranks. Weights w in {0.25,0.50,0.75} are selected separately by training-role packet-oracle F1, subject to operating-depth recall guardrails. Both branches select w=0.50. Rankings and hashes are written before the gold join. The retained packet contains the top 40 text and 20 image items, and every selector receives exactly that packet.

4.2 Organizer-Store Retrieval for External Evaluation

The external path uses the AVerImaTeC static store rather than adapting the MOCHEG packet. Text records are normalized and reduced to a deterministic claim-conditioned chunk of at most 1,500 characters. BM25 and BGE-M3 ranks are combined by equal-weight reciprocal-rank fusion with constant 60, and the first 100 fused candidates are reranked by the pinned BGE cross-encoder. Actual candidate images are loaded from the claim-matched store and ranked by SigLIP2; a BGE representation of normalized filenames supplies a second image rank, also fused with equal-weight RRF. Reverse-image-search webpage text remains text evidence rather than being mislabeled as an image.

Each candidate identifier is a hash of claim identity, normalized source, modality, content hash, and local ordinal. The label-blind preparation retains up to 100 text and 20 image candidates, writes and hashes that ranking, and only then joins exact-source targets. Training roles choose text width from {50,100} and image width from {10,20}; all five seeds select 50 text and 10 image items. The organizer store was partly constructed with gold-question-derived queries, so the resulting reachability is a benchmark condition rather than unrestricted web-search performance.

A separate map is fitted for each modality. There are six maps which are compared:

1. identity, with no effect on the raw sigmoid probability; 2. temperature scaling, sigma(z/T); 3. prior shift, which fits an intercept correction; 4. Platt mapping, sigma(az+b); 5. beta calibration, linear in p and (1-p); and 6. isotonic regression, a monotone nonparametric map.

Calibration parameters are fitted on the calibration role. The scorer and map are selected based on average precision and Brier score in the different model-selection task. We report the Brier score, the negative log likelihood (NLL), the expected calibration error (ECE), the mean predicted probability, the empirical prevalence and the Brier skill relative to the predictor which forecasts the prevalence only.

The separation ensures that a calibrator cannot be picked on the locked-validation result. Average precision screens the ordering of the scorer, while Brier score selects the probability map for threshold and expected-utility decisions. The difference lies in the fact that any monotone map will maintain the rank, but can significantly change the set that is chosen.

4.4 Evidence-Set Policies

Figure 2 shows the only operation that changes: how one shared calibrated ranking is translated to an evidence set.

Fixed top-k.

We tune k in {1,2,3,5,10,20,40,60} and select the top k candidates. The control that is created is top-5.

Calibrated threshold.

If the threshold tau is specified, the selector returns all items for which p_qi >= tau, within the usual packet limit. The threshold is selected from {0.1, ..., 0.9}.

Learned count.

The number of evidence items is predicted by a count model and the corresponding probability-ranked prefix is returned.

Expected-F1 decoding.

The candidates are ranked based on calibrated probability. A dynamic program exactly computes the expected F1 for each prefix length k, for independent Bernoulli membership variables. The selected size is

We also carry out testing of two extensions. The first subtracts lambda k from expected utility. The second also subtracts gamma R(S_q,k), where R measures same-source redundancy. The size penalties range from 0 to 0.10 and the redundancy penalties range from 0 to 0.005. Extra structure in the decoder is kept as ablations, as the fitted optima indicate whether it provides additional benefit over calibration.

4.5 Packet-Completeness-Aware Switching

The diagnostic results suggest that compact expected-F1 selection is most useful when the retrieved packet already contains the complete released-gold set. We therefore fit a low-capacity logistic model to the training-role target I(G_q ⊆ C_q). Its ten frozen features summarize expected relevant mass, top and top-five probabilities, probability entropy and rank gaps, threshold and expected-F1 set sizes, modality-specific probability mass, and dense-lexical rank agreement. Standardization and L2 logistic fitting use only fitting claims; the decision cutoff is selected from {0.05,0.10,0.20,0.30,0.50} on model-selection claims.

4.6 External Refitting and Decision Caps

Every fitted component is re-estimated from AVerImaTeC training roles. For each seed, 55% of claims fit a balanced L2 logistic membership scorer, 15% fit candidate calibrators, 15% choose the calibrator, packet width, fixed budget, threshold, and switch cutoff, and 15% form an internal holdout. At most 40 deterministic negatives per claim enter scorer fitting. Identity, temperature, prior-shift, Platt, beta, and isotonic maps compete by model-selection Brier score; no development row is loaded by the freeze.

The official ten-item cap changes the external policy grids. Fixed selection uses k in 1,3,5,10,  thresholding uses tau in 0.1,,0.9,  and expected-F1 searches prefixes from zero through ten. The completeness model is fit on calibration-role claims, its cutoff is selected on the model-selection role, and it routes to expected-F1 when predicted complete and to thresholding otherwise. This reuses algorithm families, not MOCHEG weights or decisions.

4.7 Decision Scope and Assumptions

The comparison alters just the set decision. The identity of the candidates, their rank, feature values, scorer outputs, and validation claims are shared across policies. Expected-F1 assumes that the memberships are conditionally independent Bernoullis, and that it only operates on prefixes of the frozen ranking, i.e., it does not reorder candidates or retrieve a missing item. The complete-gold mapping released is considered the evaluation target, where Section 9 comments on possible incompleteness of the mapping. These boundaries help to differentiate between a decoder error and retrieval error and help to prevent an adaptive policy from being given a more favorable candidate universe.

4.8 Shared Verifier

Evidence policies can improve set overlap while damaging the downstream verdict. We therefore hold verifier architecture, training data, features, and optimization constant while changing only the selected evidence. The primary verifier is a two-layer multilayer perceptron with one 64-unit ReLU hidden layer, inverse-frequency class weighting, 300 full-batch Adam epochs, learning rate 0.01, and L2 coefficient 0.001. It is trained on unique fixed, threshold, and expected-F1 evidence sets from fitting claims. A logistic linear verifier is retained as a capacity control. Both models compare calibrated thresholding with the frozen completeness-aware switch on the same 1,490 validation claims and five deterministic seeds. The declared non-inferiority margin is -0.01 macro-F1.

For AVerImaTeC, the same safeguard is refit for four classes. The primary model has one 64-unit ReLU layer and is optimized for 400 deterministic full-batch steps with learning rate 0.01, inverse-frequency weights, and L2=0.001; a balanced multinomial linear model is the capacity control. Selected evidence is represented by the mean, maximum, and standard deviation of candidate features plus set size, modality fractions, and distinct-domain count. The frozen model is shared across policies. When the prespecified blind rule selected the fixed family, its verdicts were obtained by deterministic inference from this already frozen verifier; no refitting or development selection was introduced.

5. EXPERIMENTAL DESIGN

5.1 Frozen Protocols and Claim Boundary

The diagnostic configuration was fixed on 4 November 2026 before its outcome-bearing run. It records data and model revisions, five claim-group partition seeds, retrieval depths, scorer families, calibration candidates, selector grids, evaluation metrics, bootstrap settings, and stopping rules. The bounded retrieval repair, completeness-aware switch, and stronger verifier were frozen on 9 November 2026 before their respective outcomes were inspected. The numeric threshold grid and verifier architecture were recorded before the affected models were fitted. All amendments and failed runs remain in an append-only experiment ledger.

The external implementation was committed before development preparation, and the five-seed train-only manifest was committed on 11 November 2026. That manifest records code, configuration, archive, pre-join ranking, candidate, gold-map, and audit hashes and rejects a mixed train-development input. The 152-claim development matrix was then executed once on 12 November 2026.

The released MOCHEG validation split had been used earlier in the project and is therefore controlled development evidence, not fresh confirmation. The formal-test annotations are absent from the execution path and remain sealed. No validation outcome automatically authorizes a formal-test run.

5.2 Independent External Protocol

AVerImaTeC training claims are deterministically partitioned for seeds 13, 37, 73, 101, and 137 into 55% fit, 15% calibration, 15% model selection, and 15% internal holdout roles. The locked development run compares train-selected fixed top-k, calibrated thresholding, calibrated expected-F1, uncalibrated expected-F1, and the completeness-aware switch on the same candidates and membership scores. No development result changes retrieval, fitting, calibration, packet width, or policy parameters.

Four claim-aligned comparisons form one multiplicity family: threshold minus fixed, expected-F1 minus threshold, calibrated minus uncalibrated expected-F1, and switch minus threshold. Each uses 10,000 bootstrap draws, Bonferroni simultaneous intervals, and Holm-adjusted two-sided values. Threshold superiority requires an effect of at least +0.010 and a positive adjusted lower bound. Expected-F1 must clear a -0.010 non-inferiority margin and return at least 15% fewer items. Switch superiority additionally requires every seed effect to be positive and no increase in mean set size. Verifier preservation uses the same -0.010 margin.

The organizer's semantic evaluator uses Gemma-3-27B in bfloat16. Its 54.86 GB of weight files do not fit the single 24.56 GiB RTX 4090 under the prescribed condition, and the organizer launcher specifies two GPUs. We therefore report exact normalized-source diagnostics and class-wise verifier results, not a replacement score labeled as official. The blind test remains outside the authorized execution boundary.

5.3 Baselines, Outcomes, and Selection Budgets

The matched policies are fixed top-5, calibrated thresholding, learned count, calibrated expected-F1, uncalibrated expected-F1, and the packet-completeness-aware switch. The primary outcome is claim-macro complete-gold evidence F1. Precision, recall, Jaccard similarity, exact-set match, selected count, calibration metrics, reachability, and verdict macro-F1 are secondary outcomes.

Scorer and calibration selection uses training roles only. The repair examines three declared weights per retrieval branch. Thresholding searches 0.1,,0.9,  and the completeness cutoff searches five declared values. No additional retrieval branch is opened because the weighted-fusion advancement gate passes. These budgets prevent outcome-driven expansion of the search after locked validation is observed.

For each comparison, claim-level differences are first averaged within claim over the five deterministic partitions. A claim-aligned bootstrap then resamples the 1,490 claims 10,000 times. The selector-superiority gate requires all of the following against calibrated thresholding: at least +0.010 evidence F1, a positive paired 95% interval after multiplicity control, positive effects in all five partitions, and no increase in mean selected-set size. The verifier uses a -0.010 non-inferiority margin. We report both the aggregate seed-aligned interval and the stricter requirement that every seed-specific lower bound clear the margin.

5.4 Robustness and Diagnostic Analyses

The original diagnostic packet is also evaluated under widths 10 and 20, text-only and image-only evidence, deterministic removal of 25% and 50% of reachable gold, promotion of five distractors, duplication of the top three items, and probability temperatures of 0.5 and 2.0. Fitted selectors do not change under these perturbations. The conditions test dependence on reachability, modality, ranking contamination, duplication, and probability scale; they are sensitivity analyses rather than estimates of production shift.

Following the primary comparison, validation claims are divided into no, partial, or complete released-gold reachability. This stratification is post hoc and descriptive. It motivates the prespecified completeness-aware switch but does not turn the earlier subgroup result into a confirmatory claim.

5.5 Implementation, Hardware, and Audit

Every run stores candidate-level scores, calibrated probabilities, selected identifiers, claim-level metrics, fitted parameters, environment metadata, and formal-test access flags. Independent audits recompute population counts, set metrics, paired effects, and gate conditions from saved records. Label-blind rankings are hashed before gold is joined. Partial outputs from four failed runs are preserved with the repair reason rather than overwritten.

The weighted retrieval run takes 9.5 seconds, candidate construction 8.4 seconds, base selector evaluation 158.8 seconds, completeness switching 334.8 seconds, and the stronger verifier 246.9 seconds on this machine. These are wall-clock records for reproducibility, not cross-system efficiency results. The faithful organizer-store preparation takes 50.5 minutes for 793 training claims and 21.2 minutes for 152 development claims; the locked five-seed comparison takes 19 seconds after preparation. These times include text encoding, cross-encoder reranking, and actual-image SigLIP2 scoring. One 144-million-pixel development image triggers a PIL safety warning but is processed without exclusion, OOM, or fallback.

6. RESULTS

6.1 A Modest Retrieval Repair Raises the Ceiling

Before repair, the retained packet reaches text Recall@40 of 0.3193, image Recall@20 of 0.2663, packet-oracle evidence F1 of 0.3560, and complete-gold coverage of 4.39% over 10,970 eligible claims. A total of 3,086 claims have no reachable released-gold item. Training-role selection chooses equal weight for the existing fused and cross-encoder text ranks and equal weight for the two image ranks.

On all eligible claims, the selected fusion raises text Recall@40 to 0.3292, packet-oracle F1 to 0.3637, and complete-gold coverage to 4.74%. The number of zero-reachable claims falls by 59 to 3,027. Image Recall@20 remains 0.2663. Thus the declared advancement gate passes, but the aspirational engineering targets of a +0.05 oracle-F1 gain and 8% complete-gold coverage do not.

The locked-validation comparison in the table shows the same direction. Oracle F1 improves by 0.00612 and complete-gold coverage by 0.604 percentage points; image recall is exactly unchanged. This is a useful packet repair, not a new retrieval state of the art.

The deeper retrieval audit explains the bounded search. Dense-BM25 rank fusion has text Recall@50 of 0.3224. Cross-encoder reranking increases it to 0.3338 while reducing early Recall@5 from 0.1772 to 0.1335. For images, the SigLIP2-filename fusion reaches Recall@50 of 0.3149, compared with 0.2183 for SigLIP2 and 0.2227 for filename retrieval. The repair therefore fuses the text ranks instead of replacing the stronger early ordering; it does not add an outcome-driven image branch after the advancement gate passes.

The earlier reachability stratification remains diagnostically useful. In the 79 validation claims where the original packet contains the complete released-gold set, expected-F1 reaches 0.5240 F1, compared with 0.3593 for fixed top-5 and 0.4237 for thresholding. Across 1,200 partially reachable claims, expected-F1 reaches 0.1839 and does not improve on fixed top-5 at 0.1841; thresholding is best at 0.1965. All policies score zero on the 211 zero-reachable claims. This post hoc result motivates a completeness-aware decision, but it is not itself a prespecified subgroup claim.

6.2 Prior-Aware Maps Repair the Main Calibration Error

The table compares probability maps on locked validation, averaged across modalities and deterministic partitions. Identity and temperature scaling have Brier scores of 0.0847 and 0.0832. Prior shift, Platt, beta, and isotonic mappings reduce the error sharply; isotonic reaches about 0.0330 and lowers ECE from 0.1314 to 0.0063 relative to identity. Each selected partition has positive Brier skill against a prevalence-only predictor. A slope-only temperature does not repair the dominant intercept error created by the changed training prior.

Calibration also changes scorer selection. Partitions 13 and 37 choose modality-specific balanced models, partition 73 chooses the unweighted cross-encoder scorer, and partitions 101 and 137 choose unweighted modality-specific models. Text calibration varies across isotonic, Platt, and beta maps, while every image branch selects isotonic regression. Scorer family, loss weighting, and calibration therefore form one selection problem; ranking performance alone does not determine the deployed evidence set.

6.3 Thresholding Is Confirmed; Completeness Switching Is Inconclusive

The table reports the matched comparison on the repaired packet. Fixed top-5 reaches 0.1712 evidence F1. Calibrated thresholding reaches 0.1864 with 5.26 items; its gain over fixed top-5 is +0.01517 with a claim-aligned 95% interval of [0.01114,0.01923]. Expected-F1 reaches 0.1815 with 3.61 items. It improves over fixed top-5 by +0.01034 [0.00501,0.01574], but remains 0.00484 below thresholding [-0.00938,-0.00020] for the expected-F1-minus-threshold comparison.

The uncalibrated ablation exposes why probability quality matters. It returns 24.90 items per claim and falls to 0.1288 F1, despite operating on the same candidate order. Learned count is similarly diffuse, selecting 31.49 items and reaching 0.1122 F1. The gain from expected-F1 is therefore not simply an effect of using a variable set size; it depends on probabilities that reflect the rare operating membership rate.

The completeness-aware switch uses expected-F1 for 37.8-46.4% of validation claims across partitions and thresholding otherwise. Every partition selects the prespecified cutoff 0.50. Its mean point estimate, 0.1877 F1 with 4.70 items, is the highest tested value. However, its gain over thresholding is only +0.00138, with a 95% interval of [-0.00191,0.00462]; one of five partition effects is negative. It fails the +0.010 practical-effect requirement, the all-positive requirement, and the positive-lower-bound requirement. The switch reduces review-set size without a confirmed loss, but it does not establish selector superiority.

The earlier size and same-source redundancy penalties remain exact matches to plain expected-F1 because all five training-role partitions select zero for both penalties. Adding these decoder terms increases complexity without changing any selected set on the diagnostic packet.

6.4 Aggregate Verifier Non-Inferiority Is Not Seed-Stable

The stronger shared verifier changes the downstream conclusion from a simple failure to a qualified safeguard. With calibrated-threshold evidence, the MLP reaches mean macro-F1 0.3911; with switch-selected evidence it reaches 0.3954. The seed-aligned paired effect is +0.00434 with a 95% interval of [-0.00277,0.01130]. Its lower bound exceeds the frozen -0.010 margin, so aggregate non-inferiority passes, but the interval crosses zero and does not show superiority.

Only seeds 37 and 73 individually clear the non-inferiority margin. Seeds 13, 101, and 137 have lower bounds of -0.01367, -0.02174, and -0.01406, respectively. The linear control also reverses the direction: macro-F1 falls from 0.3787 under thresholding to 0.3746 under the switch. Verdict preservation is therefore supported only after seed aggregation under the stronger model, not as an architecture- and seed-stable benefit.

6.5 Probability Perturbations Reverse the Adaptive Gain

On the original diagnostic packet, expected-F1 remains positive relative to fixed top-5 under widths 20 and 40, text-only evidence, and deterministic gold removal, but falls slightly behind on image-only packets. Promoting five distractors creates a large relative gain only because fixed top-5 is forced to select those distractors; it is not representative of normal operation.

Probability scale is the fragile dimension. Sharpening with temperature 0.5 makes the expected-F1-minus-fixed effect -0.0193; flattening with temperature 2.0 makes it -0.0221. Both intervals exclude zero even though candidate rankings are unchanged. This rules out a broad robustness claim and reinforces the need to report calibration jointly with adaptive set decisions.

Together, the results separate four conclusions that a single end-to-end score would obscure. Retrieval repair raises but does not remove the ceiling; prior-aware calibration is necessary; expected-F1 offers a compact precision-oriented set; and the completeness-aware switch is promising but inconclusive against calibrated thresholding. The formal test remains sealed, so all four conclusions are limited to controlled MOCHEG development evidence.

7. INDEPENDENT EXTERNAL EVALUATION

The AVerImaTeC run tests relationships rather than equality of raw scores: its candidate store, evidence unit, output cap, and four-class verdict task differ from MOCHEG. All results in this section are from the single locked development run after the five train-only seeds were committed. The official semantic judge was not executed; evidence values use exact normalized source identity and are labeled diagnostic throughout.

7.1 The Organizer Store Does Not Remove the Retrieval Ceiling

The training preparation ranks 529,580 text candidates and 81,817 images for 793 claims; development ranks 101,289 text candidates and 49,617 images for 152 claims. Every claim has store candidates, yet exact annotated targets are usually absent from the retained packet. The table shows the distinction between candidate availability and target reachability.

Development Recall@10, @20, and @50 is 0.0526, 0.0696, and 0.1143. The 100-text/20-image retained pool reaches 25.48% of annotated targets, fully reaches only 12.50% of claims, and has no exact reachable target for 91 of 152 claims. These values limit every downstream policy and explain why absolute exact-source F1 remains low.

7.2 Only the Calibration Relationship Reproduces

The table averages each claim across the five frozen seeds before averaging across claims. Calibrated expected-F1 has the highest point estimate, 0.1029, but returns 3.20 items. Thresholding returns only 1.17 items and is effectively tied with the train-selected fixed family. The uncalibrated ablation selects 9.93 of the ten allowed items and falls to 0.0497 F1, reproducing the dependence of expected-utility decoding on probability quality.

The four comparisons in the table use 10,000 aligned bootstrap draws. Their bounds are Bonferroni simultaneous 98.75% intervals; the p-values are Holm adjusted. Threshold superiority fails. Expected-F1 is non-inferior in exact-source F1 because its adjusted lower bound is above -0.010, but it returns 173.96% more items than thresholding instead of at least 15% fewer. Calibration is the only passed selector hypothesis. The switch effect is positive in every seed, but the adjusted interval crosses zero and its set is larger than thresholding's.

7.3 The Downstream Safeguard Fails Externally

Mean verifier macro-F1 is 0.1573 with threshold evidence and 0.1444 with switch evidence. The seed-aligned switch-minus-threshold effect is -0.01293 with 95% interval [-0.03242,0.00687]; its lower bound does not clear the declared -0.010 margin. The switch therefore fails verifier non-inferiority, unlike the aggregate MOCHEG result.

The class distribution is extreme: 141 Refuted, six Not Enough Evidence, four Supported, and one Conflicting claim. Neither policy resolves the single conflicting-class case across seeds, and accuracy mostly reflects the majority class. This is why macro- and class-wise F1, rather than accuracy alone, define the safeguard.

7.4 Frozen Decision

The blind-selection rule chooses the switch only if its full gate passes, otherwise expected-F1 only if it is non-inferior and at least 15% smaller, otherwise thresholding only if it beats fixed selection, and otherwise the train-selected fixed family. The first three conditions fail, so the frozen decision falls back to fixed selection. Local development exports for all five seeds pass the official ten-item and 1,500-character schema after applying the already frozen verifier. No test prediction or leaderboard submission is performed. The external result is therefore an independent diagnostic nonreplication, not an official shared-task score.

8. DISCUSSION

8.1 Calibration Changes the Decision, Not the Ranking

Expected-F1 requires probabilities with operational meaning: among items given probability 0.30, roughly 30% should be evidence members under comparable conditions. Balanced logistic training improves rare-positive ranking but changes the effective training prior. Temperature scaling changes logit slope, whereas prior shift, Platt, beta, and isotonic mappings can also repair the location or shape of the reliability curve. The uncalibrated expected-F1 ablation makes this distinction concrete: it selects nearly 25 items per claim and loses more than five evidence-F1 points relative to calibrated expected-F1, with no change to the candidate universe.

This is the one relationship that reproduces cleanly on AVerImaTeC. There, uncalibrated expected-F1 nearly saturates the ten-item cap and loses 0.0532 exact-source F1 to its calibrated counterpart. The positive simultaneous interval survives multiplicity correction despite different candidate units, class balance, and independently fitted models. Calibration is therefore not an explanation fitted only to the MOCHEG packet.

This also explains why fitted size and redundancy penalties are unnecessary in the diagnostic experiment. Once the low membership prevalence is restored, the unpenalized expected utility already favors a short prefix. Adaptive selector studies should report probability quality, prevalence, and selected set size alongside rank metrics; otherwise the decoder can appear to improve or fail for the wrong reason.

8.2 Why Thresholding Remains the Strong Baseline

Expected-F1 combines all membership probabilities under a conditional- independence approximation. A tuned threshold uses the same calibrated probabilities with fewer structural assumptions. On the repaired packet, thresholding is 0.00484 F1 stronger and recovers more evidence, while expected-F1 returns 1.64 fewer items and has higher precision. The latter is a valid review-cost operating point, but it is not the strongest selector by the primary metric.

The external result narrows this conclusion. AVerImaTeC thresholding returns the smallest set but is tied with the train-selected fixed family, whereas expected-F1 reaches a higher point estimate by selecting 3.20 rather than 1.17 items. Expected-F1 clears the non-inferiority margin but fails the required size reduction. Thresholding remains a necessary comparator, not a universally superior policy, and compactness does not transfer automatically.

The completeness-aware switch tests whether these policies can be combined without simply choosing the better one on validation. Its point estimate is higher than thresholding and its mean set is smaller, but the improvement is too small and unstable to support superiority. This negative result is useful: packet completeness is a plausible routing variable, yet the present ten- feature logistic estimate is not accurate enough to produce a reliable method-level gain. A future model should first improve completeness prediction under a frozen protocol, rather than add another decoder penalty.

8.3 Retrieval Is Still the Dominant Constraint

Weighted fusion reduces zero-reachable claims and raises the packet oracle, but the all-eligible ceiling remains only 0.3637 F1 and complete-gold coverage remains below 5%. The absolute gain is far smaller than the declared engineering target. Most selector errors therefore cannot be repaired by a different prefix rule alone.

The organizer-store evaluation is more severe under exact-source identity: 59.87% of development claims have no reachable annotated target and only 12.50% have the complete annotated set in the retained pool. The static store contains many candidates, so the bottleneck is shallow ranking and source identity rather than gross data absence. This cross-benchmark agreement on the reachability boundary is stronger than agreement on any selector ordering.

Text and image branches fail differently. Cross-encoder text ranking helps at greater depth while damaging early order; equal rank fusion recovers a small part of that tradeoff. Image fusion already combines two complementary signals, and changing their weight does not improve Recall@20. OCR, article context, or a table-aware external benchmark may help, but each would change the candidate construction and requires a separately frozen study. They are not hidden extensions of the present result.

8.4 Evidence and Verdict Safeguards Answer Different Questions

Evidence F1 measures overlap with released evidence identifiers; verdict macro-F1 measures whether selected evidence supports the final three-way decision. The stronger MLP verifier clears aggregate non-inferiority for the switch, but the interval crosses zero, three seed-specific safeguards fail, and the linear control moves in the opposite direction. The result is compatible with preserved verdict quality on average, but not with a stable end-to-end benefit.

The AVerImaTeC safeguard is less favorable. Switch evidence reduces mean macro-F1 from 0.1573 to 0.1444, and the seed-aligned lower bound falls below the -0.010 margin. The single Conflicting claim receives zero F1 under both policies across seeds, while Refuted dominates the 152-claim split. This shows why an evidence-set improvement cannot be promoted without class-wise downstream reporting.

This distinction matters for system reporting. Evidence precision, evidence recall, set size, retrieval reachability, calibration, and verdict performance should be shown separately. A single score would hide whether a compact set saved reviewer effort, omitted necessary support, or merely interacted favorably with one verifier seed.

8.5 What Transfers Externally

The external study does not reproduce a superior adaptive selector. It does reproduce two scientific dependencies: selection is bounded by candidate reachability, and expected-utility decoding depends on calibrated operating probabilities. It contradicts three more specific MOCHEG observations: thresholding does not beat the fixed family, expected-F1 is not a smaller operating point, and the completeness switch does not preserve verifier quality. These contradictions are not reasons to retune the external protocol; they define the empirical scope of the method claims.

Exact URL identity can mark a semantically valid alternative source as wrong, so the external result is a controlled diagnostic rather than the official shared-task result. The unavailable Gemma-3-27B judge prevents a direct semantic-score comparison, and the blind test remains untouched. Even with that limitation, the direction of the calibration ablation and the failed prespecified gates are valid within the released exact-source target.

8.6 What the Study Supports

The evidence supports four bounded statements. First, packet reachability must be measured before selector performance is interpreted. Second, prior-aware calibration is necessary for probability-based set decisions under imbalanced membership and is the only relationship confirmed on both benchmarks. Third, expected-F1 supplies a compact MOCHEG operating point but not a universally compact policy. Fourth, the present completeness-aware switch does not establish selector superiority or stable verdict preservation. The study does not support a state-of-the-art or deployment-ready claim; its external value is the prespecified nonreplication and the stronger boundary it places on adaptive selection claims.

9. LIMITATIONS AND ETHICS

9.1 Limitations

The main MOCHEG validity constraint is evaluation reuse. Its 1,490 validation claims are locked within each run, and every new choice is fitted on training roles, but the broader project had inspected this resource before the present protocol. AVerImaTeC supplies an independently frozen evaluation, but changes the evidence unit, label space, candidate store, and output cap; it confirms relationships rather than reproducing raw scores. Formal-test annotations for both benchmarks remain unopened.

The external evidence endpoint is also incomplete. Exact normalized source identity can penalize semantically valid alternative pages, while the official Gemma-3-27B evidence judge cannot run on the single local GPU under the organizer's prescribed condition. The development split is extremely imbalanced (141 of 152 claims are Refuted), with one Conflicting claim, and the static store was partly constructed with gold-question-derived queries. We therefore report the external result as a locked exact-source diagnostic, not an official shared-task score or unrestricted web-search estimate.

Released complete-gold sets can omit other valid web evidence. Exact-set metrics measure agreement with those identifiers and may penalize a defensible alternative source. The original complete-reachability subgroup contains only 79 validation claims and was identified post hoc. It motivates the frozen completeness switch but cannot establish a population subgroup guarantee. The repair raises validation complete-gold coverage to only 88 claims, so completeness modeling remains data-limited.

The membership scorer and completeness estimator are deliberately low capacity to preserve experimental control. Better content-aware multimodal scoring may change ranking, calibration, and the apparent value of the switch. Conversely, the stronger verifier is still a compact MLP over stored features rather than a modern generative fact-checker. Aggregate non-inferiority depends on that architecture and fails in three seed-specific intervals; the linear control reverses the mean direction.

Expected-F1 assumes conditionally independent candidate memberships and restricts decisions to prefixes of a fixed ranking. Sentences from one source, duplicate images, and complementary cross-modal evidence violate that assumption. A zero-valued redundancy penalty in this experiment does not show that dependencies are absent; it shows only that the tested penalty does not improve model-selection utility.

The robustness matrix is diagnostic rather than exhaustive. Temperature perturbations expose sensitivity to probability scale but do not estimate how often such drift occurs in practice. Runtime is recorded on one workstation without standardized warm-up, energy instrumentation, or competing-system measurements. We therefore make no throughput, energy, or deployment-efficiency claim beyond observed selected-set size.

9.2 Ethical Considerations

Automated fact-checking can create false confidence when relevant evidence is missing. A deployed interface should expose source identifiers, retrieval limits, calibration status, and the distinction between "no evidence retrieved" and "evidence supports no conclusion." The system should assist trained reviewers, not replace them.

Web evidence can contain personal, disturbing, copyrighted, geographically skewed, or politically sensitive material. Retrieval and display should follow source terms, minimize unnecessary reproduction, and provide safeguards for reviewers. MOCHEG is not representative of all languages, communities, claim types, or media ecosystems; results should not be generalized to low-resource or high-stakes domains without direct evaluation.

The study preserves unfavorable and incomplete runs, keeps both formal tests sealed, and reports uncertainty and failed gates. These controls reduce selective reporting and make the difference between a useful development finding and a deployment claim explicit.

10. CONCLUSION

We evaluated multimodal evidence selection as a decision constrained jointly by retrieval and calibration. On MOCHEG, bounded fusion raises packet-oracle F1 from 0.3560 to 0.3637, calibrated thresholding reaches 0.1864 evidence F1, and expected-F1 offers a smaller 3.61-item operating point at 0.1815. The completeness switch does not establish superiority. A separately frozen AVerImaTeC run confirms the calibration dependency but not the selector ordering: 59.87% of claims have no exact reachable target, expected-F1 is larger rather than smaller, thresholding ties fixed selection, and switch evidence fails verifier non-inferiority. Evidence-selection studies should therefore expose packet reachability, calibration, set size, uncertainty, and downstream class-wise behavior before claiming an adaptive improvement. The official semantic judge was unavailable locally and both formal tests remain sealed; the external contribution is a prespecified diagnostic nonreplication, not a shared-task score or deployment claim.
