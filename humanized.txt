ABSTRACT

However, it is impossible for an evidence selector to retrieve things that were not retrieved, and evidence selection based on probability is unreliable if the membership scores are not calibrated. We assess these coupled failures on MOCHEG and based on an organizer-store protocol that is separately frozen in AVerImaTeC. Label-blind rank fusion achieves F1 score of 0.3637 on MOCHEG compared to 0.3560 without it, and prior-aware calibration achieves mean Brier score of around 0.033 compared to 0.0847 without it. Calibrated thresholding is 0.1864 evidence F1, expected-F1 is 0.1815 with 3.61 instead of 5.26 items. The completeness-aware switch is at 0.1877, while the effect of its +0.00138 is not clear. Frozen packet has only 25.48% exact annotated sources and 59.87% of claims have no reachable target when it comes to locked AVerImaTeC development. Calibration transfers: the actual difference between a calibrated expected-F1 and an uncalibrated ablation is 0.0532 F1 and the multiplicity- adjusted interval is [0.0221,0.0885]. The other relationships fail: thresholding and fixed selection are tied, expected-F1 returns 3.20 items versus 1.17 for thresholding, the switch interval crosses zero after correction, and switch-selected evidence fails verifier non-inferiority. We do not have any local official semantic judge, and formal tests on both benchmarks have not been changed. The cross-benchmark evidence supports reachability-first, calibration-aware evaluation, but not adaptive-selector superiority or a universally compact expected-F1 policy.

1. INTRODUCTION

Only a fact-check which allows a reader to review the material behind it, is auditable. That material may consist of sentences, documents and images from various sources, for an image-text claim. While a retrieval system can provide hundreds of potential candidates, a reviewer still requires a concise set of candidates that includes sufficient supporting or refuting details to decide. MOCHEG captures this context with multimodal claims, text and image evidence, released evidence identifiers, and three-way verdicts.

Often the evidence selection reduces to a fixed top-k rule. Easy to do, but requires that equal number of items for each claim. However, adaptive policies map candidate-level scores (membership) to a claim specific set. A threshold returns all items greater than a cutoff, count model predicts the size of the set and expected-F1 decoding selects the probability- ranked prefix that has the highest expected set F1. The following rules are dependent upon each other, which can be hidden in rank-only evaluation: These scores must act as probabilities. A monotone transformation leaves the order of the candidates unchanged and alters the threshold and expected-utility decisions.

This dependency has a real impact in the presence of class imbalance. Gold evidence does not occur in a retrieved packet, and a balanced logistic training is intentionally altered class prior. A temperature map can correct an intercept error caused by that previous mismatch and thus update the confidence. Modern calibration work thus separates ranking, probability quality and the decision based on the probability quality. Apparently stronger adaptive decoder may be exploiting or amplifying a calibration error in evidence pipeline.

Retrieval forms an extra, more difficult boundary. Any item that does not make it into the packet cannot be returned by a selector. A decoding error and a retrieval miss will be given the same final evidence score if the packet reachability is not reported. The information boundaries followed here are shown in Figure 1. The same 40 text and 20 images candidates are given to all selection policies. The candidate rankings are recorded prior to the gold join, disjoint roles are used for scoring, calibration, policy selection and evaluation, and the released validation set is evaluated only after the train-role decisions have been made.

The study has the following four questions to answer:

1) What is the amount of released gold evidence at the actual depths of the packets and can a “bounded” label-blind rank fusion increase that upper bound? 2. What are the probabilities that correct the previous mistake caused by imbalanced evidence membership? 3. What are the differences between fixed top-k, calibrated thresholding, learned count, expected-F1 and an uncalibrated ablation on the same packets? 4. Is a fixed "packet completeness" switch superior to calibrated thresholding and maintains a high level of verdict quality in the presence of a more powerful shared verifier? 5. Which of these relationships reproduce under a separately frozen AVerImaTeC organizer-store protocol with different evidence units and four verdict classes?

The answers are deliberately mixed. Weighted text fusion boosts the all-eligible packet oracle from 0.3560 to 0.3637 F1 and complete-gold coverage from 4.39% to 4.74%, and does not affect image Recall@20. Maps generated from Prior-aware maps correct the dominant probability error. For the repaired packet, calibrated thresholding gets evidence F1=0.1864, calibrated expected-F1 gets 0.1815 with lower 3.61 item set and uncalibrated expected-F1 gets 0.1288 with 24.90 item set. The completeness-aware switch has the highest point estimate (0.1877 F1), but with an interval that includes zero, and is only slightly better than thresholding by 0.00138. It has a verifier outcome of aggregate non-inferiority and does not meet the per-seed safeguard. The independent assessment is more conservative: For it, the retained validation packet is only 25.48% of exact annotated sources and the target is not reachable for 59.87% of assertions. Calibration is key, and is able to increase the expected-F1 by 0.0532 on its uncalibrated ablation. Threshold superiority, expected-F1 compactness, switch superiority, and downstream verifier preservation do not reproduce.

This paper makes four contributions:

- a retrieval-conditioned evaluation that reports the operating-depth recall, the packet-oracle F1, the complete-gold coverage, and the zero-reachable claims before interpreting selector quality; - a matched calibration and set-decoding study covering six probability maps, fixed and adaptive controls, exact expected-F1, and an uncalibrated failure case; - a prespecified retrieval repair and packet-completeness-aware switch, evaluated against the strongest calibrated-threshold comparator with claim-aligned intervals and partition-level safeguards; and - a strictly split-isolated and auditable external evaluation on the organizer's static AVerImaTeC store, preserving label-blind rankings, per-claim records, failed exports and independent audits, confirming calibration but not selector superiority, compactness, or verifier preservation.

The contribution is empirical and not state of the art. The adaptive evidence sizing can only be employed after the evidence retrieval and calibration are revealed as experimental variables. In this study, the expected-F1 provides a precision-oriented operating point on MOCHEG and the completeness aware switch provides testable design direction, while calibrated thresholding is not superior externally, and expected-F1 is not compact externally. The paper keeps these negative boundaries due to the fact that they reflect the fact that the selector conclusion can turn negative when reachability, evidence units and class balance alter.

2. RELATED WORK

2.1 Evidence-Based Fact-Checking and Evaluation

Evidence-based verification can typically be broken down into four components: claim analysis, evidence retrieval, evidence assessment and verdict prediction. Results depend on the domain, the construction of the labels, the extent of modality coverage and on the controls for event or source overlap, which are reported in the surveys of datasets. Common problems include issues with cross-domain transfer, lack of explanations, and benchmark protocols. Based on this we have thus assessed the retrieval-and-selection chain instead of content-only classification.

Text evidence requirements are made transparent through text benchmarks. SciFact offers sentence evidence from scientific publications, HoVer is multi-document reasoning, COVID-Fact is following evolving claims in the public-health arena and X-Fact introduces multilingual variation. External-document systems reduce the need to use stylistic shortcuts and have retrieval, freshness, and attribution errors. So, we distinguish between evidence-set quality and verdict accuracy.

2.2 Multimodal Misinformation Detection

Content-based multimodal detectors are bilinear pooling, hierarchical attention, topic memory and cross-attention, ambiguity learning and multi-level fusion detectors. Contrastive, in-modality, stylometric or semantic-alignment signals are added in later systems. These designs can be used to classify at the post level; however, there is no traceability of the source of the verdicts, e.g. image or external document.

Current studies are on local/ global mismatch, cross-modal knowledge distillation and contextual inconsistency/crossing unimodal signals. Out of context benchmarks such as NewsCLIPpings, COSMOS and VERITE measure pairs of images and captions without matching context in their natural environment, but without bias from the unimodal modality. DGM4, SNIFFER, MMDFND, and IMFND are extensions of the localization, explanation, domain coverage or usage of vision-language models. They do not so much target a collection of external evidence identifiers which can be audited, as at manipulation or veracity.

2.3 Multimodal Evidence Retrieval and Verification

MOCHEG provides a shared assessment measure for multimodal retrieval, three-way verdict prediction and explanation generation. MEVER constructs a text-image evidence graph and fuses evidence at both the token and evidence levels, and RED-DOT finds and reranks external multimodal evidence prior to prediction. Recently, other studies differentiate between utility and semantic similarity and train when the use of visual retrieval is required. These systems see the problems of fixed retrieval, relevance filtering, modality routing, utility ranking and adaptive set decoding as different decision problems.

AVerImaTeC evaluates claims made on images and text in real-world scenarios, based on knowledge from the open-web or on knowledge provided by an organiser, and based on the shared task conditions on scoring the verdict for an evidence. In that context REVEAL is a combination of iterative sparse and dense retrieval. There is a distinct unit definition and candidate-construction protocol in MOCHEG's stable item corpus that are different from that of question-answer evidence and open-web access, hence a transfer of these requires a separate process.

2.4 Scientific and Citation-Grounded Evidence

Stable evidence locations in passages, tables and figures are provided by scientific verification. SciVer is composed of claim-evidence instances that are extracted from scientific papers and are annotated by experts; MuSciClaims is focused on figure-grounded claims and controlled reasoning perturbations; and M^2-TabFact is about tables presented across text and images. They can be utilized for more precise analysis of the situation of localization, but do not explicitly incorporate political or breaking-news verifications.

An explanation could be good, but use of the wrong source. Evidence-rationale benchmarks verify the connection between a prediction and a chosen span, while faithful-by-construction rationalizers restrict the predictors to explicit sub-sets of evidence. Citation-grounded generation (CGG) evaluates if generated statements are supported by retrieved sources, while RAGTruth evaluates the number of hallucinations on facts retrieved despite retrieval, and citation masking evaluates if the fact-checking explanation recovers the correct evidence. The present study does not use any generated rationales but rather released evidence identifiers and provides exact-set precision, recall, F1, Jaccard similarity and exact match.

2.5 Calibration and Adaptive Set Decisions

A score can correctly rank a candidate and at the same time have a low probability. Post-hoc calibration methods such as temperature and Platt mappings, beta calibration and nonparametric isotonic regression, make varying assumptions about the distortion being corrected. Prior correction is important as the optimized training distribution is different from the operating distribution after class weighting. The calibrated membership distribution can subsequently be used for expected-utility decoding, and structurally different policies (e.g., top-k, thresholding, learned count) can be used with the same scores.

In this study, the representation models and the evidence identities are fixed and only the retained ordering is different for a bounded, label-blind rank fusion. Then, the selector experiment tests packet reachability, packet reachability with prior-aware probability correction, exact expected F1 decoding and one prespecified completeness-aware switch, with matched candidates, tuning roles and downstream verification. This is a separately frozen organizer-store run test that tests the same relationships on AVerImaTeC without transferring fitted MOCHEG parameters. In this design, it determines which conclusions are specific to the evidence units and are not affected by changes in the number of construction candidates and class balance (in the summary table).

3.2 Dataset and Population Flow

MOCHEG is a dataset that includes image-text claims, separate image and text evidence as well as verdict labels. Content hash is used to pin out releases of the train, validation, corpus and sealed-test. The test annotations are not started up in the official test.

The train and validation claims (13,159) released have been encoded for retrieval. These include 10,970 that have a nonempty released complete-gold mapping—the eligible evidence-set population. The evaluation of the primary selector for repaired packets is done using the 1,490 validation claims released. Claim-grouped hashing is used to assign the left over claims to fitting, calibration, model-selection and internal-holdout occupations. For the deterministic partition seeds 13, 37, 73, 101 and 137, the roles are regenerated separately.

3.3 Independent AVerImaTeC Population

AVerImaTeC alters the evidence unit and label space, but not providing a MOCHEG replication unchanged. Candidates are claim-local organizers-store text chunks or images linked to a source. Maximum 10 evidence items are allowed in the official output along with one of four labels: Supported, Refuted, Not Enough Evidence, or Conflicting Evidence/Cherrypicking. For an auditable diagnostic, an exact normalized source identity is available, which is the primary semantic evidence score of the task, and needs to be judged separately from other evidence in the multimodal pool.

The external protocol takes all 793 of the training claims released for fitting and selection jobs and reserves all 152 development claims for one locked run. There are 756 Refuted, 18 Not Enough Evidence, 13 Supported, and 6 Conflicting claims in trainings and 141, 6, 4, and 1 in development. There are 1,586 training JSON files and 81,817 training images, 304 development JSON and 49,617 development images in the organizer archives. No transfer of MOCHEG model, threshold, calibrator or completeness estimator or verifier weight.

For an external claim, G_q are normalised annotated source identities, and C_q are a frozen organizer-store packet. When a semantically correct alternative valid webpage does not share the annotated URL with the original webpage, then it is reported as an exact-source diagnostic. For this reason, the exact-source F1 is not renamed the unavailable official semantic score.

3.4 Reachability

Two oracle summaries are used to define the retrieval boundary. For each claim that is eligible, the packet oracle returns C_q ∩ G_q. Only claims that have non-empty gold is taken into account for its F1. The fraction of eligible claims for which G_q ⊆ C_q is complete-gold coverage. We consider claims that are eligible such that C_q ∩ G_q= ∅.

These quantities provide answers to different questions. Oracle F1 is the best evidence set that can be achieved from the packet. Complete-gold coverage – whether it is possible to achieve full recovery. Zero-reachable count is the number of claims where each selector will get zero recall. All of these measures ignore any credit to be awarded to a selector in respect of evidence that is not revealed by the retrieval process.

3.5 Leakage Controls

The candidate ranks are written then hashed before joining with gold identifiers. Evidence labels are only used once the candidate construction is done, to fit the membership scorer, the calibration map and the selector hyperparameters in their respective training roles. There is no component fitted to the locked validation set. AVerImaTeC rejects any artifact that has a non-training row, and only accepts validation rows to the locked runner while rechecking all frozen input hashes after the train-only code and manifest are committed. No formal-test annotations are present on the execution path; seal records and access flags are verified before and after the outcome-bearing runs, respectively, of both benchmarks.

4. METHOD

4.1 Label-Blind Retrieval and Weighted Fusion

The text branch is an integration between BGE-M3 dense sentence retrieval and BM25, and stores a BM25 reranker ordering. The image branch holds SigLIP2 image-text similarity and dense similarity between the claim and normalized image file names. The model repositories and immutable revisions are set in the configuration: BGE-M3, BGE-reranker-v2-m3, SigLIP2.

Bounded Repair: Assigns candidate c the score

The existing dense-lexical fused rank is combined with the cross-encoder rank to calculate the text score, while SigLIP2 and filename ranks are used for the image score. Training-role packet-oracle F1 selects weights w from the set {0.25,0.50,0.75} independently (subject to operating-depth recall guardrails). In both branches choose w = 0.50. Prior to the gold join, rankings and hashes are written. The top 40 text and 20 images in the retained packet are included, and each selector gets the same retained packet.

4.2 Organizer-Store Retrieval for External Evaluation

The external path does not modify the MOCHEG packet, but utilizes static store from the AVerImaTeC. Text records are normalized and reduced to at most 1500 characters for a deterministic claim conditioned record. BM25 and BGE-M3 ranks are fused using the equal-weight reciprocal rank method with constant 60 and the top 100 fused candidates are reranked by pinned BGE cross-encoder. The actual candidates' images are loaded from the claim-matched store, and ranked by SigLIP2, a BGE representation of a normalized filename also provides an image rank with equal-weight RRF. The text on a reverse-image-search webpage is still considered to be text evidence, not mis-labeled as an image.

Each candidate identifier is a hash over the claim identity, the normalized source, the modality, the content hash and the local ordinal. The label-blind preparation stores up to 100 text and 20 image candidates, writes that ranking and hashes it and only then merges exact-source targets. Training roles select a text width from {50,100} and image width from {10,20} while all five seeds choose 50 text and 10 image items. The queries used to build the reachability relation in the organizer store were partially based on gold questions, as such the reachability relation is used as a benchmark condition and not as a condition that represents a free web search.

The difference is that any monotone map will preserve the rank, but can vastly alter the set that is chosen.

Fixed top-k.

We tune k in {1,2,3,5,10,20,40,60} and select the top k candidates. The control that is created is top-5.

Calibrated threshold.

The selector can be used to return all items with p_qi >= tau (in the standard packet size). The threshold is selected from {0.1, ..., 0.9}.

Learned count.

A count model is used to predict the number of evidence items and the prefix with the corresponding probability is returned.

4.6 External Refitting and Decision Caps

All the fitted components are re-estimated from AVerImaTeC training roles. For every seed 55% of claims are fed to a balanced L2 logistic membership scorer, 15% to candidate calibators, 15% are used to choose the calibator, packet width, fixed budget, threshold and switch cutoff, and 15% to an internal holdout. A maximum of 40 deterministic negatives will be fed into scorer fitting per claim. The freeze does not load a development row, and there is model-selection Brier score competition among identity, temperature, prior-shift, Platt, beta and isotonic maps.

The official ten-item cap provides a change in the external policy grids. The fixed selection searches for k in 1,3,5,10,  while thresholding searches for the prefix between 0.1 and 0.9,  and the expected-F1 searches for prefixes between zero and ten. The completeness model is applied to calibration-role claims, the cutoff is set on the model-selection role, and it is routed to expected-F1 if completeness is predicted, and to thresholding if not. This is NOT re-using MOCHEG weights or decisions, it is re-using algorithm families.

4.7 Decision Scope and Assumptions

The ONLY thing that changes is the set decision. Candidates, their rank, feature values, scorer outputs and validation claims are shared across policies. Expected-F1 assumes conditionally independent Bernoullis, and only acts on prefixes of the frozen ranking, it does not reorder the candidates, or retrieve a missing item. The complete-gold mapping released is considered the evaluation target, where Section 9 comments on possible incompleteness of the mapping. These boundaries help to differentiate between a decoder error and retrieval error and help to prevent an adaptive policy from being given a more favorable candidate universe.

4.8 Shared Verifier

Evidence policies can have a positive impact on set overlap and a negative impact on downstream verdict. We thus fix verifier architecture, training data, features, and optimization and only vary the evidence selected. The primary verifier is a 2-layer multilayer perceptron (MLP) with 64 ReLU hidden units, inverse-frequency class weights, 300 full-batch Adam epochs, a learning rate of 0.01 and L2 coefficient of 0.001. It has been trained on unique fixed, threshold and expected-F1 evidence sets, fitting claims. Logistic linear verifier is kept as capacity control. Both models are calibrated with the same 1,490 validation claims and five deterministic seeds and report a comparison between calibrated thresholding and the frozen completeness-aware switch. The non-inferiority margin used is -0.01 macro-F1.

The same safeguard is refitted for four classes in the case of AVerImaTeC. The main model consists of a single 64-unit ReLU layer and it is trained with 400 deterministic full-batch steps, with learning rate 0.01, inverse-frequency weights and L2=0.001; the capacity control is a balanced multinomial linear model. Selected evidence includes the mean, maximum and standard deviation of candidate features, set size, modality fractions and distinct-domain count. Frozen model is shared between policies. When the prespecified blind rule picked the fixed family, the verdicts of this blind rule were computed by deterministic inference from this already fixed verifier, without any refitting or development selection.

5. EXPERIMENTAL DESIGN

5.1 Frozen Protocols and Claim Boundary

The diagnostic configuration was frozen on 4 November 2026 prior to its outcome bearing run. It stores data and model revisions, five claim-group partition seeds, retrieval depths, scorer families, calibration candidates, selector grids, evaluation metrics, bootstrap settings and stopping rules. The bounded retrieval repair, completeness-aware switch and stronger verifier were frozen on 9 November 2026 prior to checking their respective results. Prior to fitting the affected models, the numeric threshold grid and verifier architecture was captured. Any changes to the experiment or runs that fail are stored in an append-only experiment ledger.

External implementation was committed prior to development preparation and 5-seed train-only manifest was committed on 11th November 2026. That manifest includes hashes for code, configuration, archive, pre-join ranking, candidate, gold-map, and audit, and fails if input is a mix of train-development. The development matrix was then carried out 152 times on 12 November 2026.

The MOCHEG validation split released earlier in the project has been used and is not new validation evidence. The execution path has no formal-test annotations and they are sealed. There is no automatic approval of a formal-test-run based on the validation outcome.

5.2 Independent External Protocol

For seeds 13, 37, 73, 101 and 137, the claim sizes for AVerImaTeC training are deterministically partitioned as 55% fit, 15% calibration, 15% model selection and 15% internal holdout. The locked development run is run on the same candidates and membership scores for the train-selected fixed top-k, calibrated thresholding, calibrated expected-F1, uncalibrated expected-F1 and the completeness-aware switch. All development results do not affect the retrieval, fitting, calibration, packet width and policy parameters.

The four comparisons of claim are a multiplicity family: threshold minus fixed, expected-F1 minus threshold, calibrated minus uncalibrated expected-F1 and switch minus threshold. They both utilize 10,000 bootstrap draws, Bonferroni simultaneous intervals and Holm adjusted two-sided values. For an effect to be considered superior, the Threshold value must be ≥ +0.010 and have a positive Adjusted lower bound. Expected-F1 must return at least 15% fewer items and be at least -0.010 non-inferior. Switch superiority also demands that each of the seed effects is positive and that there is no increase in the mean set size. The margin for verifier preservation is also -0.010.

Gemma-3-27B (bfloat16) is used by the organizer's semantic evaluator. The 54.86 GB of weight files will not fit in the single GPU RTX 4090 with 24.56 GiB, and the organizer launcher requires two GPUs. In view of this, we present actual normalized-source diagnostics and class-wise verifier results, rather than a 'replacement score' as official. The execution of the blind test is not in the authorized execution boundary.

5.3 Baselines, Outcomes, and Selection Budgets

Matched policies include fixed top-5, calibrated thresholding, learned count, calibrated expected-F1, uncalibrated expected-F1 and the packet-completeness-aware switch. The main result is that of claim-macro complete-gold evidence F1. Secondary outcomes are precision, recall, Jaccard similarity, exact-set match, selected count, calibration metrics, reachability and verdict macro-F1.

Only training roles will be used to select scorer and calibration. The repair will look at three weights per retrieval branch that have been declared. Thresholding searches 0.1, 0.9 and the completeness cutoff searches five values declared. Weighted-fusion advancement gate passes so no other retrieval branch is opened. Once locked validation is seen, these budgets will not support any expansion based on outcomes.

The claim-level differences are first averaged over the five deterministic partitions in each of the comparisons. Then, a claim-aligned bootstrap resamples the 1,490 claims 10,000 times. Selector-superiority gate: All the following against calibrated thresholding: Evidence F1 ≥ +0.010, Paired 95% interval > 0, No increase in mean selected-set size, and No negative in any 5 partitions. The verifier's non-inferiority margin is -0.010. Both the lower bound for the whole interval aligned with the seed, and the lower bound for each of the seeds clearing the margin are reported.

5.4 Robustness and Diagnostic Analyses

The original diagnostic packet is also checked for widths 10 and 20, for text-only evidence and image-only evidence, for removing 25% and 50% of reachable gold deterministically, for promoting 5 distractors, for duplication of the top 3 distractors, and for 0.5 and 2.0 probability temperatures. Under these perturbations, fitted selectors remain the same. The conditions are tests of reachability, modality, contamination of ranking, duplication, and probability scale; they are not estimates of production shift.

After the primary comparison, there are validation claims for no, partial, and complete released-gold reachability. This is a post hoc and descriptive stratification. It encourages the pre-specified completeness-aware switch but doesn't make the previous subgroup finding a confirmatory statement.

5.5 Implementation, Hardware, and Audit

Each run is stored with the following pieces of information: candidate-level scores, calibrated probabilities, selected identifiers, claim level metrics, fitted parameters, environment metadata, formal-test access flags. Independent audits recompute population counts, set metrics, paired effects, and gate conditions from saved records. The label-blind ranking is performed prior to the gold joining. If the run fails, the partial output of the four runs is saved with the repair reason, instead of being overwritten.

On this machine, the weighted retrieval run time is 9.5 seconds, the candidate construction time is 8.4 seconds, the base selector evaluation time is 158.8 seconds, and the completeness switching time is 334.8 seconds, and the stronger verifier time is 246.9 seconds. These are not cross-system efficiency results, but wall-clock records for reproducing. The faithful organizer-store preparation takes 50.5 minutes for 793 training claims and 21.2 minutes for 152 development claims, while the locked 5-seed comparison takes 19 seconds after preparation. These times involve text encoding, reranking by a cross-encoder, and even scoring the actual images using SigLIP2. One development image of 144 million pixels (MP) results in a PIL safety warning, but is processed without exclusion, OOM or fallback.

6. RESULTS

6.1 A Modest Retrieval Repair Raises the Ceiling

The pre-repair packet Recall@40 is 0.3193, packet-oracle evidence F1 is 0.3560 and image Recall@20 is 0.2663, with complete-gold coverage of 4.39% over 10,970 eligible claims. There are 3,086 claims which have no reachable released-gold item. For training-role selection, the two image ranks are given equal weight, as are the two text ranks (the existing fused and the cross-encoder).

The selected fusion improves text Recall@40, packet-oracle F1 and complete-gold coverage on all eligible claims to 0.3292, 0.3637 and 4.74%, respectively. This brings the number of zero-reachable claims down 59 to 3,027. Image Recall@20 remains 0.2663. This means that the declared progress gate passes, but the progress engineering goals (+0.05 oracle-F1 gain and 8% complete-gold coverage) do not.

There is a similar trend with the locked-validation comparison in the table. Oracle F1 sees a gain of 0.00612 points while complete-gold coverage improves by 0.604 percentage points—image recall remains exactly the same. This is a handy packet repair, but not a new retrieval state of the art.

The retrieval audit is explained in detail in the deeper. Dense-BM25 rank fusion has text Recall@50 of 0.3224. With the use of cross-encoder reranking, it rises to 0.3338 and the while reducing early Recall@5 from 0.1772 to 0.1335. On images, the SigLIP2-filename fusion achieves Recall@50 of 0.3149, whereas SigLIP2 and filename retrieval achieve 0.2183 and 0.2227, respectively. The repair thus skips adding an image branch after the progression gate after the advanced ordering, and merges the text ranks.

The previous reachability stratification is still useful for diagnosis. For the 79 validation claims with the full released-gold set in the original packet, 0.5240 F1 are achieved by expected-F1, while for fixed top-5 and thresholding, 0.3593 F1 and 0.4237 F1 are achieved, respectively. On 1,200 partially reachable claims, expected-F1 score 0.1839 is not better than fixed top-5 score 0.1841, and the best score is achieved by thresholding with a score of 0.1965. On the 211 zero-reachable claims, all policies have a score of zero. This is a post hoc finding which is used to support a completeness-aware decision, but not a prespecified subgroup claim.

6.2 Prior-Aware Maps Repair the Main Calibration Error

Probability maps on locked validation are compared in the table in the average over modalities and deterministic partitions. The Brier scores of identity and temperature scaling are 0.0847 and 0.0832, respectively. The error is reduced by a considerable amount by prior shift, Platt, beta and isotonic mappings, isotonic reaching approximately 0.0330 and reducing ECE from 0.1314 to 0.0063 relative to identity. For each selected partition, Brier skill is positive compared to a predictor that is based on prevalence only. The changed training prior causes the dominant intercept error which cannot be corrected with a slope-only temperature.

In the event of a calibration, scorer selection also will change. Partitions 13 and 37 select modality-specific balanced models, partition 73 selects the unweighted cross-encoder scorer and partitions 101 and 137 select unweighted modality-specific models. For all maps, text calibration is different for isotonic, Platt, and beta maps; and isotonic regression is selected in each image branch. There are thus one selection problem: scorer family, loss weighting or calibration are not a single selection problem; the deployed evidence set is not determined by ranking performance alone.

6.3 Thresholding Is Confirmed; Completeness Switching Is Inconclusive

The table shows a comparison matched on the repaired packet. Fixed top-5 [0.1712 evidence F1]. Calibrated thresholding reaches 0.1864 with 5.26 items; its gain over fixed top-5 is +0.01517 with a claim-aligned 95% interval of [0.01114,0.01923]. Expected-F1 reaches 0.1815 with 3.61 items. It improves over fixed top-5 by +0.01034 [0.00501,0.01574], but remains 0.00484 below thresholding [-0.00938,-0.00020] for the expected-F1-minus-threshold comparison.

In the uncalibrated ablation, the significance of probability quality is revealed. It achieves 24.90 items/claim and drops to 0.1288 F1 operating on the same order of candidate. The same applies to learned count which selects 31.49 items, and achieves 0.1122 F1. The gain of using expected-F1 is thus not only because of the variable set size, but because of probabilities that are determined by the rare operating membership rate.

The completeness aware switch is expected F1 for 37.8-46.4% of the validation claims across the partitions, thresholding otherwise. Each partition chooses the pre-specified cut-off 0.50. This is the highest mean point estimate tested, 0.1877 F1 with 4.70 items. However, it has a gain over thresholding of only +0.00138 with a 95% interval of [-0.00191,0.00462] and one of five partition effects is negative. It fails the +0.010 practical-effect requirement, the all-positive requirement, and the positive-lower-bound requirement. The switch reduces review-set size without a confirmed loss, but it does not establish selector superiority.

The size and same-source redundancy penalties are unchanged from the earlier version, and are still exact matches to plain expected-F1, since all five training-role partitions select zero for both penalties. These decoder terms are added to increase complexity but don't change the selected set on the diagnostic packet.

6.4 Aggregate Verifier Non-Inferiority Is Not Seed-Stable

The conclusion downstream is moved from a simple failure to a qualified safeguard due to the stronger shared verifier. The MLP achieves a mean macro-F1 of 0.3911 when using calibrated-threshold evidence; and 0.3954 when using switch-selected evidence. The seed-aligned paired effect is +0.00434 with a 95% interval of [-0.00277,0.01130]. Its lower bound exceeds the frozen -0.010 margin, so aggregate non-inferiority passes, but the interval crosses zero and does not show superiority.

Only the two seeds 37 and 73 are able to individually clear the non-inferiority margin. Seeds 13, 101, and 137 have lower bounds of -0.01367, -0.02174, and -0.01406, respectively. The directionality of the linear control is also inverted: macro-F1 falls from 0.3787 under thresholding to 0.3746 under the switch. Verdict preservation thus is supported only in the more powerful model, and not as an architecture- and seed-stable benefit.

6.5 Probability Perturbations Reverse the Adaptive Gain

The expected-F1 is positive for original diagnostic packet relative to fixed top-5 under widths 20 and 40, text-only evidence and deterministic gold removal, but is slightly below on image-only packets. The promotion of five distractors (where fixed top-5 had to make those selections) gives a high relative gain, but is not representative of normal operation.

The delicate dimension is the probability scale. These numbers are -0.0193 for sharpening with temperature 0.5 and -0.0221 for flattening with temperature 2.0. Both intervals do not include 0 although the candidate rankings are the same. This means that it cannot be used to make a general robustness statement and it further provides motivation for reporting calibration along with adaptive set decisions.

Together, the results separate four conclusions that a single end-to-end score would obscure. Retrieval repair lifts, but doesn't lift the ceiling; prior-aware calibration is required; expected-F1 is compact precision-oriented set; completeness-aware switch looks like a good idea, but isn't compared to calibrated thresholding. The formal test is sealed and the four conclusions are only based on controlled MOCHEG development evidence.

7. INDEPENDENT EXTERNAL EVALUATION

Unlike MOCHEG, the AVerImaTeC candidate store, evidence unit, output cap, and verdict task with four classes are different. Everything in this section is the outcome of 1 single locked development run and was based on the 5 train-only seed. The evidence values are exact normalized sources, and are marked as diagnostic throughout; the official semantic judge is not executed.

7.1 The Organizer Store Does Not Remove the Retrieval Ceiling

The figures for the training preparation are: 529,580 text candidates / 81,817 image candidates, for 793 claims; the development figures are: 101,289 text candidates / 49,617 image candidates, for 152 claims. There are store candidates for every claim, but the annotated targets are typically not contained in the retained packet. The table indicates the difference between the number of candidates available and the number of candidates that are 'reachable' for the target.

Development Recall@10, @20, and @50 is 0.0526, 0.0696, and 0.1143. The 100-text/20-image retained pool achieves 25.48% of annotated targets, 12.50% of claims and has no exact reachable target for 91/152 claims. All downstream policies are constrained by these values and that's why abs_exact_source F1 is low.

7.2 Only the Calibration Relationship Reproduces

The table first takes an average of each claim for the five different frozen seeds and then averages across claims. Calibrated expected-F1 has the highest point estimate 0.1029 with 3.20 items returned. The thresholding method yields only 1.17 items and it is considered to be practically equivalent to the family chosen by the train. The uncalibrated ablation selects 9.93 out of ten allowed items and ends up with 0.0497 F1, which recovers the probability quality dependence of expected-utility decoding.

The four comparisons in the table are with 10,000 aligned bootstrap draws. They are Bonferroni simultaneous 98.75% intervals, the p-values are Holm adjusted. Threshold superiority fails. However, instead of no less than 15% fewer items, Expected-F1 returns 173.96% more items as it's adjusted lower bound is not below -0.010 in exact-source F1. One hypothesis for the selectors, calibration, passes. The switch effect is positive for all the seeds, but the adjusted interval also crosses the zero and it is bigger than the interval determined by thresholding.

7.3 The Downstream Safeguard Fails Externally

The mean verifier macro-F1 is 0.1573 (threshold evidence) and 0.1444 (switch evidence). The seed-aligned switch-minus-threshold effect is -0.01293 with 95% interval [-0.03242,0.00687] which does not exceed the reported -0.010. The switch therefore fails verifier non-inferiority, unlike the aggregate MOCHEG result.

There are 141 Refuted, six Not Enough Evidence, four Supported and one Conflicting claim in the class distribution. Both of the policies fail to solve the problem of one conflicting class across seeds, and accuracy is largely dependent on the majority class. That is why macro/class level F1 along with accuracy constitutes the safeguard.

7.4 Frozen Decision

The blind selection rule only selects the switch if the full gate passes, otherwise selects expected-F1 if it is at least 15% smaller and non-inferior, otherwise the thresholding selection if it beats the fixed selection, otherwise the train-selected fixed family. If all three of the above is false, then the selection is set to fixed and the frozen decision takes precedence. After running the already frozen verifier on the list, local development exports for all five seeds meet the official 10 item and 1,500 character requirements. There is no test prediction, or leaderboard submission. It is therefore an independent diagnostic nonreplication, rather than an official shared-task score, from the outside.

8. DISCUSSION

8.1 Calibration Changes the Decision, Not the Ranking

Expected-F1 needs an operationally meaningful probability: if we set the probability of an item to be an evidence member to be 0.30, then about 30% of such items should be evidence members under similar circumstances. Effective training prior is modified by balanced logistic training which enhances the rare-positive ranking. The logit mapping, prior shift, Platt, beta and isotonic mappings can all correct the shape and/or location of the reliability curve, while temperature scaling changes the logit slope. This distinction becomes reality with the uncalibrated expected-F1 ablation which selects almost 25 items per claim and forfeits over 5 evidence-F1 points compared to calibrated expected-F1 and with no change to the candidate universe.

This is the only relationship that can be reproduced without troubles on AVerImaTeC. There, uncalibrated expected-F1 almost reaches the maximum of ten and is 0.0532 lower than the corresponding calibrated expected-F1. The positive simultaneous interval remains significant after multiplicity correction, and despite different candidate units, class balance and independently fitted models. So calibration is not necessarily an explanation that is only suitable for the MOCHEG packet.

This is the reason for not having to include fitted size and Redundancy penalties in the diagnostic experiment. Even if you restore the low membership prevalence, the unpenalized expected utility is already towards the short prefix. To avoid this the probability quality, prevalence, and selected set size should be reported in adaptive selector studies along with the rank metrics.

8.2 Why Thresholding Remains the Strong Baseline

Expected-F1 is a combination of all the membership probabilities using the conditional-independence approximation. A tuned threshold is the same calibrated probabilities, but with a lessened number of structural assumptions. On the repaired packet, thresholding is 0.00484 F1 stronger and recovers more evidence, while expected-F1 returns 1.64 fewer items and has higher precision. The latter is a good review cost operating point, but not the best selector on the primary metric.

The outside outcome restricts this conclusion. The AVerImaTeC thresholding is the smallest set, and it is tied with the train-selected fixed family, while the expected-F1 is able to reach its higher point estimate by selecting 3.20 rather than 1.17 items. Expected-F1 is not reducing the size enough, but is not inferior to the non-inferiority margin. However thresholding is not an always better policy, and nor does compactness transfer.

The completeness-aware switch is a test to determine if these policies can be merged without merely opting for the better policy on validation. It has a higher point estimate compared to thresholding and a lower mean set compared to thresholding, but this does not provide sufficient support for superiority as the difference is too small and unstable. This negative result is helpful, because the completeness of packets is a reasonable routing variable, but the current ten- feature logistic estimate is not yet sufficiently precise to be used to create a sound method-level gain. The model for the future should start with enhancing the completeness prediction with the same protocol (the same decoder, not another penalty), and not by introducing another penalty.

8.3 Retrieval Is Still the Dominant Constraint

Weighted fusion brings down the zero-reachable claims and up the packet oracle, but the all-eligible ceiling is only 0.3637 F1 and complete-gold coverage is less than 5%. The absolute increase in gain is much less than that for the engineering goal. Most of these selector errors can thus not be fixed with another prefix rule.

Exact-source identity is the tougher evaluative criteria: Only 12.50% of development claims have the complete annotated set in the retained pool, while 59.87% of development claims have no reachable annotated target. The problem with the static store is not that there is no data, but that very few candidates are ranked high and it's difficult to identify the source. This cross-benchmark reachability boundary agreement supersedes any selector ordering agreement.

The error behavior of text and image branches is different. To alleviate the tradeoff, cross-encoder text ranking is helpful deeper into the list, but not at the top. Since image fusion is already a fusion of two complimentary signals, changing their respective weights does not have a positive effect on Recall@20. But each would alter the candidate construction, and OCR, article context or table-aware external benchmark might be helpful, but requires a separately frozen study. They are not “extensions of the present result” that are concealed.

8.4 Evidence and Verdict Safeguards Answer Different Questions

Evidence F1 is the overlap between released evidence identifiers and verdict macro-F1 is about whether there is a support of the final three-way decision using selected evidence. The more powerful MLP verifier shows a positive aggregate non-inferiority for the switchover, while the interval straddles zero, three of the seed specific safeguards are not met and the linear control also moves in the opposite direction. The outcome is on average verdict quality preserved, but not end to end benefit.

The AVerImaTeC safeguard isn't as good. Switch evidence reduces mean macro-F1 from 0.1573 to 0.1444, and the lower bound that is aligned with the seed is below the -0.010 threshold. The single Conflicting claim is zero F1 in both policies for seeds and Refuted dominates the 152 claim split. This demonstrates the need for class-wise downstream reporting in order to promote an evidence-set improvement.

This is important when it comes to system reporting. There should be separate numbers for evidence precision, evidence recall, set size, retrieval reachability, calibration and verdict performance. Now it's possible that the single score could tell if a compact set saved reviewer effort, it could not have provided adequate support, or it could just have worked out well with one verifier seed.

8.5 What Transfers Externally

The external study does not replicate an adaptive selector that is superior. It nevertheless establishes two scientific relationships: selection is limited by candidate reachability; and expected-utility decoding by calibrated operating probabilities. It goes against three more specific MOCHEG observations: thresholding is not the "smaller operating point," expected-F1 is not a smaller operating point, and completeness switch does not maintain verifier quality. Such contradictions are not to be thought of as reasons to retune what is going on out there, but as the empirical range of the method claims.

Controlled diagnostic rather than the official shared-task result may be produced, because semantically valid alternatives may be wrongly identified as such due to exact identity of URL. The judge Gemma-3-27B is unavailable and the blind test is untouched, so they can not be compared in semantic-score. Despite this restriction, the direction of the calibration ablation and the gates that were not specified correctly are valid in the target area that has been released as an exact-source.

8.6 What the Study Supports

There are four bounded statements for which there is supporting evidence. In order to interpret selector performance, first packet reachability must be measured. Second, prior-aware calibration is required for probability based set decisions with imbalanced membership and is the only relation that holds on both of the benchmarks. Third, expected-F1 provides a "small" MOCHEG operating point, but does not provide a universally "small" policy. Fourth, the current completeness-aware switch is not able to create selector superiority or stable verdict preservation. The study is not a state of the art or deployment-ready claim, but the external value of the study is a prespecified nonreplication, and a more stringent boundary on adaptive selection claims.

9. LIMITATIONS AND ETHICS

9.1 Limitations

The evaluation reuse is the main MOCHEG validity constraint. Before the present protocol, the larger project had examined this resource and its 1490 validation claims are captured in each run as are training roles based on each additional choice. AVerImaTeC provides an independent freeze evaluation, changes the evidence unit, label space, candidate store and output cap but does not replicate actual scores. Both benchmarks have formal test annotations which have not been opened up.

The external evidence endpoint is not available either. Strict normalized source identity can be a disadvantage for semantically correct alternate pages; the official Gemma-3-27B evidence judge cannot be executed in the organizer's specified condition, which is on a single local GPU. The development split is very much weighted towards Refuted (141 of 152 claims), with one Conflicting claim; and part of the static store was developed using queries from gold-question. We thus include the external result as a locked exact-source diagnostic, rather than as an official shared-task score or as an estimate of unrestricted web-search.

Other web evidence that is valid may not be included in released complete-gold sets. Exact-set metrics assess conformity to those identifiers, and can be harsh on a defensible alternative source. A post hoc validation claim was identified and there are only 79 claims in the original complete-reachability subgroup. It only encourages the frozen whole completion switch but cannot set up a population sub-group guarantee. The repair increases coverage of validations complete-gold to just 88 claims (data-limited completeness modelling).

The size of the membership scorer and the completeness estimator are intentionally kept small for the purpose of maintaining the experimental control. Improved content-aware multimodal scoring can impact ranking, calibration and the perceived value of the switch. In contrast, the powerful verifier remains a small MLP using features from a knowledge store instead of a state-of-the-art generative fact-checker. The aggregate non-inferiority relies on that architecture and breaks down over three seed-intervals, which is where the linear control has a mean direction switch.

Expected-F1 makes the assumption of being conditionally independent in the memberships of candidates and only considers taking decisions on prefixes of a fixed ranking. Sentences from a single source, duplicate pictures and complementary cross-modal evidence violate this assumption. This experiment finds no sign of a dependency in the case of a zero valued redundancy penalty, but only that that particular penalty does not provide a utility to model selection.

The robustness matrix is diagnostic and does not necessarily list all the relevant values. Probability scale sensitivity is revealed by temperature perturbations but it cannot be estimated how often such drift occurs in practice. The runtime is measured on one workstation, there is no standard warmup, energy instrumentation or competing-system measurements. We thus do not make a claim for throughput, energy or deployment-efficiency other than observed selected-set size.

9.2 Ethical Considerations

When relevant evidence is not present, automated fact-checking may engender false sense of trust. The distinction between "no evidence retrieved" and "evidence supports no conclusion" should be made and source identifiers and retrieval limits should be provided for a deployed interface, as should be the status of the calibration. The system is intended to support not replace trained reviewers.

Web evidence may include personal, disturbing, copyrighted, geographically biased and politically sensitive content. Retrieval and display should be after source terms, minimize unnecessary reproduction and include safeguards for reviewers. MOCHEG does not support the assessment of all languages, communities, media ecosystems or claim types; results should not be assumed to apply to low resource or high stakes domains without direct assessment.

The study is not made up of bad or incomplete runs, both formal tests are kept sealed and uncertainty and failed gates are reported. These controls minimise selective reporting and clearly distinguish between a development finding and a deployment claim.

10. CONCLUSION

We evaluated multimodal evidence selection as a decision constrained jointly by retrieval and calibration. On MOCHEG, bounded fusion raises packet-oracle F1 from 0.3560 to 0.3637, calibrated thresholding reaches 0.1864 evidence F1, and expected-F1 offers a smaller 3.61-item operating point at 0.1815. The completeness switch does not establish superiority. A separately frozen AVerImaTeC run confirms the calibration dependency but not the selector ordering: 59.87% of claims have no exact reachable target, expected-F1 is larger rather than smaller, thresholding ties fixed selection, and switch evidence fails verifier non-inferiority. Evidence-selection studies should therefore expose packet reachability, calibration, set size, uncertainty, and downstream class-wise behavior before claiming an adaptive improvement. The official semantic judge was unavailable locally and both formal tests remain sealed; the external contribution is a prespecified diagnostic nonreplication, not a shared-task score or deployment claim.
