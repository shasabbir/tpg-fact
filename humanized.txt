ABSTRACT

Fact-checkers require evidence sets that are small and succinct; a selector cannot recover evidence that retrieval misses or make reliable set decisions if the scores are miscalibrated. This retrieval-conditioned study of MOCHEG distinguishes these sources of failure. All methods are fed with the same set of 40 text and 20 image candidates. A total of 1,490 locked validation claims are tested on six different probability maps and four selection policies across five deterministic training-role partitions. Among 10,970 claims with released complete-gold evidence, the packet oracle achieves an evidence F1 of 0.3560, complete gold is present for 4.39% of claims, and 3,086 claims do not have any reachable gold item. By taking prior information into account, prior-aware calibration lowers the mean Brier score from 0.0847 to 0.0330. Expected-F1 then returns 3.50 items per claim and reaches 0.1759 evidence F1, improving on fixed top-5 by 0.0085 with a claim-aligned 95% interval of [0.0030, 0.0140]. The calibrated threshold remains stronger at 0.1807. A post hoc reachability analysis localizes the expected-F1 benefit: it reaches 0.5240 F1 when complete gold is available in the packet, but offers no gain over fixed top-5 when gold is only partially reachable. Size and redundancy penalties select zero throughout, probability perturbations reverse the adaptive gain, and a shared verifier does not establish the declared non-inferiority margin. Calibrated expected-F1 provides a precision-oriented operating point. It does not establish end-to-end superiority; retrieval reachability is still the chief limitation, and calibrated thresholding performs better overall.

1. INTRODUCTION

Only an evidence-based verdict is auditable, and then only if the reader can view the material underlying the verdict. In the multimodal context that might be sentences, documents and images. While modern systems can draw a huge list of candidates, the fact-checker still requires a concise list of candidates that is relevant enough to examine and substantive enough for a decision. MOCHEG explicitly states the problem with image-text claims, text evidence, image evidence, and three-way verdicts [CITE: yao2023mocheg].

The final evidence is most commonly selected by a fixed top-k rule that most pipelines use. This is very easy to replicate, but assumes that each claim requires the same number of items. Adaptive selectors, however, select a claim-specific set on the basis of membership scores. A threshold returns all candidate items with a score above a cutoff. A count model predicts the set size. Expected-F1 decoding finds the candidate prefix with the highest expected set F1. All these rules have a common dependency: their scores must behave like probabilities. Ranking quality alone is not enough. A monotone score transformation does not affect the ranking, but does change the threshold and expected-F1 decisions.

Such a dependency is particularly dangerous when classes are imbalanced. Membership of evidence is uncommon in a retrieved packet and balanced logistic training makes a deliberate change to the effective class prior. Temperature scaling can change confidence but cannot correct an intercept error caused by the prior. When this mismatch is not taken care of, an expected-utility decoder could over-pick candidates despite the increase of average precision.

Retrieval creates a second dependency. A selector can't get an item that's not in its packet. If the packet oracle is not included, a decoding error and a retrieval miss can look identical in the reported selector F1. Thus, we consider selection within one pipeline (and one only), as illustrated in Fig. [REF: fig:architecture]. All methods are given the same 40 text and 20 image candidates, and the same complete-gold mapping, which was released.

Study design and information boundaries (figure caption). Retrieval fixes one packet per claim before gold labels are attached. Three disjoint training roles are used for scoring, calibration and policy selection. Each evidence policy is then assessed with the same locked validation claims, followed by a shared-verifier safeguard.

The study has four questions:

1. What is the amount of released gold evidence that can be retrieved at the real packet depths?
2. What are the probability maps that correct the calibration error due to imbalance?
3. Is there an advantage to calibrated expected-F1 selection over a fixed top-k selection and a calibrated threshold?
4. Are evidence-set gains maintained across probability, modality, packet-width and retrieval perturbations, and do they maintain verdict quality under a shared verifier?

Isotonic, Platt and prior-shift maps all produce a dramatic decrease in the Brier error. With prior-aware calibration, expected-F1 returns 3.50 items on average with an improvement of 0.0085 evidence F1 compared to fixed top-5. The fitted size and redundancy penalties are zero for all the deterministic partitions, and thus do not affect the output. The calibrated threshold does better, improving on fixed top-5 by 0.0133. The two adaptive rules are still limited to a packet oracle of 0.3560 F1.

This paper makes four contributions:

- retrieval-conditioned evaluation which provides information on operating-depth recall, packet-oracle F1, complete-gold coverage and unreachable claims;
- a controlled comparison of six calibration maps for balanced (and unweighted) multimodal membership scorers;
- a claim-aligned comparison of fixed, threshold, count, and expected-F1 selection with a reachability-stratified error analysis, a shared-verifier safeguard, and controlled perturbations.
- an auditable implementation with frozen data revisions, saved candidate probabilities, independent recomputation of metrics and a clean deterministic rerun.

It is possible to create a better Expected-F1 selection than having a fixed evidence budget, provided that the inputs to the selection are set appropriately to the deployment prior. Calibrated thresholding works better in these experiments and the expected-F1 gain is eliminated when there is little probability distortion. Experimental variables of calibration and reachability should accordingly be reported as part of evaluations of adaptive evidence selection.

2. RELATED WORK

Remark: Comparison with evidence-aware multimodal verification and retrieval studies. An output of a set of different sizes of evidence is called adaptive, reranking/filtering/modality routing is not adaptive set decoding.

2.1 Evidence Based Fact Checking and Evaluation

Evidence-based verification can typically be broken down into 4 components: claim analysis, evidence retrieval, evidence assessment and verdict prediction [CITE: zeng2021automated, zhou2020survey, islam2020survey]. The findings vary with the domain, the construction of labels, the coverage of modalities and controls for event or source overlaps based on the dataset surveys in [CITE: dulizia2021datasets,shah2022systematic,rastogi2022threet]. Difficulties with cross-domain transfer, deficient explanations, and benchmark protocols are still common issues [CITE: mridha2021review, aimeur2023review, lv2025survey]. Our assessment thus runs along the retrieval-and-selection chain, and has not been concerned with content-only classification.

Evidence Requirements are revealed through Text Benchmarks. SciFact contains sentence evidence from scientific publications, HoVer is multi-document reasoning, COVID-Fact is following evolving claims in the public-health arena, and X-Fact introduces multi-lingual variation [CITE: wadden2020scifact,jiang2020hover,saakyan2021covidfact,gupta2021xfact]. External-document systems minimize the reliance on stylistic shortcuts, while also incurring retrieval, freshness, and attribution errors [CITE: zhang2023realtime]. So, we separate evidence-set quality and verdict accuracy.

2.2 Multimodal Misinformation Detection

Content-based multimodal detectors include bilinear pooling [CITE: kumari2021amfb], hierarchical attention [CITE: meel2021han], topic memory [CITE: ying2021topicmemory] and cross-attention [CITE: ying2021crossattention], ambiguity learning [CITE: chen2022cafe] and multi-level fusion [CITE: jing2023mpfn] detectors. Contrastive, in-modality, stylometric or semantic-alignment signals are added in later systems [CITE: yan2024fmc,zhu2024ifis,nadeem2023ssm,uppada2022imagetext]. These designs help to classify post-level, however the fused representation does not reveal the source of a verdict, such as an external document or image.

Recent works focus on local-global mismatch [CITE: wu2023mfir], cross-modal knowledge distillation [CITE: hu2024matching,peng2024contextual], contextual inconsistency [CITE: shang2026semantic] and conflicting unimodal signals [CITE: wang2024neutralization]. Out-of-context benchmarks like NewsCLIPpings [CITE: luo2021newsclippings], COSMOS [CITE: aneja2023cosmos], and VERITE [CITE: papadopoulos2024verite] evaluate pairs of images and captions where they do not match in context but control unimodal bias. DGM4, SNIFFER, MMDFND, and IMFND extend localization/explanation/coverage of domain or vision language model usage [CITE: shao2023dgm4,qi2024sniffer,tong2024mmdfnd,jiang2025imfnd]. They are not primarily aimed at a set of external evidence identifiers that can be audited, but at manipulation or veracity.

2.3 Multimodal Evidence Retrieval and Verification

MOCHEG offers a common evaluation criterion for multimodal retrieval, three-way verdict prediction and explanation generation [CITE: yao2023mocheg]. M-RAV utilizes dense retrieval, image-text consistency verbalization, relevance estimation, and language-model verification [CITE: luu2027mrav] while MEVER builds a text-image evidence graph and does a token- and evidence-level fusion [CITE: zhang2026mever]. Before making a prediction, RED-DOT identifies and re-ranks external multimodal evidence [CITE: papadopoulos2025reddot]. Relevant studies to distinguish the utility from the semantic similarity and learning when visual retrieval is needed have been conducted [CITE: luo2026utility,tsoneva2026selective]. The various decision problems are addressed using fixed retrieval, relevance filtering, modality routing, utility ranking and adaptive set decoding.

AVerImaTeC assesses real-world image-text claims using open-web or organizer-provided knowledge, and its shared task conditions verdict scoring on evidence quality [CITE: cao2026averimatecshared]. In that context, REVEAL combines iterative sparse and dense retrieval [CITE: tariq2026reveal] whilst MMCV introduces multi-hop text, image and table evidence [CITE: wang2025mmcv]. MOCHEG's stable item corpus is different from their evidence units and access conditions, which means that their units need a separate transfer protocol.

2.4 Scientific and Citation-Grounded Evidence

Stable evidence locations in passages, tables and figures are provided by scientific verification. SciVer consists of claim-evidence instances extracted from scientific papers and annotated by experts; MuSciClaims focuses on figure-grounded claims and controlled reasoning perturbations; and M^2-TabFact covers tables presented across text and images [CITE: wang2025sciver,lal2025musciclaims,zhou2025m2tabfact]. These tools can be used to make more precise localization analysis, but they do not explicitly reflect political or breaking news verification.

An explanation might be expressed effectively, but with the use of the incorrect source. Evidence-rationale benchmarks check whether a prediction is connected to a selected span, and faithful-by-construction rationalizers limit predictors to explicit evidence subsets [CITE: deyoung2020eraser, jain2020faithful]. Citation-grounded generation assesses whether retrieved sources support generated statements [CITE: gao2023alce]; RAGTruth catalogs hallucinations that persist despite retrieval [CITE: niu2024ragtruth]; and citation masking tests whether a fact-checking explanation recovers the correct evidence [CITE: xing2025attribution]. The present study adopts released evidence identifiers rather than generated rationales and allows exact set precision, recall, F1, Jaccard similarity and exact match.

2.5 Calibration and Adaptive Set Decisions

A score may place the candidate correctly, but be a poor probability. There are various types of distortion families: temperature scaling, Platt mapping and nonparametric calibration [CITE: guo2017calibration]. After class weighting, prior correction is important as the optimized training distribution is different from the operating distribution. This calibrated membership distribution is then used in expected-utility decoding, while structurally different policies, such as top-k, thresholding and learned-count, use the same scores.

In this study, the encoder, retrieval model, evidence graph and verifier are all constant. The experiment evaluates packet reachability, prior-aware probability correction and exact expected-F1 set decoding for matched candidates, representations, tuning roles, and downstream verification. This design helps to isolate when adaptive evidence sizing is helpful, and when a calibrated threshold is adequate (Table [REF: tab:related-comparison]).

3. PROBLEM DEFINITION AND DATA

3.1 Task

Let claim q have a complete-gold evidence set G_q released and let retrieval return an ordered-packet

Equation: C_q = (c_q1, ..., c_q60).

which can hold a maximum of 40 text candidates and 20 image candidates. A selector returns S_q ⊆ C_q. The main claim-level score is

Equation: F1(S_q, G_q) = 2 |S_q ∩ G_q| / (|S_q| + |G_q|).

We macro-average over claims Eq. [REF: eq:set-f1]. Other evidence metrics are precision, recall, Jaccard similarity, exact-set match and selected count.

The primary population includes only claims for which the released annotation defines a nonempty complete-gold set. Claims without such a mapping remain in retrieval construction and do not get a perfect oracle score. If they are given an oracle F1 of one, this will give the false impression of a high apparent packet ceiling.

3.2 Dataset and Population Flow

MOCHEG is a dataset that includes image-text claims, separate image and text evidence as well as verdict labels [CITE: yao2023mocheg]. By content hash we pin released versions of the train, validation, corpus and sealed-test. The test annotations are not opened in the official test.

The 13,159 released train and validation claims are encoded for retrieval. Of these, 10,970 have a nonempty released complete-gold mapping and are the eligible evidence-set population. The reported selector test is based on the 1,490 released validation claims. Claim-grouped hashing places the remaining released claims in the fit, calibration, model-selection and internal-holdout roles. The roles are regenerated separately for the five deterministic partition seeds of 13, 37, 73, 101 and 137.

3.3 Reachability

The retrieval boundary is specified by two oracle summaries. For every claim that is eligible, the packet oracle returns C_q ∩ G_q. Its F1 is averaged only for claims with nonempty gold. Complete-gold coverage is the fraction of eligible claims for which G_q ⊆ C_q holds. We count eligible claims for which C_q ∩ G_q=empty set.

These quantities provide answers to different questions. Oracle F1 is the best evidence set that can be achieved from the packet. Complete-gold coverage asks whether exact recovery can be made. The zero-reachable count identifies claims on which every selector must receive zero recall. None of these measures gives credit to a selector for evidence not exposed in the process of retrieval.

3.4 Leakage Controls

Candidates' ranks are maintained before joining the gold identifiers. Only after candidate construction are the evidence labels used to fit the membership scorer, the calibration map, and selector hyperparameters in their respective roles. The locked validation set is not part of any fitted component. The formal-test label files are outside the execution path and their hashes are verified before and after each run.

4. METHOD

4.1 Fixed Retrieval Packet

To combine BGE-M3 dense sentence similarity with BM25, we use the reciprocal-rank fusion (RRF) [CITE: cormack2009rrf] approach. A reranker from BGE provides scores for the fused text candidates. Image retrieval is a blend of SigLIP2 image-text similarity and dense similarity between the claim and normalized image file names, also in the style of RRF. The model repositories and immutable revisions are specified in the frozen configuration, and the operational model cards are BGE-M3 (URL: https://huggingface.co/BAAI/bge-m3), BGE-reranker-v2-m3 (URL: https://huggingface.co/BAAI/bge-reranker-v2-m3), and SigLIP2 (URL: https://huggingface.co/google/siglip2-base-patch16-224). The top 40 text items and top 20 image items are kept in the retained packet. All the selectors get this same packet.

4.2 Membership Scorers

The final score and its square, dense and lexical or filename scores, RRF and cross-encoder scores, reciprocal ranks, modality indicators, token count, available-candidate count, and duplicate-group size are stored for each claim-candidate pair. Fitting-role statistics are used for the standardisation of features. A logistic scorer then estimates what the score is

Equation: q_qi = sigma(w^T ((x_qi - mu) / s) + b).

where q_qi is not an assumed operating probability, but rather a ranking score. Three different setups are compared: a shared text-image model, separate modality models and a shared model with the full feature vector with cross-encoder scores. Each organisation is trained with balanced and unweighted binary cross entropy variants plus L_2 regularisation. Balanced loss is convenient for rare evidence ranking but is not calibrated since it is based on a modified effective class prior.

Let z_qi=logit(q_qi). A map g_m is used to convert it to the membership probability used by the decision policy,

Equation: p_qi = g_m(z_qi) ≈ P(c_qi in G_q | x_qi).

A separate map is fitted for each modality. There are six maps which are compared:

1. identity (no effect on the raw sigmoid probability);
2. temperature scaling, sigma(z/T);
3. prior shift, which fits an intercept correction;
4. Platt mapping, sigma(az+b);
5. beta calibration (linear in log p and log(1-p)); and
6. isotonic regression, a monotone nonparametric map.

Calibration parameters are fitted on the calibration role. The scorer and map are selected based on average precision and Brier score in the different model-selection task. We report the Brier score, the negative log likelihood (NLL), the expected calibration error (ECE), the mean predicted probability, the empirical prevalence and the Brier skill relative to the predictor which forecasts the prevalence only.

The separation ensures that a calibrator cannot be picked on the locked-validation result. Average precision screens the ordering of the scorer, while Brier score selects the probability map for threshold and expected-utility decisions. The difference lies in the fact that any monotone map will maintain the rank, but can significantly change the set that is chosen.

4.3 Evidence-Set Policies

Figure [REF: fig:policy-decoding] shows the only operation that changes: how one shared calibrated ranking is translated to an evidence set.

Description: Decision policies for the same calibrated ranking. The top-5 uses one global size; thresholding uses an absolute cutoff; learned count predicts the size; expected-F1 evaluates all prefixes and returns the shortest utility-maximizing prefix. The numbers shown are not measurements, but represent the process.

Fixed top-k.

We tune k in [1, 2, 3, 5, 10, 20, 40, 60] and select the top k candidates. The control that is created is top-5.

Calibrated threshold.

If the threshold tau is specified then the selector returns all items for which p_qi>=tau, within the usual packet limit. The threshold is selected from [0.1, ..., 0.9].

Learned count.

The number of evidence items is predicted by a count model and the corresponding probability-ranked prefix returned.

Expected-F1 decoding.

The candidates are ranked based on the calibrated probability. A dynamic program exactly computes the expected F1 for each prefix length k, for independent Bernoulli membership variables. The selected size is

Equation: k_q* = arg max over 0 <= k <= 60 of E[F1(S_q,k, Y_q) | p_q1:60].

For prefix S_q,k the utility can be written

Equation: U_q(k) = E[2 T_q,k / (k + T_q,k + R_q,k) | p_q1:60].

with T_q,k being the number of items of relevance within the prefix and R_q,k the number of items of relevance outside the prefix. Two Poisson-binomial recurrences are used to compute their distributions as well as the expectation exactly. The shorter prefix is kept for ties. This rule is based on the actual membership distribution, not a single cut-off, or a separately predicted number.

We also carry out testing of two extensions. The first subtracts lambda k from expected utility. The second also subtracts gamma R(S_q,k), where R measures same-source redundancy. The size penalties range from 0 to 0.10 and the redundancy penalties range from 0 to 0.005. Extra structure in the decoder is kept as ablations, as the fitted optima indicate whether it provides additional benefit over calibration.

4.4 Decision Scope and Assumptions

The comparison alters just the set decision. The identity of the candidates, their rank, feature values, scorer outputs, and validation claims are shared across policies. Expected-F1 assumes that the memberships are conditionally independent Bernoullis, and that it only operates on prefixes of the frozen ranking, i.e., it does not reorder candidates or retrieve a missing item. The complete-gold mapping released is considered the evaluation target, where Sec. [REF: sec:limitations] comments on possible incompleteness of the mapping. These boundaries help to differentiate between a decoder error and retrieval error and help to prevent an adaptive policy from being given a more favorable candidate universe.

4.5 Shared Verifier

Evidence policies can improve set overlap and damage the downstream verdict. To isolate this possibility, a single verifier architecture and training process are used for all policies. Only the selected evidence is changed. We compare the verdict macro-F1 on the same 1,490 claims, and include the declared non-inferiority margin of -0.01 for expected-F1 vs fixed top-5.

5. EXPERIMENTAL DESIGN

5.1 Frozen Protocol

The setup of the configuration was fixed on 4 November 2026 prior to the outcome-bearing run. It stores data and model revisions, the 5 partition seeds for the claim groups, depths of retrieval, scorer groups, all calibration candidates, selector grids, evaluation metrics, bootstrap settings, and stopping rules. Prior-aware maps were added because the change of the training prior due to class weighting cannot generally be fixed by a temperature-only map. No locked-validation outcome was used for selecting between the calibration maps.

There are 36 different combinations of scorer families, loss weightings and calibration maps possible with the configuration. The results of screening are not included in scientific evidence. Only the training roles assigned are used for final model selection, with all the selected configuration then being evaluated once on locked validation.

5.2 Baselines and Outcomes

Fixed top-5, calibrated thresholding and learned count prediction are the controls which are managed by the selector. The adaptive set-utility variants are Expected-F1, size-penalized expected-F1 and size-plus-redundancy expected-F1. The main result is the claim-macro complete-gold evidence F1. The precision, recall, Jaccard similarity, exact-set match, selected count, calibration metrics and verdict macro-F1 are also reported.

The claim-level differences are averaged over the 5 deterministic partitions for a given validation claim, for each method comparison. A claim-aligned bootstrap resamples the 1,490 claims. Selector intervals reported are based on 10,000 samples. Familywise comparisons against fixed top-5 use Holm correction. Robustness intervals are descriptive, based on 5,000 samples.

Following the primary comparison, a distinction is made between validation claims with no released gold item reachable, some but not all released gold items reachable, or complete gold reachable. This diagnostic is clearly post hoc and descriptive. It identifies the origin of the aggregate gains without affecting the fitted models or the stated decision rule.

The superiority condition is to have a mean evidence-F1 gain of at least 0.015 over the best fixed rule, a positive gain in every partition, a corrected interval lower bound above zero, calibrated Brier performance better than the prevalence predictor, a reasonable selected-to-gold size ratio, verdict non-inferiority and exact clean reproduction. The selector will be superior if all the conditions are true at the system level.

5.3 Robustness Matrix

We rerun selection under five different packets (10 and 20 items, the full retained width and two packets with only text or only images), deterministic removal of 25% and 50% of reachable gold, promotion of five distractors, duplication of the top three items, and probability- and temperature perturbations of 0.5 and 2.0. The fitted selector does not change as a result of perturbations. They quantify the dependence on reachability, modality, contamination of the ranking, duplication and probability scale.

5.4 Audit and Reproduction

All candidate-level scores, calibrated probabilities, selected identifiers, claim-level metrics, selected hyperparameters, environment metadata and formal-test access flags are saved for every run. An independent audit re-calculates population size, calibration summaries, set metrics, paired effects and guard conditions from saved records. A clean rerun writes to a new directory and compares the core artifacts using SHA-256 instead of aggregate agreement.

The reporting and selector analyses are conducted using Python 3.13.14 with NumPy 2.5.4 on Windows 11. The light-weight scorers and verifier use deterministic numerical routines; the five partition seeds control claim-role assignment instead of stochastic neural training. All the pre-trained retrieval revisions, packet depths and decoding grids are fixed in the recorded configuration.

6.1 Retrieval Defines a Low Ceiling

At the actual operating depths of the selector, complete-gold recall for text is 0.3193 at 40 candidates and for images is 0.2663 at 20 candidates (Table [REF: tab:operating-retrieval]). The combined packet has an oracle evidence F1 of 0.3560. Among the 10,970 eligible claims, only 4.39% have all released gold items in the packet, while 3,086 have no reachable gold item. Most claims thus cannot be exactly recovered before set decoding starts.

The expanded retrieval audit also provides a better understanding of what parts are beneficial. The Dense-BM25 RRF has a text Recall@50 of 0.3224. When reranking with cross-encoder, it is improved to 0.3338 while early Recall@5 is decreased from 0.1772 to 0.1335. As for images, SigLIP2-filename achieves Recall@50 of 0.3149, whereas SigLIP2 gets 0.2183 and filename retrieval gets 0.2227. The text reranker is used to sacrifice the ordering aspect for a greater recall; image fusion is used to enhance retrieval.

As shown in Table [REF: tab:retrieval-matrix], the two branches, text and image, fail differently. Text RRF improves both MAP and nDCG over the first-stage models, while the cross-encoder gains deeper Recall@50 at the cost of early precision-sensitive ordering. Image fusion enhances all the reported cutoffs and both ranking metrics, yet its Recall@50 is still below the text branch. This means that a larger packet would contain some evidence that might be missing, but would also reveal a more challenging distractor distribution for the selector.

Table [REF: tab:reachability-strata] shows how adaptive selection comes in handy in the post hoc validation stratification. For the 79 claims where the entire released-gold set can be obtained, expected-F1 is 0.5240 F1, compared with 0.3593 for fixed top-5 and 0.4237 for thresholding. On the 1,200 partially reachable claims, expected-F1 is 0.1839 and does not improve on fixed top-5 at 0.1841; thresholding is best at 0.1965. The 211 claims where the gold is not reachable have a score of zero for all methods. The aggregate expected-F1 gain is thus localized in the small complete-reachability stratum, while thresholding is more effective for incomplete packets.

6.2 Prior-Aware Maps Repair Calibration

Table [REF: tab:calibration] makes a comparison between maps on locked validation, averaged over modalities and deterministic partitions. Brier scores for identity and temperature are 0.0847 and 0.0832, respectively. They differ by a small amount, consistent with a prior error for which a slope-only temperature is not a cure. Isotonic, Platt and prior-shift calibration further lower the Brier score to around 0.033, and improve the NLL and ECE. When compared to identity calibration, isotonic calibration decreases Brier error by approximately 61% and ECE from 0.1314 to 0.0063. All five partitions have positive Brier skill relative to the prevalence-only reference, with partition-level skill of roughly 14%.

Reliability on locked validation: Figure caption. Temperature scaling is still overconfident as it is unable to correct the class-prior shift. Three types of maps, prior-shift map, Platt and isotonic maps, closely approximate the observed membership rate.

Also, after enforcing absolute calibration, scorer choice changes. Partitions 13 and 37 use modality-specific balanced models while 73 represents an unweighted cross-encoder model, and 101 and 137 represent unweighted modality-specific models. For text maps, there is variation between isotonic, Platt and beta maps, while for image scoring, the map is isotonic in all five partitions. This variation may be helpful in considering scorer family, loss weighting, and calibration as a single selection problem.

6.3 Adaptive Selection Helps but Thresholding Wins

The important locked-validation results are shown in Table [REF: tab:selector-means]. Fixed top-5 reaches 0.1673 evidence F1. Expected-F1 reaches 0.1759 while selecting just 3.50 items per claim. Its precision rises from 0.2511 to 0.3725, while recall falls from 0.1753 to 0.1483. Expected-F1 trades recall for increased precision and a smaller review set.

The highest F1 measure is achieved by calibrated thresholding which selects 5.51 items and has an F1 of 0.1807. Learned count prediction gets 31.90 items and 0.1100 F1. Without a well matched set objective it is not competitive to predict the size.

The expected-F1 effect is consistent across the partition-level results in Table [REF: tab:partition-selector-results]. It has a stable precision-recall profile, a gain of +0.0076 to +0.0101 over fixed top-5 and a size range of 3.43 to 3.59. The method returns approximately 30% fewer items on average and improves F1 by approximately 5.1% relative to fixed top-5. There is no change in the baseline ordering (thresholding always wins).

The claim-aligned effects in Table [REF: tab:selector-effects-detail] and Fig. [REF: fig:selector-effects] confirm these differences. Expected-F1 improves on fixed top-5 by 0.00851 with a 95% interval of [0.00302,0.01400] and Holm-adjusted p=0.0078. Thresholding improves by 0.01334 [0.00931,0.01744]. Its direct advantage over expected-F1 is 0.00483 [0.00003,0.00957].

Figure caption: Claim-aligned evidence-F1 effects with respect to top-5 fixed. Points are mean differences and Bars are 95% intervals.

Both of the penalties are a perfect fit with plain expected-F1. In each partition, the size penalty selected is zero and the redundancy penalty selected is zero. The extra terms introduced complexity without altering the answer on this packet, as can be seen from their claim-level outputs. The quantifiable improvement is through calibrated probabilities.

There is not sufficient evidence to support the superiority condition. Despite the fact that the expected-F1 is good in all partitions and that the corrected interval does not contain zero, it still has a mean gain of 0.00851 which is less than the stated target of 0.015. This experiment, therefore, provides a small positive development effect, but not enough to surpass the superiority threshold.

6.4 The Verifier Safeguard Remains Unresolved

We find that the mean difference between expected-F1 and fixed top-5 is +0.00099 with a 95% interval of [-0.01053,+0.01279] under the shared verifier. The lower limit is just below the stated non-inferiority margin of -0.01. The point estimate is not far from zero, but the interval does not establish that verdict quality was preserved. Threshold-selected evidence has the highest verifier macro-F1 in every partition (Table [REF: tab:verifier-effects]).

6.5 Robustness Exposes Calibration Sensitivity

There is a positive effect of Expected-F1 under width 20 and 40, text-only evidence, and deterministic gold removal. It falls slightly behind fixed top-5 on image only packets. The impact of the promotion of five distractors is much bigger as the resulting fixed top-5 collapses when all five top positions are filled with distractors - this should not be interpreted as the normal operating impact.

The results are most sensitive to probability perturbations. Sharpening the probabilities with the temperature of 0.5 makes the effect -0.0193 and flattening with the temperature of 2.0 makes the effect -0.0221. The intervals are not inclusive of 0. So, Expected-F1 is sensitive to probability scale, which does not change candidate order.

Prior-aware maps correct the dominant calibration error and retrieval becomes a hard constraint on the performance that can be achieved. Calibrated expected-F1 offers a slight precision-oriented gain compared to fixed top-5, which is mostly based on claims with full reachable gold. Calibrated thresholding is stronger overall. The advantage depends on probability quality and still does not meet the verifier safeguard.

7. DISCUSSION

7.1 Calibration Changes the Scientific Conclusion

There are a number of requirements for the probabilities to have an operational meaning in the context of expected-F1 decoding; a probability input of 0.30 should represent roughly a 30% chance of membership. Balanced logistic training increases attention to the rare positives, but the intercept reflects the reweighted training distribution. This intercept problem remains largely unchanged with temperature scaling, since it only affects the slope of the logits. The low deployment prevalence is restored and the selected set size is changed significantly by prior-shift, Platt and isotonic maps.

The direction of the evidence-F1 comparison is changed by calibration. It also explains why the learned size and redundancy penalties are unnecessary: if the probabilities reflect the rare membership rate, then the unpenalized utility already favors short prefixes. When making an adaptive selection assessment, report the absolute calibration against the prevalence reference and the difference between the model's uncalibrated and calibrated scores.

7.2 Why Thresholding Is the Strong Baseline

Expected-F1 uses the memberships of all candidates, but its independence approximation and complete-gold objective may not match the actual data. A tuned threshold directly uses the same calibrated probabilities with fewer assumptions. Here it has more recall, moderate set growth and is 0.00483 F1 better than expected-F1.

A calibrated-threshold baseline is required for future evaluations. Beating a fixed budget is not enough if the same probabilities support a stronger threshold rule. An adaptive decoder should exceed that rule, offer an alternate precision-cost operating point, or enhance a downstream outcome. Expected-F1 gives the second: it returns approximately two fewer items than thresholding while being substantially more precise, which might be important if human review time is limited.

This interpretation is supported by the reachability strata. The main advantage of Expected-F1 is where the released-gold set is already complete, then there is the possibility of pruning a coherent short prefix from the packet. Once only a portion of the gold set is contained in the packet, it is not of benefit. The larger output and greater recovery of evidence is an advantage to thresholding in that larger stratum. This was a post hoc stratification, and therefore is not an appropriate way to make a prespecified claim for a subgroup. It does propose a concrete design target, that selection should take into consideration the probability of incomplete retrieval.

7.3 Retrieval Is the Larger Bottleneck

This packet oracle of 0.3560 puts the observed selector scores into perspective. Over 25% of eligible claims do not have any reachable released gold item and coverage for complete-gold claims is less than 5%. There is a relatively small range of possible improvements that can be achieved in decoders. The image-only results are particularly poor and the image-only expected-F1 effect is slightly negative.

Based on the retrieval audit, there are some ideas to move forward. The performance of image fusion is better than either image signal. Deeper recall is improved by text cross-encoder reranking, but the first few ranks are affected. Another ranker trained to maximize early complete-gold coverage, a claim-adaptive retrieval budget, or a selector that explicitly models packet completeness may yield more value than another selection penalty. This diagnosis can be made using the fixed packet since retrieval and decoding are performed separately.

7.4 Evidence Metrics and Verdict Metrics Differ

Evidence F1 and verdict macro-F1 answer related but distinct questions. A positive evidence effect does not establish verifier non-inferiority. The gap is close to the margin and could be due to limited validation size, a weak shared verifier, or different information content in the shorter sets. A larger or stronger verifier study is needed before claiming an end-to-end benefit.

For deployed fact-checking, evidence precision, evidence recall, review-set size, retrieval reachability, calibration and verdict performance are useful metrics for the operating report. An aggregate score obscures the balance between conciseness of evidence and downstream adequacy.

7.5 What the Study Supports

There are three claims that can be substantiated with the evidence. For adaptive probability-based evidence selection under imbalance, prior-aware calibration is needed. Calibrated expected-F1 can be better than the fixed top-5 rule for complete-gold evidence F1 and return fewer items, primarily when the retrieved packet contains the complete released-gold set. The most successful tested policy on this development population is calibrated thresholding. There is no evidence of an end-to-end advantage, a benefit from size or redundancy penalties, or generalization beyond the MOCHEG setting evaluated.

8. LIMITATIONS AND ETHICS

8.1 Limitations

For one, the reported study on selectors is based on MOCHEG validation claims which have been published. This run was locked, but previous analysis conducted as part of this overall project had utilized the same development resource. The outcome is a reanalysis which is controlled; new external confirmation is still pending. The official MOCHEG test annotations are kept sealed.

Second, complete-gold annotations may be incomplete in terms of all valid web evidence. Exact-set metrics reward agreement with the given mapping and may label a reasonable alternative as incorrect. The reachability-stratified analysis is post hoc and the complete-reachability result is based on 79 validation claims. It is a mechanism diagnosis which necessitates prespecified replication and cannot offer a population-level subgroup guarantee.

Thirdly, the membership scorers are designed to be as lightweight as possible to maintain experimental control and reproducibility. They are not intended as state-of-the-art representation learners. Better multimodal rerankers could change both ranking and calibration. Another corpus should be used to test the repeated selection of isotonic regression for images.

The saved records can determine the software environment and the runtime of the deterministic analysis but do not necessarily reveal a complete end-to-end analysis of embedding and retrieval in terms of compute, latency or energy. Thus, no efficiency claim is made on the study other than the number of items of evidence returned to a reviewer.

Fourth, the expected-F1 calculation is based on the assumption that there is conditional independence of the candidate memberships. The evidence items within the same source, duplicate images and complementary sentences are in violation of this assumption. The tested redundancy penalty wasn't of assistance, though, which doesn't preclude a learnt structured model.

Fifth, the robustness conditions are diagnostics and only address a small portion of the possible production drift. The temperature perturbations do not estimate the frequency of such distortion in a deployed system; they only measure sensitivity to probability scale.

Lastly, external datasets are actually different. AVerImaTeC involves web or curated question answer evidence and MMCV involves tables and multi-hop support. Porting the selector involves a redefinition of evidence units, a new calibration study and an evaluation done with sensitivity to access conditions of each resource.

8.2 Ethical Considerations

Automated fact checking can also engender a false sense of security, especially when evidence is not retrieved. The interface for any deployment should include identifiers for the sources, restrictions on the retrievals, probability calibration status, and uncertainty. The system should support trained reviewers, not replace them.

Geographic, language and source selection biases can be carried over to data and model artefacts. MOCHEG is not meant to be a representative sample of all claims or communities. Generalisation of performance to low-resource languages or sensitive domains should be avoided unless specifically evaluated. Personal, disturbing or copyrighted material may be included in web evidence and access and display should be in keeping with the terms of the source and minimize unnecessary reproduction.

The formal test is still kept sealed, bad and unfavourable results are kept and all comparisons reported are accompanied by uncertainty. These controls minimize the selective reporting and it is easier to differentiate a helpful development finding from a deployment claim.

9. CONCLUSION

We studied adaptive multimodal evidence selection as a probability-dependent decision within a fixed retrieval pipeline. Prior-aware calibration allows expected-F1 to reach 0.1759 evidence F1 while returning 3.50 items per claim, a gain of 0.0085 over fixed top-5. The gain focuses on claims with a complete released-gold set that is accessible. Calibrated thresholding proves stronger overall at 0.1807, and the size and redundancy penalties are of no measurable benefit. The shared verifier does not establish non-inferiority, and probability perturbations reverse the adaptive gain. Evidence-selector studies should thus report jointly: 1) what retrieval makes reachable, 2) whether membership scores are calibrated to the operating prior, and 3) whether a structured decision rule outperforms a calibrated threshold. In this context, calibration is useful, additional penalties on the decoder are not, and retrieval is the primary constraint.
