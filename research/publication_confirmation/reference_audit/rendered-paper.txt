Abstract—An evidence selector cannot recover items missed by
retrieval, and probability-based set decisions become unreliable
when membership scores are miscalibrated. We study these
coupled failures on MOCHEG with a common packet of 40
text and 20 image candidates. The protocol covers 10,970 claims
with released complete-gold evidence and evaluates 1,490 locked
validation claims across five deterministic training-role partitions.
A label-blind weighted rank fusion increases text Recall@40 from
0.3193 to 0.3292 over all eligible claims, packet-oracle evidence F1
from 0.3560 to 0.3637, and complete-gold coverage from 4.39%
to 4.74%; image Recall@20 is unchanged at 0.2663. Prior-aware
calibration reduces the earlier mean Brier score from 0.0847
to about 0.033. On the repaired packet, fixed top-5, calibrated
thresholding, and expected-F1 reach 0.1712, 0.1864, and 0.1815
evidence F1, respectively. Expected-F1 returns 3.61 items per
claim, while thresholding returns 5.26. A prespecified packetcompleteness switch attains the highest point estimate, 0.1877 F1
with 4.70 items, but its gain over thresholding is only 0.00138
(95% interval [−0.00191, 0.00462]) and one partition is negative.
Under a stronger shared verifier, the switch changes macroF1 from 0.3911 to 0.3954; aggregate non-inferiority passes, but
only two of five seed-specific intervals clear the margin. Formaltest annotations remain sealed. The study supports retrievalconditioned, calibration-aware evaluation and a compact operating
point, but not selector superiority or external generalization.
Index Terms—multimodal fact-checking, evidence retrieval,
evidence selection, probability calibration, expected F1, reproducibility

I. I NTRODUCTION
A fact-check is auditable only when a reader can inspect
the material behind its verdict. For an image–text claim, that
material can include sentences, documents, and images drawn
from different sources. A retrieval system may return hundreds
of plausible candidates, but a reviewer still needs a short set
that contains enough support or refutation to make a decision.
MOCHEG formalizes this setting with multimodal claims,
separate text and image evidence, released evidence identifiers,
and three-way verdicts [1].
Evidence selection is often reduced to a fixed top-k rule. It
is simple and reproducible, but it assumes that every claim
needs the same number of items. Adaptive policies instead
map candidate-level membership scores to a claim-specific
set. A threshold returns every item above a cutoff, a count
model predicts the set size, and expected-F1 decoding chooses
the probability- ranked prefix with the greatest expected set

F1. These rules share a dependency that rank-only evaluation
can hide: the scores must behave like probabilities. A monotone transformation preserves candidate order while changing
threshold and expected-utility decisions.
Class imbalance makes this dependency consequential. Gold
evidence is rare in a retrieved packet, while balanced logistic
training deliberately changes the effective class prior. A
temperature map can adjust confidence without fixing an
intercept error produced by that prior mismatch. Modern
calibration work therefore distinguishes ranking, probability
quality, and the decision made from those probabilities [2]. In
an evidence pipeline, an apparently stronger adaptive decoder
may otherwise be exploiting or amplifying a calibration error.
Retrieval creates a separate, harder boundary. A selector
cannot return an item that never enters its packet. If packet
reachability is not reported, a retrieval miss and a decoding
error receive the same final evidence score. Figure 1 shows
the information boundaries used here. All selection policies
receive the same 40 text and 20 image candidates. Candidate
rankings are written before the gold join; scoring, calibration,
policy selection, and evaluation use disjoint roles; and the
released validation set is evaluated only after train-role choices
are fixed.
The study asks four questions:
1) How much released gold evidence is reachable at the
actual packet depths, and can a bounded label-blind rank
fusion improve that ceiling?
2) Which probability maps correct the prior error induced
by imbalanced evidence membership?
3) How do fixed top-k, calibrated thresholding, learned
count, expected-F1, and an uncalibrated ablation compare
on identical packets?
4) Does a prespecified packet-completeness switch beat
calibrated thresholding and preserve verdict quality under
a stronger shared verifier?
The answers are deliberately mixed. Weighted text fusion
improves the all-eligible packet oracle from 0.3560 to 0.3637
F1 and complete-gold coverage from 4.39% to 4.74%, while
image Recall@20 does not change. Prior-aware maps repair the
dominant probability error. On the repaired packet, calibrated
thresholding reaches 0.1864 evidence F1, expected-F1 reaches
0.1815 with a smaller 3.61-item set, and uncalibrated expectedF1 collapses to 0.1288 while returning 24.90 items. The

A. Evidence-Based Fact-Checking and Evaluation

C. Multimodal Evidence Retrieval and Verification

Evidence-based verification can typically be broken down
into four components: claim analysis, evidence retrieval,
evidence assessment and verdict prediction [10], [11], [12].
The findings vary with the domain, label construction, modality coverage, and controls for event or source overlap, as
documented in dataset surveys [13], [14]. Difficulties with
cross-domain transfer, deficient explanations, and benchmark
protocols remain common [15], [16], [17]. Our assessment
therefore follows the retrieval-and-selection chain rather than
content-only classification.

MOCHEG offers a common evaluation criterion for multimodal retrieval, three-way verdict prediction and explanation
generation [1]. MEVER builds a text–image evidence graph
and performs token- and evidence-level fusion [7], while REDDOT identifies and reranks external multimodal evidence before
prediction [3]. Other recent studies distinguish utility from
semantic similarity and learn when visual retrieval is needed [8],
[9]. These systems address fixed retrieval, relevance filtering,
modality routing, utility ranking, and adaptive set decoding as
different decision problems.

E. Calibration and Adaptive Set Decisions
A score may rank a candidate correctly while remaining a
poor probability. Post-hoc calibration includes temperature and
Platt mappings, beta calibration, and nonparametric isotonic
regression; each makes different assumptions about the distortion being corrected [2]. After class weighting, prior correction
is important because the optimized training distribution differs
from the operating distribution. The calibrated membership
distribution can then support expected-utility decoding, while
structurally different policies such as top-k, thresholding, and
learned count use the same scores.
In this study, representation models and evidence identities
remain fixed while a bounded, label-blind rank fusion changes
only the retained ordering. The selector experiment then
evaluates packet reachability, prior-aware probability correction,
exact expected-F1 decoding, and one prespecified completenessaware switch under matched candidates, tuning roles, and
downstream verification. This design isolates when adaptive
evidence sizing helps and when a calibrated threshold is
sufficient (Table I).

Stable evidence locations in passages, tables and figures
are provided by scientific verification. SciVer consists of
claim-evidence instances extracted from scientific papers and
annotated by experts; MuSciClaims focuses on figure-grounded
claims and controlled reasoning perturbations; and M2 -TabFact
covers tables presented across text and images [4], [5], [6].
These tools can be used to make more precise localization
analysis, but they do not explicitly reflect political or breakingnews verification.

Calibration parameters are fitted on the calibration role. The
scorer and map are selected based on average precision and
Brier score in the different model-selection task. We report the
Brier score, the negative log likelihood (NLL), the expected
calibration error (ECE), the mean predicted probability, the
empirical prevalence and the Brier skill relative to the predictor
which forecasts the prevalence only.
The separation ensures that a calibrator cannot be picked
on the locked-validation result. Average precision screens the
ordering of the scorer, while Brier score selects the probability
map for threshold and expected-utility decisions. The difference
lies in the fact that any monotone map will maintain the rank,
but can significantly change the set that is chosen.

E. Decision Scope and Assumptions
The comparison alters just the set decision. The identity
of the candidates, their rank, feature values, scorer outputs,
and validation claims are shared across policies. Expected-F1
assumes that the memberships are conditionally independent
Bernoullis, and that it only operates on prefixes of the frozen
ranking, i.e., it does not reorder candidates or retrieve a missing
item. The complete-gold mapping released is considered the
evaluation target, where Sec. VIII comments on possible
incompleteness of the mapping. These boundaries help to
differentiate between a decoder error and retrieval error and

F. Shared Verifier
Evidence policies can improve set overlap while damaging
the downstream verdict. We therefore hold verifier architecture, training data, features, and optimization constant while
changing only the selected evidence. The primary verifier is a
two-layer multilayer perceptron with one 64-unit ReLU hidden
layer, inverse-frequency class weighting, 300 full-batch Adam
epochs, learning rate 0.01, and L2 coefficient 0.001. It is trained
on unique fixed, threshold, and expected-F1 evidence sets from
fitting claims. A logistic linear verifier is retained as a capacity
control. Both models compare calibrated thresholding with the
frozen completeness-aware switch on the same 1,490 validation
claims and five deterministic seeds. The declared non-inferiority
margin is −0.01 macro-F1.

D. Evidence and Verdict Safeguards Answer Different Questions
Evidence F1 measures overlap with released evidence identifiers; verdict macro-F1 measures whether selected evidence
supports the final three-way decision. The stronger MLP verifier
clears aggregate non-inferiority for the switch, but the interval
crosses zero, three seed-specific safeguards fail, and the linear
control moves in the opposite direction. The result is compatible
with preserved verdict quality on average, but not with a stable
end-to-end benefit.
This distinction matters for system reporting. Evidence precision, evidence recall, set size, retrieval reachability, calibration,
and verdict performance should be shown separately. A single

score would hide whether a compact set saved reviewer effort,
omitted necessary support, or merely interacted favorably with
one verifier seed.
E. What the Study Supports
The evidence supports four bounded statements. First, packet
reachability must be measured before selector performance is
interpreted. Second, prior-aware calibration is necessary for
probability-based set decisions under imbalanced membership.
Third, expected-F1 supplies a compact alternative to fixed
top-5, but calibrated thresholding is the strongest confirmed
policy. Fourth, a completeness-aware switch is a plausible
design direction whose present gain is too small and uncertain
to establish superiority. The study does not support a state-ofthe-art, externally generalizable, or deployment-ready claim.

B. Ethical Considerations
Automated fact-checking can create false confidence when
relevant evidence is missing. A deployed interface should
expose source identifiers, retrieval limits, calibration status, and
the distinction between “no evidence retrieved” and “evidence
supports no conclusion.” The system should assist trained
reviewers, not replace them.
Web evidence can contain personal, disturbing, copyrighted,
geographically skewed, or politically sensitive material. Retrieval and display should follow source terms, minimize
unnecessary reproduction, and provide safeguards for reviewers.
MOCHEG is not representative of all languages, communities,
claim types, or media ecosystems; results should not be
generalized to low-resource or high-stakes domains without
direct evaluation.

completeness, and verifier summaries and independent audits
for each stage. Runtime-bearing summaries are independently
recomputed rather than expected to match byte-for-byte.
There are exactly 1,490 locked-validation claim records
in each of the five selector partitions for each method. The
repaired candidate bundle contains 789,540 rows for 13,159
released train and validation claims before the completegold eligibility filter. Candidate records store scorer output,
calibrated probability, rank, modality, gold membership, and
selected-set decisions. Selector, completeness, verifier, audit,
and reporting records all contain false formal-test access flags.
F. External Evaluation Path
AVerImaTeC offers an evaluated test setting and a public task
report [45]. A useful transfer study would map its question–
answer evidence to a stable selectable unit, fit calibration only
on released training resources, and report the official conditional
evidence–verdict score together with set size and reliability. A
separate three-modality study would need a hash-locked split,
table representation, and multi-hop set objective. MOCHEG
calibration parameters should not be reused in either transfer.
