Efforts aimed at detecting widespread dissemination fake news,
which has caused societal damage, face inherent constraints
indicative of ineffectual endeavor. To tackle this dilemma and
identify fake news at an early stage, various social context in-
formation has been leveraged. These include user proﬁling [12],
[13], assessment of publisher credibility [14], and analysis of
user comments [15]. Nonetheless, the incorporation of extra
auxiliary information demands substantial time and labor in-
vestments, limiting its applicability in data-sparse scenarios.
Moreover, the propagation topology structures and temporal
features within the limited propagation paths may not sufﬁce for
comprehensive detection. Consequently, the reliance solely on
propagation-enhanced detection methods may leave signiﬁcant
blind spots, allowing fake news to evade detection until after
substantial societal hazards have been inﬂicted from widespread
dissemination.
The integration of propagation paths improves the detection
performance, albeit accompanied by associated social hazards
stemming from the proliferation of fake news. This leads us
to ponder: Can we leverage the abundant social context in the
propagation paths to enhance the early-stage detection before
widespread dissemination? In contrast to current discriminative
models, our motivation lies in forecasting and generating the
forthcoming propagation paths based on the multimodal content
before the proliferation of fake news. As depicted in Fig. 1(b),
our aim is to capture the propagation topology pattern within the
user engagement network, thereby facilitating the generation of
propagation paths, thus enhancing the capacity for early detec-
tion of emergent news. This generation-based strategy does not
necessitate additional social context during news spreading and
is anticipated to generate rich propagation features to enhance
detection capabilities while migrating the social hazards from
news spreading.
However, generating propagation paths to simulate propaga-
tion process encounters signiﬁcant challenges, primarily due
to three key reasons. First, the effective and reasonable user
encoding is the pre-requisite for path generation task. The
propagation process in the whole social platform contains large
unlabeled interactions among various roles involved in the news
dissemination process, making it non-trivial to model the whole
propagation process and learn the user representation effec-
tively. Second, the sheer size of the user engagements makes
it intractable to generate the propagation paths in discrete space
that adequately captures the pattern of real news spreading. Fi-
nally, modeling structural and temporal features from generated
propagation paths simultaneously while enhancing the detec-
tion performance of multimodal fake news poses signiﬁcant
challenges.
In this paper, we propose a novel Reinforced P ropagation
Path Generation Fake News Detection (RPPG-Fake) model for
multimodal fake news detection. Diverging from the prevail-
ing discriminative detection methods, we propose to simulate
realistic propagation paths based on news content at the early
detection stage. To generate reasonable user embedding, the
global user interaction process is modeled as the heterogeneous
graph. In particular, the self-supervised dual-contrastive learning
mechanism is designed to capture the propagation topology fea-
ture and heterogeneous relation features in heterogeneous social
graph. The generation process of propagation path is facilitated
by a novel reinforcement learning module. This module aids in
simulating user engagements temporally, which contributes to
conducting an efﬁcient exploration from steer size user searching
space. Moreover, a temporal-aware detection module is pro-
posed, wherein a dynamic graph neural network is designed
to encode temporal information from generated propagation
paths, facilitating enhanced performance for ﬁnal detection. The
RPPG-Fake undergoes comprehensive evaluation across three
datasets, with the experimental results illustrating the efﬁcacy
and superiority of our proposal.
Our contributions are summarized as follows:
r To the best of our knowledge, we are the ﬁrst to explore the
challenge of early fake news detection by generating prop-
agation paths, which leverage the advantages of extensive
social context while mitigating social hazards.
r We propose a novel RPPG-Fake model to generate rea-
sonable propagation paths via reinforcement learning, and
further integrate propagation topology structure and tem-
poral depth information through a temporal-aware fake
news detection module.
r Comprehensive experiments across three real-world
datasets demonstrate that our proposal consistently sur-
passes state-of-the-art baselines in fake news detection.
II. P ROBLEM FORMULA TION
Deﬁnition 1 (Fake News Detection): In the context of fake
news detection, a news item is represented as n = {nT,nV },
where nT refers to the textual content and nV refers to the
visual content. The collection of all news items is denoted
as N = {n1,n2,...,n |N|}. We also deﬁne the set of users
as U = {u1,u2,...,u |U|}, and the set of publishers as P =
{p1,p2,...,p |P|}. For a speciﬁc news item, the propagation
paths at a given timet are depicted asGt =( Ut,Et), where each
edge ei ∈ Et represents a connection between two interacting
users. The complete set of propagation paths during all spreading
times is denoted asG = {G1,G2,...,G T}, whereT denotes the
ﬁnal spreading time. The primary goal of fake news detection
is to model a probability distribution P(y|n,G), where y =0
indicates fake news and y =1 stands for real.
Deﬁnition 2 (Fake News Early Detection): During the early
detection stage, the available propagation paths are limited and
may even be nonexistent. In this condition, the absence of user
interactions for a speciﬁc news item n is represented by the
sequence G = {∅}. Our objective is to generate propagation
paths and effectively distinguish between the real news and
fabricated news with insufﬁcient propagation paths at an early
detection stage.
