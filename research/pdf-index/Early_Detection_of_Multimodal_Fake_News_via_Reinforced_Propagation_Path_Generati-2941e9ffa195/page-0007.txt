B. Quantitative Evaluation
Each detection model is executed ﬁve times, with the average
outcomes presented in Tables II and III. A comprehensive com-
parison of full propagation paths with propagation-enhanced
methods is conducted, as shown in Table II. Our proposed
RPPG-Fake achieves competitive results when compared with
other propagation-enhanced baselines. Notably, RPPG-Fake
surpasses all baselines on the PolitiFact and PHEME dataset
across all evaluated metrics. This indicates that the gener-
ated propagation paths effectively provide additional contex-
tual information, even in scenarios where data is sufﬁcient.
The improvement of detection capabilities is achieved through
the incorporation of comprehensive propagation paths into
propagation-enhanced methods. The propagation-enhanced ap-
proach Bi-GCN effectively leverages all propagation-path data
during news dissemination, resulting in the optimal performance
on the GossipCop dataset. Notably, the propagation paths within
the GossipCop dataset provide abundant supplementary social
context information for fake news detection. While an increase
in generated propagation paths may not necessarily introduce
additional social context for detection, our proposed method still
achieves the second-best performance on precision metric on
the GossipCop dataset. This ﬁnding suggests that the generation
path is particularly effective in data-sparse scenarios and can
enhance detection accuracy even when sufﬁcient propagation
path information is available.
To assess the detection efﬁcacy of RPPG-Fake at early stages
of news propagation, comparative analyses with early detection
benchmarks are presented in Table III. For the simulation of
early detection scenarios within the test dataset, early user
engagements are retained randomly within the range of three
to ﬁve as the accessible propagation paths. It is evident that
the performance of propagation-enhanced methods signiﬁcantly
deteriorates, at times falling below that of some content-based
methods such as CAFE. This suggests that a limited number
of propagation paths struggle to capture the complex global
structures and temporal information effectively during news
spreading. Further comparisons are made with large language
model (LLM) baselines such as LLAMA2 [36] and Detect-
GPT [37], which utilize zero-shot prompt engineering. Inter-
estingly, these LLM-based approaches underperform compared
to both propagation-enhanced methods (in Table II) and our
RPPG-Fake, indicating that textual features alone are inadequate
for improving the accuracy of detection. The integration of
generated propagation paths by RPPG-Fake markedly enhances
detection capabilities, underscoring the critical importance of
ample propagation paths. Notably,RPPG-Fakeconsistently sur-
passes all baselines across three datasets, registering accuracy
improvements of +4.0% on PolitiFact, +4.1% on GossipCop,
and +2.7% on Pheme. Our proposal RPPG-Fake, which gen-
erates propagation paths from news content, presents mini-
mal social hazards while demonstrating superior performance
at early stage detection compared to propagation-enhanced
methods.
