3.3. DFN module based on GAN and D–S evidence theory
The simple concatenation mechanism has been employed in several 
previous multimodal fake news detection tasks [10–12], where fixed 
fusion weights are applied to the text and image unimodalities. How-
ever, certain posts may exhibit high uncertainty due to various factors. 
To address this issue, the DFN module is designed to incorporate the 
confidence of each modality within its internal structure. The architec-
ture of the DFN is illustrated in Fig.  5. Intuitively, evidence 1 from the 
textual perspective exhibits lower uncertainty than evidence 2 from the 
visual perspective. Consequently, the unimodal-text decision strategy 
is selected for the final output. In this section, we provide a detailed 
explanation of the rules governing the strategy selection.

captured with respect to the final decision value. Subsequently, the con-
fidence scores (normalized coefficients of variation) of each modality, 
along with the inherent uncertainty associated with each, are combined 
to derive the final decision opinion, as shown in Fig.  6. We fully lever-
age the advantage of D–S evidence-theoretic fusion. Specifically, when 
both modalities exhibit low confidence levels, the final classification 
must align with the low-confidence set; in this scenario, the strengths 
of the multimodal heterogeneous graph are utilized to determine the 
final decision value. Conversely, when both modalities demonstrate 
high confidence, the classification result is typically drawn from the 
high-confidence set, at which point the decision value is randomly 
selected from one of the modalities. If only one modality has high 
confidence, the final classification is determined by that modality. In 
cases of conflicting decisions between the two modalities, the final 
classification confidence is reduced, and the decision-making capabili-
ties of the multimodal heterogeneous graph are adopted. To facilitate 
this, a threshold is established as a measurement criterion, detailed 
in Algorithm 1. The selection of this threshold is discussed further in 
Section 4.8.2.
