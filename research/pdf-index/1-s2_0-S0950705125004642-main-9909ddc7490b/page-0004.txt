SpotFake [10] uses a pre-trained language model to extract features, 
leveraging large-scale textual and visual pre-trained models to create 
a multimodal representation vector by simply concatenating features 
from each unimodal state, which is then fed into a binary classifier 
for fake news detection. Similarly the SAFE [12] model employs sim-
ple fusion to detect fake news through a similarity-based approach. 
Wu et al. [30] proposed the multimodal common attention network 
(MCAN) for fake news detection, which learns the inter-dependencies 
between multimodal features and achieves commendable results. Wang 
et al. [33] proposed a fake news detection framework called MetaFEND, 
which is effective in detecting breaking news because it learns from 
a small number of verified posts. However, this method increases the 
cost of detection. Song et al. [28] added a graph convolutional network 
(GCN) to their framework, called KATMF, using textual information 
fused with knowledge concepts and visual information for multimodal 
modal semantic representation. In addition, other researchers, such Xue 
et al. [29] and Fung et al. [34], have explored the consistency of mul-
timodal data to help detect fake news. Wang et al. [31] developed the 
COOLANT framework using powerful cross-modal contrastive learning 
methods. This framework aims to achieve more accurate image–text 
alignment. To further capture the fine-grained alignment between the 
visual and linguistic elements, they employed auxiliary tasks to miti-
gate the loss associated with negative samples during the contrastive 
process. Zhang et al. [26] introduced an end-to-end model named 
BDANN, which is a BERT-based domain-adaptive neural network de-
signed for effective detection of multimodal fake news. MPFN [25] can 
more accurately identify fake news by integrating multiple information 
sources, such as text and images. Specifically, this approach first ex-
tracts features from each information source and then combines these 
features using a progressive fusion strategy to obtain the final detection 
results. Compared to methods relying on a single information source, 
this approach captures the complex characteristics of fake news more 
effectively, thereby improving detection accuracy. Wang et al. [35] 
proposed an instance-guided multimodal graph fusion method that 
detects fake news by modeling both internal and external relationships 
between images and text. This method uses multimodal information 
to dynamically fuse the relationships between different modalities, 
enhancing the identification of fake news. Singhai et al. [36] introduced 
a multimodal fake news detection architecture that effectively identifies 
and suppresses information from weak modalities while extracting 
relevant information from strong modalities. CAFE [32] captures am-
biguous relationships between different modalities, thereby enhancing 
the accuracy of fake news detection. Existing multimodal FND ap-
proaches typically directly fuse unimodal features into the multimodal 
representation before detecting the authenticity of news samples. How-
ever, this approach overlooks the authenticity neutralization effect 
across different modalities. Therefore, Wang et al. [37] proposed a 
novel detection framework named MINER-UVS, which utilizes uni-
modal authenticity signals in multimodal fake news detection. Wang 
et al. [24] observed that users on media platforms are likely to label 
only a small number of posts from categories they are interested in. 
As a result, they introduced positive unlabeled learning to the domain 
of fake news detection and proposed a curriculum-based multimodal 
masking transformer network named CMMTN for detecting positive 
unlabeled fake news.
The feature fusion mechanisms of existing SOTA techniques are 
presented in Table  1. As observed, in the existing detection frameworks, 
the multimodal features are obtained by performing concatenation 
on the learned unimodal features, where each modality contributes 
equally to the final multimodal feature. This fusion method is re-
ferred to as static fusion. As illustrated in Fig.  2(a), traditional static 
fusion approaches fail to account for the modality noise generated 
during unimodal feature learning. These methods simply concatenate 
each modality with fixed weights, disregarding the confusion level 
inherent in each modality’s data. Although such methods have im-
proved fake news detection performance to some extent, the features 
produced by the multimodal feature learner are often cluttered with 
noise interference from individual modalities. This ultimately affects 
the performance of the final detector. To address this, we propose a 
dynamic fusion mechanism based on D–S theory, assigning a confidence 
score to each modality and dynamically adjusts the modality weights 
accordingly.
2.3. Multimodal dynamic fusion
Multimodal dynamic fusion allows the model to take full advantage 
of the complementary features of different modalities, which in turn 
improves robustness and generalization performance. To better analyze 
human multimodal languages, Han et al. [38] proposed a new trust-
worthy multimodal classification algorithm that dynamically evaluates 
both the feature and modality levels of information for different sam-
ples, thus credibly integrating multiple modalities. Based on theoretical 
analyses, Zhang et al. [14] further revealed that the generalization 
ability of dynamic fusion is closely linked to the performance of un-
certainty estimation. They proposed generalized dynamic multimodal 
fusion framework for low-quality data, which uses the generalization 
error derived from multimodal fusion to dynamically update each 
unimodal predictor. This approach enables the multimodal decision-
making process to rely more heavily on higher-quality modalities while 
mitigating the influence of unreliable ones by dynamically determining 
the fusion weights for each modality.
Inspired by these studies, we propose a dynamic fusion mechanism 
for the fake news detection task that effectively balance the weights of 
each modality.
