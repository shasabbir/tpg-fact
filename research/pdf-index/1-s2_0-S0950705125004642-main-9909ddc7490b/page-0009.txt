4. Experiments
In this section, we first present the public dataset used in the 
experiments and the basic setup of the experiments. Common baseline 
models used for fake news detection are also listed. In addition, the 
performance of the proposed dynamic fusion framework for fake news 
detection is experimentally evaluated. The superior performance of 
the MDF-FND framework is well demonstrated in relevant ablation 
experiments. Finally, we further explore the effective selection of each 
module and loss function by means of qualitative and quantitative 
analyses and analyze the impact of hyperparameters on the model.
4.1. Datasets
To demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed model, we con-
ducted experiments using three public datasets: Twitter, Weibo, and 
PolitiFact. These datasets originate from different language environ-
ments and provide valuable reference points for evaluating the gen-
eralization performance of the MDF-FND framework.
Twitter dataset. This dataset was collected and published by the 
MediaEval Benchmarking Initiative (Boididou et al. [23]) to evaluate 
the performance of multimodal models. The fake news in the datasets 
was sourced from news websites that were manually assessed to have 
low credibility, while the real news was crawled from well-known 
authoritative news sites such as the New York Times. Specifically, the 
dataset contains a large amount of social tweet content consisting of 
text and images, and its training set consists of 6000 fake news posts 
and 5000 real posts. The test set contains different types of breaking 
news, with up to 2000 posts. Posts containing only images or text are 
forcibly removed and not allowed to participate in the testing process of 
the model. Given its extensive data samples and diverse content types, 
this dataset closely resembles real news environments. Moreover, many 
posts in the Twitter dataset contain noise, which can significantly affect 
the final classification outcomes. Therefore, it is crucial to conduct 
experiments using uncertainty estimation and dynamic fusion strategies 
on this dataset.
Weibo dataset. To validate the robustness of the MDF-FND frame-
work, the Weibo dataset is included, which belongs to a different 
linguistic environment than Twitter. This Chinese dataset has been 
widely used in recent studies to verify the superior performance of 
their fake news detectors. This dataset was first collected and publicly 
released by Jin et al. [4] and contains only Chinese posts and com-
ment information. Weibo actively encourages users to report suspicious 
accounts, malicious hype, and comments related to trending events. 
The generation of fake posts involves two major steps: first, ordinary 
users report suspected fake news on the Weibo platform, but this alone 
cannot serve as the final judgment. Subsequently, a committee of well-
known users conducts a further review to verify the authenticity of 
these reports. For non-rumor posts, those verified by the authoritative 
Chinese news agency Xinhua are used. Low-quality images and posts 
were kept in our experiments to further demonstrate the robustness of 
our classification model. The training set constitutes 70% of the entire 
dataset and the rest is divided into validation and test sets in a 1:2

detection based on factual consistency checks, whereas the MDF-FND 
model is primarily designed for semantic detection. To explore the

4.2. Experimental setup
Parameter settings. We adopt a common approach used in many 
previous studies [11,12,48]. The text embeddings are initialized us-
ing bert-base-chinese and bert-base-uncased sentence- and word-level 
annotations for the Weibo and Twitter datasets, respectively, with 
the obtained feature dimensions of 𝑑𝑡 equal to 768. To avoid the 
catastrophic forgetting of previous generalized knowledge [52], the 
parameters of the BERT model [46] were frozen during model training. 
For the image data, we standardized the image size to 224 × 224 × 3 
and fed it into the pre-trained ResNet-50 network [47], extracting 
features with a dimension of 𝑑𝑣 = 2048. In the UEM module, which 
models intra-modal uncertainty, a linear network was employed to 
obtain the query, key, and value vectors for each modality. Prior to this, 
the layer normalization is applied. To prevent the risk of overfitting, 
dropout = 0.1 was considered for the attention linear layer, and each 
linear layer was followed by a nonlinear transformation using the GELU 
activation function, employing 12 heads. Experiments showed that a 
threshold 𝛾 of 0.4 in Algorithm 1 performs excellently on the Twitter 
dataset. Similarly, for the Weibo dataset, the threshold 𝛾 is set to 0.35. 
The reparameterized sample size was set to 5. The dropout of the GAT 
layer was set to 0.4, while the ELU activation function was employed. 
After we embedded the fake news detector into the GAT structure, it 
consisted of 2 linear layers and employed a dropout value of 0.4 and 
ReLU activation function for nonlinear mapping. The model was trained 
on a batch size of 128 over 40 epochs, with the learning rate initialized 
to 1e−4. For the PolitiFact dataset, the same setup as for the Twitter 
dataset is used.
