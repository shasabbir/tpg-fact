The results of our experiments on the Twitter dataset are visual-
ized in Fig.  7. The overall performances of the uncertainty modeling 
modules excluding either unimodal text or unimodal images, that is, 
w/o UEM-Text and w/o UEM-Image, both degraded. Specifically, when 
excluding the uncertainty modeling module for unimodal text, the 
accuracy shows a significant decrease. This exemplifies the extent to 
which unimodal text affects the accuracy of fake news detection and 
further illustrates the importance of uncertainty modeling. Similarly, 
when the uncertainty module used to model unimodal images was 
removed, the overall model performance also decreased. However, the 
extent of the effect of the above two methods compared to that after the 
direct deletion of the UEM module, i.e., w/o UEM, is smaller. When the 
UEM module was directly deleted, the overall performance exhibited 
a sharp decline. The w/o DFN method, which relies only on simple 
splicing instead of the dynamic fusion strategy, shows high degrees of 
degradation in both accuracy and the F1 score.
It is clear that excluding the DFN in our proposed dynamic fusion 
architecture realizes poor results, with the worst accuracy directly 
decreasing by up to 29%, further illustrating the superior performance 
of our proposed dynamic fusion architecture. This is attributed to the 
superiority of the dynamic fusion framework, which is capable of ad-
justing the final decision-making strategy based on the dynamic weights 
held by each modality using the normalized coefficient of variation 
as the fusion weight. The performance of the w/o UEM variant used 
to indicate the deletion of uncertainty modeling was also significantly 
degraded, which is a good indication of the adaptability of uncertainty 
modeling to our dynamic fusion framework.

4.7. Qualitative analysis
In this subsection, numerous experiments are conducted to prove 
the validity of the model. The first one combined the traditional cross-
attention mechanism with our DFN module to form an MDF-FND 
framework using the cross-attention mechanism; however, this did not 
work as well as expected. This experiment is mainly used to show that 
our proposed UEM module is a perfect fit with the DFN module and that 
the role of uncertainty modeling in MDF-FND cannot be replaced by the 
traditional simple attention mechanism. In addition, the DFN module 
was replaced with many existing methods. However, the results still did 
not show excellent performance.
4.7.1. MDF-FND using cross-attention mechanism vs. MDF-FND using
graph-attention mechanism
We also continued the approach of employing cross-attention in the 
MDF-FND framework, as proposed in numerous previous studies, but 
obtained poor experimental results, as shown in Fig.  8(a).
As can be seen in Fig.  8(a), the value of F1 score constantly shows a 
stepwise decrease during the model testing phase, while accuracy and 
precision show different degrees of decrease. This indicates that the tra-
ditional cross-attention mechanism does not fit well with our proposed 
dynamic fusion framework. By contrast, using the GAT mechanism can 
substantially improve the effectiveness of the model, the effect of which 
can be seen in 8(b). Although there is an unanticipated decrease in 
efficiency in a small region, the overall trend of the model is positive, 
and the best F1 score is improved by 7% compared to the effect of 
using cross-attention. Table  5 presents the best and worst performance 
capabilities of the MDF-FND framework with cross-attention versus the 
MDF-FND framework with the GAT mechanism on the Twitter dataset. 
This is attributed to the fact that the GAT mechanism can better capture 
robust feature representations obtained using unimodal uncertainty 
modeling, whereas the cross-attention-only mechanism does not ade-
quately represent the heterogeneous properties of features and is not 
able to model inter-modal uncertainty.
