Multimodal features are frequently affected by the impact of noise 
in high-dimensional data, which can adversely impact the final fusion 
results [15]. To better integrate posts and images from heterogeneous 
data sources, inspired by the concept of trusted multi-view classifi-
cation [16], we propose a framework for multimodal dynamic fusion 
applied to fake news detection. The main motivation of this work is to 
develop a dynamic decision-making mechanism for multimodal news 
samples in which the quality varies significantly, based on the assess-
ment of their uncertainty. Specifically, to reduce the uncertainty re-
sulting from noise accumulation, it first uses an Uncertainty Estimation
Module (UEM) to model the uncertainty within each modality. The ro-
bust feature representations obtained are input into the Dynamic Fusion
Network (DFN) to dynamically assess the quality of different modali-
ties, allowing for the dynamic fusion of features, as shown in Fig.  2(b). 
When uncertainty is high across all modalities, a multimodal heteroge-
neous graph structure is relied upon to model inter-modal uncertainty 
and generate the final decision value. Conversely, when uncertainty 
is lower, we rely on the decision outcomes from the Dynamic Fusion
Network (DFN), which is based on Dempster–Shafer (D–S) evidence 
theory. Experiments conducted using the MDF-FND framework on three 
public datasets — Twitter, Weibo, and PolitiFact — demonstrate that 
our model delivers exceptional performance in multimodal fake news 
detection.
Recent advances in large language models (LLMs) have led to 
outstanding performance in various natural language processing (NLP) 
tasks; however, they still face significant challenges in multimodal fake 
news detection. For example, as noted by [17], although complex LLMs 
such as GPT3.5 can provide valuable multi-perspective justifications for 
exposing fake news, they are still less effective than smaller language 
models (SLMs) such as fine-tuned BERT in these tasks. Therefore, we 
focused on fine-tuning a small model specifically for multimodal fake 
news detection.
The main contributions of this study are as follows:
∙ We developed an uncertainty modeling module utilizing multi-
head attention to effectively extract robust feature representations from 
noisy multimodal news content.
∙ We designed a multimodal DFN based on D–S evidence theory, 
which dynamically balances the contributions of both modalities in 
the final classification, effectively addressing the limitations of weight 
fixation in static fusion strategies.
∙ Our experimental results on three real-world multimodal bench-
mark datasets demonstrate the effectiveness and superiority of our 
model.

2. Related work
In this section, the distinction between multimodal and multiview 
data is shown, followed by briefly review existing research on multi-
modal fake news detection. Relevant studies on multimodal dynamic 
fusion strategies and D–S evidence theory are also the most important 
background, as these techniques are closely related to our approach.
2.1. Feature selection in multi-view learning and multi-modal learning
Multi-view learning refers to the technique of using multiple differ-
ent representations or descriptions of the same data object, typically 
originating from the same type of sensor or modality. Each ‘‘view’’ 
reflects a distinct perspective of the data, potentially observed from dif-
ferent times, locations, or conditions. Recently, some researchers have 
focused on the influence of feature selection in multiview learning. For 
example, Han et al. [18] explored the relationship between features, 
views, and labels, and proposed a new method that integrates feature 
correlation and redundant representation. Specifically, for multiviews, 
particularly in multi-label data, the feature correlation component uses 
label information coefficients to assess the information gain of each 
label, while the feature redundancy component uses view redundancy 
factors to address feature redundancy both within and across views. 
This approach effectively strikes a balance between acquiring label 
information and reducing feature redundancy. Hao et al. [19] examined 
the inconsistencies present in each view, including noise and specific 
elements, which may affect the quality of the mapping between labels 
and feature representations. Moreover, neglecting certain aspects could 
lead to suboptimal outcomes, as each label should have its own distinc-
tive features. To address this, they proposed a unified loss function, 
which is a completely partitioned structure for the observed labels as 
hybrid labels, i.e., common labels, view-specific labels, and noise labels. 
The view-specific labels are further subdivided into several specific 
labels for each view. This method simultaneously takes into account 
the consistency and complementarity of different views. By examining 
the feature weights of the hybrid labels, the mapping relationship 
between labels and features can be established sequentially based on 
their attributes. Additionally, their work investigates the correlations 
between hybrid labels and incorporates these relationships into the loss 
function.

of sensory data, such as vision, audio, text, sensor data, and more.
