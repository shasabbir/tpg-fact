2.5. D–S evidence theory
D–S evidence theory, also known as confidence function theory, is a 
complete theoretical architecture that integrates multi-source informa-
tion and is widely used in multimodal fusion tasks. D–S evidence theory 
was first introduced by [42]. Subsequently, [43] introduced mathemat-
ical evidence theory and applied it to multi-source information fusion. 
Currently, D–S evidence theory has a wide range of applications in the 
field of fusion decision-making [44]. The D–S evidence theory frame-
work facilitates the development of Bayesian theory and simultaneously 
relaxes the limitations of Bayesian theoretical reasoning methods [45]. 
It aims to compute and update the confidence of a proposition by 
integrating evidence from multiple sources, serving as a mathematical 
framework for addressing uncertainty. However, traditional synthesis 
rules cannot be directly applied within the MDF-FND framework ; thus, 
we will elaborate on our algorithm in Section 3.3.
In our approach, we input robust feature representations generated 
by the uncertainty modeling module, which employs multi-head atten-
tion, into the fusion network to learn dynamic inter-modal weight mod-
eling. Ultimately, D–S evidence theory is used to achieve the dynamic 
fusion of textual and visual modalities, establishing a comprehensive 
framework for multimodal fake news detection.
3. Methodology
In this section, we introduce a dynamic fusion framework for fake 
news detection that leverages D–S evidence theory and GAT. Sec-
tion 3.1 provides an overview of the proposed MDF-FND. In Section 3.2, 
we detail the Uncertainty UEM, which utilizes an attention mechanism 
for effective feature extraction. Finally, Section 3.3 introduces the 
dynamic fusion module that integrates GATs with D–S evidence theory 
to enhance the fusion of multimodal data.
