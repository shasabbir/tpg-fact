capability, using the entropy of the Gaussian distribution as the final 
penalty term is much better than using K–L scatter. This is attributed to 
the entropy of a Gaussian distribution, which encourages the model to 
generate a broad distribution rather than one resembling the standard 
normal distribution. This is beneficial in Bayesian optimization because 
the model is forced to quantify the uncertainty of the prediction. Based 
on the above conclusions, the entropy of the Gaussian distribution was 
incorporated as a penalty term into the loss function of the MDF-FND 
framework to encourage the model to learn a broader distribution.
4.7.4. Research on sustainability
Different regions, cultural backgrounds, and linguistic environments 
result in diverse understandings and manifestations of fake news. Re-
search on the sustainability of fake news detection frameworks will 
foster fairness in global fake news detection efforts. In this subsection, 
the contributions of the sustainability of the MDF-FND framework are 
clearly outlined.
Specifically, it includes two main aspects. First, the MDF-FND 
framework plays a significant role in technological evolution. Tra-
ditional methods like concatenation or weighted fusion often fail to 
dynamically adjust the contributions of each modality based on the 
actual data needs. The MDF-FND framework overcomes this limitation 
by introducing a dynamic adjustment mechanism. This development 
will encourage other multimodal fake news detection models to be-
come more flexible and adaptable to a broader range of news types. 
Furthermore, it will generate positive social impacts. By improv-
ing computational efficiency and reducing resource consumption, the 
application of the framework to real-world scenarios will assist fact-
checkers in mitigating the impact of fake news. This will help maintain 
social fairness and harmony, promote the healthy development of the 
information environment, and strengthen societal cohesion and unity.
4.8. Quantitative analysis
In this subsection, we analyze the role of the relevant hyperparam-
eters involved in the model and their impact on the final effect of the 
MDF-FND framework in detail.
4.8.1. Parameter analysis of the attention mechanism in the UEM module
The UEM is primarily responsible for modeling uncertainty within 
each modality, in which we mainly use the multi-head attention mech-
anism. To evaluate the impact of the number of heads in the multi-head 
attention mechanism on overall performance, comparative experiments 
were conducted on the Twitter and Weibo datasets, with the results 
shown in Fig.  11. The results show that on both the Twitter and 
Weibo public datasets, excellent performance can be achieved when the 
number of heads is set to 8. However, as the number of heads decreases, 
the overall performance also show a small decrease.
In Fig.  11(a), employing a multi-head attention mechanism with 12 
heads on the Twitter dataset is identified as a promising strategy for 
modeling uncertainty, although it has a performance degradation of 0.3 
in terms of precision compared to the multi-head attention mechanism 
with 8 heads. Meanwhile, the effect on the Weibo data is shown in Fig. 
11(b), where the 12-head attention is superior to 8-head attention, both 
in terms of accuracy and F1 score. However, it is not the case that more 
heads are more effective, and we conducted further experiments that 
showed a drop in performance after setting the number of attention 
heads to 24. This is mainly because when the number of heads is set 
to 12, the model’s performance has already saturated, and when 24 
heads are used, redundant representations appear due to the excessive 
number of heads. This result is consistent with that of [60]. For the 
subtask of modeling the uncertainty within each modality, excessive 
subspaces are not necessary to capture complex features and achieve 
excellent performance.
