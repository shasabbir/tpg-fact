For example, Zhang et al. [20] observed that both the candidate and 
selected features contribute valuable classification information when 
designing the feature correlation term. As a result, they extracted 
relevant classification information from the joint mutual information 
and proposed a new feature correlation term called Conditional Weight 
Joint Relevance (CWJR). Based on CWJR, they proposed a feature 
selection method named CWJR-FS, which incorporates the concept of 
conditional weight joint relevance. Zhang et al. [21] examined the 
influence of selected features on feature correlation, as well as the 
effect of label correlation on feature correlation. They designed a new 
feature correlation term called ‘‘Dual Conditional Correlation’’ (DCR), 
which employs two conditional mutual information terms to account 
for the correlation between selected features and labels. Gao et al. [22] 
proposed an information-theory-based feature selection method: simi-
larity retention and gaze-based decision. This method first leverages the 
similarity between samples to generate transformed data, mitigating 
outliers or erroneous features in the original data. Additionally, the new 
transformed data improves the discriminability of features, which helps 
enhance the quality of the data.
Multiview focuses on multiple angles or representations of the same 
type of data. In contrast, multimodal focuses on different types of 
data sources (such as images, text, audio, etc.). Additionally, the data 
sources in view-based methods are usually of the same type (e.g., im-
ages from different perspectives), whereas in multimodal methods, 
the data sources come from different modes of perception (e.g., text 
and image, video and audio, etc.). Thus, multi-view feature selection 
emphasizes different representations of the same dataset, while mul-
timodal feature selection focuses on integrating heterogeneous data 
sources. In this paper, we focus on the multimodal perspective of fake 
news detection.
2.2. Multimodal fake news detection
As multimodal data continue to evolve, several researchers have 
become increasingly enthusiastic about processing tasks that involve 
both text and images. Antol et al. [13] proposed a visual question-and-
answer task at learning feature representations from multiple aspects. 
This work promotes cross-disciplinary research in image understanding 
and NLP, laying a foundation for multimodal fake news detection. 
The event adversarial neural network (EANN) model introduced by 
Wang et al. [9] employs an event learner to capture feature repre-
sentations from both text and images in news articles; however, the 
inclusion of auxiliary features increases detection costs. MVAE [11] 
extracts intra-modal information from two unimodal modalities, text 
and images, utilizing a multimodal variant encoder for simple fusion.
