4.3. Baselines
Most previous research has focused on unimodal and multimodal 
fake news detection. To evaluate the superiority of our proposed model, 
we compared it with a baseline model that employs a single textual 
modality. In this comparison, we eliminated the dynamic decision-
making component and directly input the noisy single-textual posts into 
the UEM module. A fully connected layer splicing was then used to 
output the predicted values.
(a) Fake news detection model based on single modality. The SVM-
TS model [54] employs a machine learning approach to detect 
fake news through heuristic rules and linear multi-layer per-
ceptron classifiers. To capture temporal feature information, the 
GRU model [55] implements a RNN for fake news detection. 
In addition, a CNN is employed to learn feature representa-
tions by framing related posts into fixed-length sequences, as 
introduced in [56]. Additionally, a model based on the pre-
trained BERT [46] with a fully connected layer is constructed 
for detecting fake news from plain text input. Meanwhile, to 
assess the performance of the unimodal image baseline model, 
VGG-19 [58] and TextCNN [59] are incorporated into the com-
parative experiments.
(b) Fake news detection model based on multimodal features. VQA 
[13] aims to answer questions about the given images. The 
original VQA model is designed for multi-class classification 
tasks. SAFE [12] is a similarity-aware cross-modal fusion func-
tion for multimodal FND. The MVAE model [11] extracts modal 
information from text and images and uses a multimodal variant 
encoder for simple fusion. [9] constructed an event adversarial 
neural network (EANN) that uses an event learner to learn the 
feature representations of text and images in an article; however, 
additional auxiliary features will increase the cost of detection. 
Intuitively, the SpotFake model [10] can use large-scale textual 
and visual pre-trained models to extract features for each modal-
ity and concatenate them into a multimodal representation of the 
news, which is then placed into a binary classifier for detecting 
fake news. MCNN [29] considers the consistency of different 
modalities and captures the global characteristics of social media 
information. BDANN [26] is a BERT-based domain adaptive 
neural network for detecting multimodal fake news. LIIMR [36] 
utilizes both intra- and inter-modal relationships for fake news 
detection. MFIR [57] recognizes that traditional fusion meth-
ods, such as union or addition, can enhance the performance 
of multimodal fake news detection to some extent. However, 
this approach is mainly limited by shallow cross-modal feature 
fusion. Therefore, this model focuses on identifying inconsis-
tent semantics across modalities to achieve interpretable fake 
news detection. MPFN [25] is a progressive fusion network 
designed for multimodal fake news detection, capable of cap-
turing representation information for each modality at different 
levels. It uses mixers to facilitate fusion between modalities at 
the same and different levels, thereby establishing strong con-
nections between them. DIIF [35] employs a dynamic internal 
and cross-modal fusion model for multimodal fake news detec-
tion, dynamically learning the interactions and fusions among 
different modalities. The SAFE [12] model detects fake news 
on social media using a similarity-aware approach. CAFE [32] 
enhances the accuracy of fake news detection by intuitively 
and adaptively aggregating unimodal features and cross-modal 
correlations. CMMTN [24] enhances the performance of fake 
news detection by jointly modeling both the inter- and intra-
modal relationships of multimodal information, while masking 
irrelevant context. COOLANT [31] is a cross-modal contrastive 
learning framework for multimodal fake news detection, aiming 
to achieve more accurate image–text alignment. It implements 
an attention mechanism with an attention-guided module to help 
effectively and interpretably aggregate the aligned unimodal 
representations and cross-modal correlations.
