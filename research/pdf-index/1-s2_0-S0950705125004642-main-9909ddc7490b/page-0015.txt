4.8.3. Parameters in the optimization algorithm
The cost function is responsible for evaluating the variance between 
the MDF-FND framework and the real results, and therefore also its 
performance. Using only the cross-entropy loss function will lead to the 
collapse of the modeled variance and degradation of the distribution 
representation from the original high-dimensional Gaussian distribu-
tion to a deterministic point embedding approach, which further leads 
to a reduction in the model’s generalization ability for multimodal 
information. Therefore, the entropy of the Gaussian distribution is 
added as a penalty term to reduce the impact of modal uncertainty on 
the results. The hyperparameter 𝜂, which acts as the lower bound for 
the model’s minimum uncertainty output, has a direct impact on the 
final decision-making performance. To identify the optimal value for 
the MDF-FND framework, we employed a grid search strategy using 
the Optuna library [61], with a search space of 𝐹 𝑙𝑜𝑎𝑡{0.001, 0.05}. We 
set the number of trials to 10 and step size to 0.002, and we report 
the optimal convergence range on the Twitter and Weibo datasets in 
Fig.  12. Consequently, the best values for 𝜂 are 0.018 for the Twitter 
dataset and 0.017 for the Weibo dataset. The experimental results 
further indicate that the MDF-FND framework is highly sensitive to the 
choice of 𝜂.
4.9. Case study
To investigate the effectiveness of the dynamic fusion mechanism 
in multimodal fake news detection more deeply, we conducted a set 
of case studies, as shown in Fig.  13. Four representative cases from 
the Twitter and Weibo datasets are exemplified, presenting the overall 
uncertainty scores of the two modalities in detail, along with their 
respective unimodal uncertainty scores. The uncertainty score reflects 
the credibility of the modality for the final prediction outcome and is 
inversely related to uncertainty. As shown in Algorithm 1, when the 
value is high, particularly when it is larger than the set threshold, the 
decision opinion of the multimodal heterogeneous graph is directly 
used. When this value is low, we focus on the decision results for 
unimodal text and unimodal images. In the first case, where the overall 
uncertainty is relatively low and there is a large difference between the 
uncertainties of the two unimodal modalities, it is clearly observable 
that the unimodal text has a lower uncertainty score. Therefore, the 
MDF-FND framework adopts the decision opinion of the image. The 
opposite situation occurs in the second case, where the confidence level 
of the unimodal text is much higher than that of the unimodal image. 
Therefore, final decision power is granted to the unimodal text. When 
the overall uncertainty score is high, the decision-making power of the 
heterogeneous GNN is considered, as shown in case four. Obtaining 
a plausible decision from only two unimodal uncertainty scores may 
produce poor results. For example, in the third case, the unimodal 
image has a much larger uncertainty score than the text, but the image 
itself is of low quality, so the data tend to have uncertainty. Fortunately, 
when the overall uncertainty is considered, the final decision no longer 
relies on a low-quality unimodal image with a high uncertainty score 
but rather on the decision value of a multimodal heterogeneous image, 
which avoids catastrophic results to some extent.
