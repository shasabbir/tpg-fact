5. Limitations and potential research directions
Our framework named MDF-FND mainly has two limitations. On the 
one hand, it is evident that the computational complexity of the self-
attention mechanism is 𝑂(𝑘 ⋅ 𝑁 2 ⋅ 𝑑). As the sequence length increases, 
the effect of the number of heads 𝑘 becomes negligible, so it can be 
ignored. Therefore, the complexity can be expressed as 𝑂(𝑁 2 ⋅𝑑), which 
is clearly impractical when processing long sequences. On the other 
hand, although the proposed framework demonstrates excellent perfor-
mance in modeling uncertainty, it is limited to data processing within 
a single modality. During feature fusion, there are often multi-modal 
samples that are weakly aligned or misaligned, which are referred to 
as cross-modal noise [62]. Unlike modality-specific noise, cross-modal 
noise resides in a higher-level semantic space.
To address the above challenges, our future potential research di-
rections lies in the following aspects. First, we will employ a linear 
attention mechanism, such as the Polarity-aware Linear Attention in 
PolaFormer [63], to facilitate uncertainty modeling in multimodal 
fake news detection, making it more suitable for widespread deploy-
ment. Additionally, we will adopt a cross-modal denoising approach 
to address redundant information representations generated during 
multimodal interactions.
6. Conclusion
Multimodal user posts containing fake news on social media involve 
data uncertainty caused by unimodal noise. Therefore, in this paper, 
a dynamic fusion framework called MDF-FND was proposed that can 
model inter- and intra-modal uncertainty while providing final decision 
results using a dynamic fusion strategy. The MDF-FND framework first 
contains a UEM module for performing uncertainty modeling, which 
maps the unimodal text and unimodal image features through multi-
head attention mechanism mapping to a latent subspace that satisfies 
a Gaussian distribution. Subsequently, a DFN module employing the 
GAT mechanism with evidence theory is employed to conveniently 
capture inter-modal uncertainty information and is responsible for 
dynamically balancing the weights of the two modalities. Multiple sets 
of experiments on three publicly available datasets, Twitter, Weibo, and 
PolitiFact, demonstrated the effectiveness of the proposed framework.
Recent advances in LLMs have demonstrated excellent performance 
in various NLP endeavors. However, they still face significant chal-
lenges in multimodal fake news detection tasks. For example, it is 
mentioned in [63] that although complex LLMs like GPT3.5 can provide 
desirable multi-perspective justifications for exposing fake news, they 
are still not as effective as basic SLMs, such as fine-tuned BERT.
